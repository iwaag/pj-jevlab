# Independent evaluations and comparisons of Jev and alternatives

Survey date 2026-10-01. Legend: [V] = I read the primary repo/README/blog directly; [S] = seen only via a summary/search snippet (not independently opened or reproduced). I reproduced nothing myself.

## 0. Big picture
- There is no authoritative leaderboard. Closest: JevBench (Benchmark Heaven), typed-decision-bench (kyr0), and the vendor's own evals.typesafe.ai. systemonemodels.org has no performance tables.
- Vendor evidence base is thin: the only published accuracy-type figure is "67.8% agreement" with averaged frontier-LLM judgments (labels from "GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, both at high thinking"), self-run, unreproduced, with no calibration figure (yodablocks/jev-orderby-bench [V]; evals.typesafe.ai page [V via summary]). Speed/cost claims: "193.6x faster, 444.6x cheaper" vs the worst baselines; ~25x faster/~76x cheaper vs the closest comparable model (pearpages [S]).
- Independent measurements cluster into: (a) Jev vs LLMs on a user's own routing/classification task (usually Jev matches or slightly beats cheap LLMs, 3-30x cheaper, 3-9x faster); (b) calibration audits (probabilities decent in-distribution, overconfident where the label rule is unknowable, quantized to 0.01, many exact 0/1); (c) Jev vs open replicas (Jev wins zero-shot out-of-box; replicas win speed/cost, and win only after fine-tuning or on home-turf tasks).

Unseen synthetic support tickets (900): queue choice 89.0% acc but ECE 0.082 and wrong answers often get 1.00; "angry?" boolean 91.7%, underconfident; org-specific priority score 44.7% acc, ECE 0.325, mean stated prob 0.74; overall ECE 0.107 = 4.4x noise floor 0.024. ## 5. Measurement pitfalls surfaced by these studies
1. Labels are LLM-generated (vendor) or synthetic (many indie tests); "agreement" != accuracy != calibration.
2. Quantization to 0.01 plus frequent exact 0/1 breaks log-loss fits, ties sorts, and creates artefacts in temperature-scaling (jev-ood-calibration correction).
3. Request shape changes results (batching 40 rows/request degrades ranking).
4. Cost multiplier depends entirely on the comparator (3.8x to 580x in the evidence above); speed multiplier is more stable at ~3-9x in third-party tests, vs claimed 193x.
5. Home-turf specialists and fine-tuned replicas look great on their own eval; zero-shot out-of-distribution is where replicas fall (Laya 40.3% adversarial, 77-label 0.425).
6. Several indie studies use n <= 100 with no CIs, except jev-benchmarks (bootstrap CIs), jev-orderby-bench (pre-registered gates) and jev-ood-calibration (noise floor).
