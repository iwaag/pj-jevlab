# Evidence cards: statistics, collective judgement and reliable computation

Areas A (statistics and collective judgement) and B (reliable computation and communication) of step 2 of [plan.md](../../../devdocs/episodes/study/statistically_correct/plan.md). Written 2026-10-06; all sources accessed that day. Search record: [search-log.md](../search-log.md).

Labelling convention in every card: **[Source]** = what the source claims or proves; **[Lab]** = a local observation (only where one exists; see [framing.md](../framing.md)); **[Inference]** = this survey's reading, not the source's claim. "Read" means the PDF or full text was opened and the cited numbers were checked; "summary-only" means only a search or fetch summary was seen.

## Summary of findings

### Positive results (aggregation or redundancy helps)

- **Condorcet jury theorem (S-01).** With state-conditionally independent voters who are each right with probability above 1/2, majority accuracy rises with group size toward 1. This is a theorem, not an application result.
- **Ensemble ambiguity identity (S-03).** For squared-error regression with weights summing to one, ensemble error equals the average member error minus the members' disagreement around the ensemble mean. The ensemble is never worse than the average member.
- **Self-consistency (S-07).** Sampling several reasoning paths from one LLM and taking the most frequent answer raised accuracy over greedy chain-of-thought on arithmetic benchmarks, for example PaLM-540B on GSM8K from 56.5% to 74.4%.
- **von Neumann multiplexing and the Evans-Schulman thresholds (S-08).** Reliable computation from noisy gates is possible below a gate-noise threshold, at the price of redundancy and extra depth.
- **Shannon (S-09).** For a known noisy channel, error probability can be made arbitrarily small at any rate below capacity, by encoding.
- **Gács (S-10).** Fault-tolerant computation is possible in a one-dimensional asynchronous cellular automaton, with a bounded-probability error model and a hierarchical construction.
- **Coding-agent N-version programming (S-11).** On one task, majority voting over three AI-written programs cut the mean failure count from 387.44 (single) to 130.99 (triple), even though failures were far from independent.

### Negative and qualifying results

- **Correlation caps what aggregation can do.** Kim et al. (S-04) measured error agreement between 349 LLMs (HuggingFace leaderboard) and between 71 LLMs (Helm leaderboard); on Helm, pairs agree about 60% of the time when both are wrong, against 1/3 for uniformly random wrong choices. More accurate models had more correlated errors. Kohli (S-05, preprint) estimates that 9 LLM judges carry about 2.2 independent votes of information, and the panel did not beat its best member.
- **Voting can fail outright.** Denisov-Blanch et al. (S-06, preprint) report that polling-style aggregation gave no consistent accuracy gain over a single sample on tasks without a verifier, even at 25 times the inference cost.
- **Not all error is "noise given the state".** In Dietrich-Spiekermann (S-01), if problems are "easy" (every voter right with probability p) or "hard" (every voter right with 1-p), majority accuracy converges to the share of easy problems, not to 1.
- **Heterogeneous competence can make larger groups worse.** The SEP entry says majority reliability can be decreasing in group size when new members are less competent (summary-only, S-01).
- **Social influence can destroy diversity without gains.** In Lorenz et al. (S-02), human groups that saw others' estimates converged without lower collective error. A later study (Becker et al. 2017, abstract read) reports that decentralised networks can give the opposite result, so the effect depends on network structure.
- **Nominal diversity is not behavioural diversity.** Ron et al. (S-11) found 429 coincident failures against 115.36 expected under independence (3.7 times), across 48 AI-written programs from different agents, models and languages. Knight and Leveson (1986) reportedly found the same for human programmers (secondary account only).
- **Ensemble benefit needs disagreement, which is not free (S-03).** [Inference] If disagreement is raised by degrading members, the average-error term rises, so entropy or diversity alone does not show benefit.
- **Reliable-computation guarantees break above a noise threshold (S-08).** For 3-input majority gates in formulas the tolerable gate error is below 1/6; for 2-input NAND gates it is about 0.0886 (the threshold depends on the gate type). Above the threshold, no amount of redundancy helps. All these thresholds assume independent gate faults.
- **Explicit specification is what reliable computation and communication assume (S-08, S-09, S-10).** The function, the code and the "correct output" are fixed in advance and the error model is stated. None of this exists for a semantic decision with no unique correct action. [Inference]

### Transfer caveats common to all cards

- The 80 to 90% local-accuracy scenario in the [brief](../../../devdocs/episodes/study/statistically_correct/braindump.md) is not an established threshold, and no source here sets such a threshold. Numerical tolerances below (1/6, 0.0073, 0.0107, 0.0886) belong to their gate models and are not accuracy targets for decisions.
- Repeating one model across many agents does not establish independence (S-04, S-05, S-06, S-11 all point this way). [Inference] Independence needs to be measured per task and per state.

## S-01 Condorcet jury theorem and its independence assumptions (Dietrich and Spiekermann 2013; SEP summary)

1. **Source.** Dietrich, F. and Spiekermann, K. (2013), "Independent Opinions? On the Causal Foundations of Belief Formation and Jury Theorems", *Mind* 122(487): 655-685. Author PDF: https://personal.lse.ac.uk/spiekerk/papers/DietrichSpiekermann-IndependentOpinions.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read: introduction, state-conditional independence (SI), the common-cause discussion, Theorem 3 (Easy/Hard Dichotomy). Also: Dietrich, F. and Spiekermann, K., "Jury Theorems", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jury-theorems/, accessed 2026-10-06, **fetch-summary only** (sections 2.2, 3.2, 4.1 as cited by the summary). Condorcet (1785), Ladha (1992), Boland (1989) were **not** opened; see the search log.
2. **System, decision, objective.** A group of voters each forms an opinion on a binary factual question with one correct answer; the group decides by majority. Objective: probability that the majority is correct.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Error is disagreement with the true state. It is a modelled probability (correctness events), not measured. Assumed natural, not injected.
4. **Dependence assumptions.** [Source] The classical theorem needs state-conditional independence (SI) plus equal competence above 1/2. Opinions are unconditionally correlated because the state is a common cause. SI fails if any other common cause exists (the paper's example: weather affecting every juror), and the paper proposes conditioning on all common causes. No dependence across time is modelled. Shared information is the main route to dependence.
5. **Mechanism.** Absorption: independent errors average out as group size grows.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Theorem: under SI plus homogeneous competence p > 1/2, P(majority correct) increases in odd n and converges to 1. [Source] Theorem 3: under their assumption PI and an Easy/Hard Dichotomy (all voters right with probability p on easy problems, with 1-p on hard problems), P(majority correct) converges to Pr(problem is easy), and is strictly decreasing in n if hard problems are more likely than easy ones. Empirical effect size and uncertainty: unreported (theory).
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** [Source, SEP summary] "The wisdom-of-crowds conclusion indeed breaks down in all three theorems if one removes homogeneity from the competence premise"; majority reliability can be a decreasing function of group size. Positive correlation through shared evidence or influences "undermines diversity and reinforces tendencies and errors". [Source, paper introduction] Information cascades and systematic biases are named as harmful dependence.
8. **Evidence type.** Theorems; no empirical test of the assumptions in these sources. No code or data. No conflicts identified.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous, for binary factual judgments only. [Inference] The easy/hard structure is the closest formal match to state-dependent errors of one model across many agents: if the same model errs on the same states, group accuracy tends to Pr(easy), not 1. Missing for transfer: whether Jev's errors are state-conditionally independent ([Lab] none measured, framing.md observation 1); a binary truth (the SEP entry itself separates factual from normative questions); homogeneous competence.
10. **Cost.** n independent opinions per decision, i.e. n times the decision cost; no communication if opinions are formed independently.

## S-02 Social influence undermining the wisdom-of-crowds effect (Lorenz et al. 2011), with a network qualification

1. **Source.** Lorenz, J., Rauhut, H., Schweitzer, F., Helbing, D. (2011), "How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect", *PNAS* 108(22): 9020-9025. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108 (publisher page returned HTTP 403). Full text read via PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/, accessed 2026-10-06; Results, Table 1, Fig. 1. Qualifying work, **abstract only (Europe PMC)**: Becker, J., Brackbill, D., Centola, D. (2017), "Network dynamics of social influence in the wisdom of crowds", *PNAS* 114(26), https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1615978114. A comment by Farrell (2011), "Social influence benefits the wisdom of individuals in the crowd", PNAS, was found by title only and not read.
2. **System, decision, objective.** Human subjects estimate factual numeric quantities (for example murders in Switzerland in 2006) and may revise after seeing others' answers. Objective: accuracy of the group estimate and diversity of estimates.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Collective error is the squared deviation of the group average from the truth; group diversity is the average squared deviation from the group mean; both computed on log estimates normalised by the true value. Natural errors, observed.
4. **Dependence.** Three conditions: no information, aggregated (average) information, full information about the others' estimates, over five consecutive rounds. Social influence creates dependence across agents and time by design.
5. **Mechanism.** Not a correction mechanism: the paper studies how information sharing reduces the diversity on which absorption by averaging relies.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** N = 144 subjects, groups of 12, six questions, five rounds; 24 groups per condition in the plotted aggregates. Control: no-information condition. [Source] Social influence "strongly reduces the group's diversity without significantly reducing its collective errors" (Fig. 1D, E; error bars are 10% confidence intervals). The arithmetic mean was closer to the truth than individual first estimates in only 21.3% of cases on raw data and 77.1% in log space (geometric mean); Table 1 gives, for example, +1,337.2% arithmetic-mean error against -28.1% geometric-mean error for population density of Switzerland. The abstract also reports a range reduction effect (the truth moves to the edge of the estimate range) and a confidence boost without accuracy gain.
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** [Source] Groups are "wise" under the geometric mean or median, not the arithmetic mean, on these right-skewed estimates. Questions were obscure quantities where many subjects had the wrong order of magnitude, unlike a repeated choice among options. [Source, abstract of Becker et al.] "General network conditions under which social influence improves the accuracy of group estimates" exist in decentralised networks; centralised networks make group error more likely to rise. [Inference] The result is not that interaction is harmful in general; it depends on topology and information.
8. **Evidence type.** Empirical (controlled laboratory) study with humans. Becker et al. report theory plus experiments and qualify the generalisation. Code and data availability: not checked. Conflicts: none identified.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous only. [Inference] A population of agents that observe each other's choices can lose the diversity averaging relies on; whether LLM agents behave like these humans is not tested here. Missing assumptions: numeric estimation (not discrete action choice); human social-influence dynamics; network structure of the agent population.
10. **Cost.** None as an intervention; the cost is the loss of independence when agents share information.

## S-03 Ensemble ambiguity: error equals mean error minus disagreement (Krogh and Vedelsby 1995)

1. **Source.** Krogh, A., Vedelsby, J. (1995), "Neural Network Ensembles, Cross Validation, and Active Learning", *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 7* (NIPS 1994), pp. 231-238. https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/1994/file/b8c37e33defde51cf91e1e03e51657da-Paper.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-06; read Sections 1-3 (Eqs. 1-11). Kuncheva and Whitaker (2003) was not opened; see the search log.
2. **System, decision, objective.** An ensemble of regression networks approximates a real-valued function; the combined output is a weighted average. Objective: ensemble generalisation error.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Squared error against the target function, averaged over the input distribution. "Ambiguity" is each member's squared deviation from the ensemble average and can be computed without labels. Natural and measured.
4. **Dependence.** Captured by ambiguity itself. [Source] The identity "separates the generalization error into a term that depends on the generalization errors of the individual networks and another term that contain all correlations between the networks".
5. **Mechanism.** Absorption: E = Ē - Ā (Eq. 10), so the ensemble error is never above the weighted mean member error.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Exact identity for convex weights (sum to one) and squared error. With uniform weights the ensemble error is at most the mean member error (Eq. 11). Effect size depends on ambiguity; no empirical effect size is taken from this paper. Uncertainty: not applicable.
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** [Source] "A combination of the output of several networks ... is only useful if they disagree on some inputs"; a million identical networks add nothing over one. If the ensemble is strongly biased, ambiguity is small and the ensemble error is about the average member error. The paper notes that increasing ambiguity helps "while not increasing individual generalization errors". [Inference] A bias shared by all members is untouched by the identity. [Inference] It is a regression result, not a majority-vote result.
8. **Evidence type.** Mathematical identity plus small empirical illustrations (not extracted here). Replication not applicable. No code or data used. No conflicts.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous if Jev outputs are averaged scores (regression-like), not for argmax choices. Missing for transfer: that scores are averaged rather than voted, that squared error is the right measure, and a measured ambiguity in the agent population.
10. **Cost.** N members per prediction; diversity-inducing training choices (different architectures or data) may raise member cost.

## S-04 Correlated errors across 349 LLMs (Kim, Garg, Peng, Garg 2025)

1. **Source.** Kim, E., Garg, A., Peng, K., Garg, N. (2025), "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models", ICML 2025 (PMLR 267); arXiv:2506.07962, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962 (PDF read). Accessed 2026-10-06. Sections 1, 3 (Table 1, Fig. 1), 4 (Fig. 2), 5 (Figs. 3-4). Code and data at https://github.com/nikhgarg/llm_correlated_errors_public/ (link as printed in the paper; not opened).
2. **System, decision, objective.** Many LLMs answer multiple-choice questions (HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard: 349 models, 12,032 questions; Helm: 71 models, 14,042 questions) and rate resumes against job descriptions (20 LLMs, 1,800 pairs, 450 hand-labelled). Objective: how correlated are errors, and what does correlation do to LLM-as-judge accuracy estimates and a simulated hiring market.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** On multiple choice, a wrong answer against the MMLU label; correlation is the rate at which two models give the same wrong answer when both are wrong. On resumes, the residual against hand labels (subjective ground truth, noted by the authors). Natural errors, observed in existing leaderboard outputs; resume data generated by the authors.
4. **Dependence.** Across models, measured. Shared provider, base architecture and similar size raise agreement; [Source] "pairs of models that are more accurate individually also have more correlated errors". No temporal dependence studied.
5. **Mechanism.** None proposed as correction. The paper measures the limit on the diversity benefit of using several models.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Baseline: random choice among wrong answers (1/3 on Helm). [Source] Mean pairwise error agreement is 0.60 on Helm and 0.423 on HuggingFace (random baseline 0.127); 97.5% of Helm pairs and 100% of HuggingFace pairs exceed the baseline. Some pairs with no stated relationship agree on 0.9987 of mutual errors (google/text-unicorn and writer/palmyra-x-v3). Regressions (Table 1) explain 34%, 61.3% and 41.5% of agreement variation (HuggingFace, Helm, Resumes). LLM-as-judge: each judge inflates the accuracy of models weaker than itself (Fig. 2). Hiring simulation: with firms each using a random LLM, about 20% of applicants are systemically excluded even with 20 firms (Fig. 3a); the paper gives (1-0.25)^n as the expectation under random preferences. [Inference] That is about 0.3% for n = 20. Standard errors appear in Table 1; no other uncertainty was seen in the sections read.
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** Leaderboard multiple choice and one resume task at one point in time. The authors note the resume ground truth is subjective, and the hiring simulation uses assumed applicant preferences. The paper does not say how many diverse models restore independence.
8. **Evidence type.** Empirical study (existing responses and a new resume task) plus a simulation. Peer-reviewed (ICML 2025); public code and data stated; no replication by others found in this search. Conflicts: none stated; academic authors.
9. **Jev relevance.** Directly demonstrated that different LLMs share errors and that accuracy and error correlation rise together; not shown for Jev's encoders or label-scoring readouts. [Lab] No local measure of error correlation exists. Missing assumptions: that Jev models behave like leaderboard LLMs; that errors on game decisions resemble errors on MMLU.
10. **Cost.** Running many models multiplies inference cost; the finding is that the cost may buy less independence than expected.

## S-05 Nine LLM judges carry about two independent votes (Kohli 2026)

1. **Source.** Kohli, G. (2026), "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels", arXiv:2605.29800 (28 May 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800. PDF read: abstract, introduction, methodology (Kish formula), results, limitations. Accessed 2026-10-06. Author affiliation: Apple. Preprint; no peer review found.
2. **System, decision, objective.** A panel of 9 LLMs from 7 families votes on natural language inference labels (ChaosNLI-MNLI, SNLI, AlphaNLI; 1,000 sampled items each) plus a pairwise preference task (RewardBench). Objective: accuracy of the majority vote against the 100-annotator human majority label, relative to the independent-voter prediction.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** A judge errs when it disagrees with the human majority label. [Source] For high-entropy items the label "may represent a plurality preference rather than a 'correct' answer" (author's limitation; distributional checks reported in an appendix). Natural errors, observed.
4. **Dependence.** Measured across judges: Kish effective sample size n_eff = k / (1 + (k-1) φ̄), where φ̄ is the mean pairwise phi correlation of error vectors (φ̄ = 0.391 on MNLI, giving n_eff of about 2.18). The Condorcet null simulates independent voters from per-judge, per-difficulty-bin confusion matrices (10,000 Monte Carlo runs per item). Dependence is across agents and state, not time.
5. **Mechanism.** Absorption by majority vote, tested against an independence ideal; also tests Dawid-Skene EM and accuracy-weighted voting as corrections.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Baseline: best single judge and the independent-vote prediction. [Source] n_eff = 2.18 [2.07, 2.31] on MNLI and about 2.0 to 2.5 across datasets and prompt/temperature variants. MNLI panel 72.0% vs best judge 71.8%; SNLI best judge 84.2% vs panel 77.7%; AlphaNLI best 91.2% vs panel 88.7%. Gap to the independent prediction 8 to 22 percentage points (permutation p < 10^-4). Dawid-Skene EM and accuracy weighting close at most 11% of the gap even with oracle gold access. Chain-of-thought prompting increased correlation (n_eff 1.94).
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** Classification and binary-preference tasks only; a snapshot of current models; the authors state correlation may differ for open-ended generation. The Condorcet model's confusion matrices are estimated from the same items (split-half overfitting ratios 0.96 to 1.00 reported). [Inference] n_eff of about 2 is for this panel and these datasets, not a general constant.
8. **Evidence type.** Empirical study with a simulation null model. Replication across three NLI datasets and RewardBench is within the paper; no independent replication. Data public (ChaosNLI, RewardBench); code availability not seen in the sections read. Single industry author; no conflict stated.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous: it supplies a measurement method (effective votes from error correlation) usable on any set of deciders. Directly demonstrated only for LLM judges on NLI. Missing assumptions: task similarity; that game-agent populations share errors at similar levels; that votes rather than scores are aggregated.
10. **Cost.** Nine model calls per item for an information content of about two.

## S-06 Polling-style aggregation fails when LLM errors are correlated (Denisov-Blanch et al. 2026)

1. **Source.** Denisov-Blanch, Y., Kazdan, J., Chudnovsky, J., Schaeffer, R., Guan, S., Adeshina, S., Koyejo, S. (2026), "Consensus is Not Verification: Why Crowd Wisdom Strategies Fail for LLM Truthfulness", arXiv:2603.06612 (20 Feb 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06612. Read: abstract, introduction, captions of Figs. 3 and 5, Sections 5.1 and 5.3. Experimental result tables not read. Accessed 2026-10-06. Preprint; Stanford and Amazon authors.
2. **System, decision, objective.** Open-source LLMs answer binary questions from five benchmarks (factual knowledge, commonsense, expert-level, forecasting). Aggregators tested: majority voting, confidence weighting, the Surprisingly Popular algorithm. Objective: accuracy above a single sample.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** A wrong binary answer against the benchmark label; forecasting questions resolve after model cutoffs. Natural errors. Correlation measured by agreement and Cohen's kappa.
4. **Dependence.** Across models and samples. [Source] Models give correlated outputs "even when conditioned on out of distribution random strings and asked to produce pseudo-random outputs" (kappa above chance at temperatures 0 and 1, Fig. 3), interpreted by the authors as shared inductive biases, not shared knowledge. Confidence tracks consensus nearly as strongly as correctness.
5. **Mechanism.** Absorption by voting and weighting; the paper reports that it does not work in this regime.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Baseline: single-sample accuracy. [Source, abstract] "Even at 25x the inference cost of naive sampling, polling-style aggregation yields no consistent accuracy gains over single-sample baselines and often amplifies shared misconceptions"; on forecasting questions after the cutoff "all methods perform at chance". Per-benchmark effects and uncertainty: not extracted.
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** [Source] Binary questions only; verbalised confidence only; in domains with an external verifier (maths, code) additional samples help, consistent with S-07. The authors state the results do not contradict successes of self-consistency but identify the regime where such methods stop working.
8. **Evidence type.** Empirical study (preprint); no independent replication found. Code and data availability not checked. Conflicts: none stated.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous, and the closest LLM counterexample to "many noisy deciders average out". Missing assumptions: that Jev's readouts (label scoring, pointer heads) behave like these generative LLMs; that its decisions are binary factual judgments. [Lab] The local hype-and-reality note of confident wrong answers (error-ranking AUC 0.73) is consistent with the confidence result but does not test it.
10. **Cost.** Up to 25 times the inference cost in the failed configurations.

## S-07 Self-consistency over sampled reasoning paths (Wang et al. 2023)

1. **Source.** Wang, X., Wei, J., Schuurmans, D., Le, Q., Chi, E., Narang, S., Chowdhery, A., Zhou, D. (2023), "Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models", ICLR 2023; arXiv:2203.11171, https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171. Read: abstract, Sections 1-3, Tables 2-3, Figures 2-3, discussion. Accessed 2026-10-06.
2. **System, decision, objective.** One LLM samples many chain-of-thought reasoning paths; the most frequent final answer is chosen. Objective: benchmark accuracy on arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** A wrong final answer against a unique labelled answer. Variability is injected on purpose through temperature (T = 0.5 for UL2 and LaMDA) and similar sampling.
4. **Dependence.** All samples come from one model and one prompt. The paper does not measure independence between samples; its premise is that correct reasoning paths agree on the answer more than incorrect ones.
5. **Mechanism.** Constructive variability combined with absorption by voting: sampling creates diverse paths, voting marginalises them.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Baseline: chain-of-thought with greedy decoding; also sample-and-rank and beam search (Fig. 3). Up to 40 sampled paths, averaged over 10 runs (standard deviation at most 0.5 for all tasks, Table footnote). [Source] Arithmetic gains: GSM8K +17.9 points (PaLM-540B 56.5 to 74.4; code-davinci-002 60.1 to 78.0), SVAMP +11.0, AQuA +12.2 (Table 2). Smaller elsewhere (for example StrategyQA +6.3 on PaLM-540B). Null or near-null: Letter (4) +0.0 on UL2-20B and LaMDA-137B; Coinflip +0.1 on UL2-20B (Table 3). Performance saturates after a few paths (Fig. 2).
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** [Source] More computation; models "can sometimes generate incorrect or nonsensical reasoning paths". Tasks have a unique extractable answer. Gains vary strongly with model and task. [Inference] The benefit shown is sampling plus voting versus greedy decoding with a known answer set; it does not show that randomness helps where there is no unique answer, and S-06 shows failure under shared bias.
8. **Evidence type.** Evaluated implementation (benchmarks). Replication by others: not checked here. Authors are Google researchers using Google models; code not checked.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous for factual tasks with a defined answer. Missing assumptions: independence between samples is untested; Jev's selectors may not sample; many game decisions have no unique correct action.
10. **Cost.** 5 to 40 times the generations of greedy decoding; the authors suggest 5 to 10 paths capture most of the gain.

## S-08 Reliable computation from noisy gates: von Neumann multiplexing and the tolerable-noise thresholds

1. **Source.** von Neumann, J. (1956), "Probabilistic Logics and the Synthesis of Reliable Organisms from Unreliable Components", in *Automata Studies* (Annals of Mathematics Studies 34), pp. 43-98 (Caltech lectures, January 1952). PDF: https://static.ias.edu/pitp/archive/2012files/Probabilistic_Logics.pdf; read: the error-model passage, the multiplexing and bundle discussion, the 0.0073 and 0.0107 values, and the closing remark on the constant-error assumption. Evans, W. S. and Schulman, L. J., "On the Maximum Tolerable Noise of k-input Gates for Reliable Computation by Formulas", PDF https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~schulman/Papers/vnthresh.pdf (abstract, introduction, Theorem 1; publication year not shown in the extracted text and not verified). Unger, F., "Noise threshold for universality of 2-input gates", arXiv:0711.0351, https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0351 (introduction, Theorem 1). All accessed 2026-10-06. Pippenger (1985, 1988), Hajek-Weller (1991), Feder (1989) and Evans-Pippenger (1998) were taken only as reported by Evans-Schulman and Unger; they were not opened.
2. **System, decision, objective.** A circuit of unreliable gates (organs) computes a Boolean function. Each gate outputs the wrong value with probability ε. Objective: output correct with probability bounded away from 1/2 for every input.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Gate fault: output complemented with probability ε. Assumed (model), injected by definition. [Source] "The simplest assumption concerning errors is this ... malfunctioning is assumed to occur statistically independently of the general state of the network and of the occurrence of other malfunctions." von Neumann also describes a more general, "good deal more realistic" model with state- and history-dependent errors bounded by ε, but adopts the independent case.
4. **Dependence.** Independent across gates, time and state in the main model. [Source] In multiplexing the lines feeding a majority organ "must be statistically independent, in order to permit the application of the statistical formula"; he leaves the choice of the randomising permutation unproven ("intuitively plausible that most 'complicated' permutations will be suited"). His closing remark calls the constant, history-independent ε "an unrealistic assumption".
5. **Mechanism.** Correction by redundancy: interleaved computation and error-correction levels (majority of copies), or multiplexed bundles read with a fiduciary level.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** [Source, von Neumann] The 3-input majority formula method works for ε < 0.0073 (a restriction that "has no absolute significance"); multiplexing works for ε < 0.0107 with fiduciary level Δ = 0.07, and his numerical estimate uses ε = 0.005. [Source, Evans-Schulman] For odd k, noisy formulas can compute all Boolean functions reliably if and only if ε < β_k (closed form in their Theorem 1, not transcribed here); for k = 3 the threshold is 1/6 (Hajek-Weller). [Source, Unger] For 2-input gates the formula threshold is β_2 = (3 - √7)/4, about 8.856% (possible direction attributed to Evans-Pippenger, tightness shown by Unger); extension to circuits is a conjecture. The summary of Pippenger's result (formulas tolerating failures must be deeper) came from search results and is not verified at source. Uncertainty: theorem bounds, none statistical.
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** Above the threshold no amount of redundancy helps. Guarantees hold for independent gate faults and for functions given in advance. [Source, Unger] Thresholds for even fan-in gates were open for a long time.
8. **Evidence type.** Theorems by several authors over decades. Replication: independent proofs of the same k = 3 threshold (Hajek-Weller; Evans-Schulman) as reported by Evans-Schulman. No code or data. No conflicts.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous only. [Inference] What carries over: a majority restoring organ corrects errors only if the errors are independent and below a threshold; above it, more redundancy does not rescue the computation. What does not carry over: gates are binary, the function and its encoding are fixed, the fault model is independent and symmetric. A semantic decision has no designed redundancy (copies of one model's decision are not independent, S-04 to S-06) and no specified correct output. These ε values are gate error rates, not decision-accuracy targets, and must not be read as "an 89% accurate decider is enough".
10. **Cost.** Redundancy factor (copies per computation), extra depth, and a randomising permutation or other mechanism that restores independence.

## S-09 Noisy-channel coding theorem (Shannon 1948)

1. **Source.** Shannon, C. E. (1948), "A Mathematical Theory of Communication", *Bell System Technical Journal* 27. PDF copy: https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf. Read in extracted text: Theorem 9 (noiseless channel), Section 13 "The Fundamental Theorem for a Discrete Channel with Noise", Theorem 11. Accessed 2026-10-06.
2. **System, decision, objective.** A source sends symbols over a discrete noisy channel. Objective: reproduce the message at the receiver with arbitrarily small error frequency.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Channel noise characterised by conditional probabilities; equivocation H_y(x). Modelled, not measured.
4. **Dependence.** The channel statistics are known and fixed; noise and source are separate statistical processes. [Inference] The theorem is stated for a specified stochastic channel model; its behaviour under a mismatched model is not part of the theorem.
5. **Mechanism.** Correction by encoding: redundancy added by a code lets the receiver correct errors.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** [Source] "It is possible to send information at the rate C through the channel with as small a frequency of errors or equivocation as desired by proper encoding. This statement is not true for any rate greater than C." Theorem 11: if the source entropy H is at most C, a code exists with arbitrarily small error frequency. The proof is, in Shannon's words, "not a pure existence proof" but "has some of the deficiencies of such proofs"; no explicit code is given. Block length grows as the error target falls. Numerical effect: none.
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** Above capacity, error cannot be driven to zero (equivocation at least the excess). Requires a code agreed by sender and receiver and a channel model.
8. **Evidence type.** Theorem. Constructive codes came later and were not read. No conflicts.
9. **Jev relevance.** Speculative, analogy only. [Inference] The theorem separates "reliable below a capacity limit" from "impossible above" and shows errors can be removed by encoding rather than by a better channel. A semantic decision has no sender-side encoding of the intended action, the "message" is not a symbol sequence, and model error is not independent of the message. A structure with the same logic would need an explicit redundancy code on decisions, which none of the sources studied here provides for semantic choices.
10. **Cost.** Coding rate loss (rate below capacity), block length and decoding delay.

## S-10 Reliable cellular automata with bounded-probability faults (Gács 2001)

1. **Source.** Gács, P. (2001), "Reliable Cellular Automata with Self-Organization", *Journal of Statistical Physics* 103: 45-267; corrected arXiv version math/0003117v2 (January 2024 printing), https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0003117. Read: abstract and the noise-model passage around Eq. 7.7. The one-dimensional construction (about 9,700 extracted text lines) was not read in detail. Accessed 2026-10-06.
2. **System, decision, objective.** A one-dimensional asynchronous probabilistic cellular automaton in which every local transition has positive probability. Objective: keep information and carry out computation indefinitely despite faults with small probability ε.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Local transitions deviate from the rule with probability bounded by ε. [Source] Faults need not be strictly independent: the model requires that the probability of violating local conditions in disjoint windows is bounded by the product of per-window bounds ("these violations happen 'independently'", Eq. 7.7). Assumed (model).
4. **Dependence.** Across cells and time, bounded by the product condition; the paper discusses a perturbation process created by an "adversary" within those probability bounds.
5. **Mechanism.** Correction by hierarchy: blocks of cells simulate a more reliable automaton, which in turn is simulated by still larger blocks, with continuous self-repair of the "software" hierarchy.
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** Infinite or arbitrarily large systems and unbounded time are the target. Effect sizes and overhead figures: unreported in this card (not extracted).
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** [Source] Simpler constructions do not "help much" for remembering a bit in 1D, computing in dimensions below 3, or non-synchronised transitions, which is why a long hierarchical construction is needed. Faults must have small bounded probability ε; this is a threshold-style guarantee.
8. **Evidence type.** Theorem (long proof). A reader's guide and later papers exist (search results only; not checked). No code or data.
9. **Jev relevance.** Speculative. [Inference] It shows that local repair rules plus hierarchy can contain faults that are not strictly independent, under a product bound. Missing: an explicit, bounded local error model; a specified computation; hierarchical redundancy. It says nothing about semantic errors.
10. **Cost.** Large overhead (blocks simulating cells across levels) and continuous repair work; overhead not extracted.

## S-11 N-version programming with AI coding agents (Ron, Baudry, Monperrus 2026; revisiting Knight and Leveson 1986)

1. **Source.** Ron, J., Baudry, B., Monperrus, M. (2026), "N-Version Programming with Coding Agents", arXiv:2606.20158 (18 June 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20158; read: abstract, method, RQ2 and RQ3 results. Code at https://github.com/ASSERT-KTH/Knight-Leveson-Redux (as stated in the paper; not opened). Background, **secondary account only**: Knight, J. C., Leveson, N. G. (1986), "An Experimental Evaluation of the Assumption of Independence in Multiversion Programming", *IEEE Trans. Software Eng.* 12(1): 96-109; the original was not opened; the abstract was read in a course summary at https://www.csc.kth.se/utbildning/kth/kurser/DA2210/vettig13/Seminarier/KnightLeveson.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-06. Preprint; KTH and Université de Montréal authors.
2. **System, decision, objective.** AI coding agents (five harnesses, 23 models, three languages; 69 [harness, model, language] triples, of which 48 passed acceptance) implement the Launch Interceptor Program specification; three-version units use majority voting. Objective: failure count on 1,000,000 random test inputs.
3. **Error meaning and measurement.** Disagreement with a shared oracle on a random test input. Natural errors, measured. Knight-Leveson: 27 student programs, one million tests (course summary).
4. **Dependence.** Across versions, measured by coincident-failure counts against an independence prediction (Bernoulli model, z-test) and pairwise phi. Specification ambiguity acts as a shared cause.
5. **Mechanism.** Absorption by majority voting over redundant implementations (N-modular redundancy in software).
6. **Comparison, size, horizon, metric, effect.** [Source] Independence rejected: 429 coincident-failure cases observed vs 115.36 expected (about 3.7 times; z = 29.20). Per-language ratios from 17.5 times (Python) to 155.1 times (Pascal). Of 146 cross-language pairs with defined phi, 81 have phi = 1. Majority voting over three-version units: mean failure count falls from 387.44 (single versions) to 130.99 (triples); 11,844 units show zero observed failures (of 17,296 possible triples). [Source, Knight-Leveson via the course summary] programs were individually "extremely reliable" but "the number of tests in which more than one program failed was substantially more than expected".
7. **Conditions and failure modes.** Failures concentrate where the specification is hard or ambiguous. Crossing agent or language boundaries does not remove perfectly correlated failure clusters. The comparison is with the average single program, not the best version. One specification, one task.
8. **Evidence type.** Empirical (evaluated implementation) and a replication-style study of an older experiment; the 1986 result is known here only through a secondary source. No independent replication of the 2026 result. Conflicts: none stated.
9. **Jev relevance.** Mechanistically analogous, and one of the few cases combining a formal redundancy scheme with a measured departure from independence. Missing assumptions: a task with an oracle; deterministic output comparison; an analogue of the specification-ambiguity cause for game decisions. [Inference] Ambiguity in a prompt or option set could play the same role.
10. **Cost.** Producing and running N versions (N times development and execution cost) plus a voter.
