Written for: `devdocs/episodes/study/prompt_best_practice/p1` step1 ([plan](../../devdocs/episodes/study/prompt_best_practice/p1/plan.md)). Compiled 2026-10-03 from the lab's own reports. Every row below is a **lab result** (measured here, on the lab's own synthetic or authored cases), kept separate from external sources. Nothing here is a literature finding. Numbers are copied from the cited reports; where a number comes from a result table under `lab/` (because the report only gives it in aggregate) the row says so.

## How to read the tables

Readout sources: [set3 p2 report](../../devdocs/episodes/experiments/set3-weakstrong/p2/report.md) (new-system setup table), [set5 p1 report2](../../devdocs/episodes/experiments/set5-regressive_repeat/p1/report2.md) (W12 and A27 read option-letter logits, K27 scores spans with a pointer head, L27 generates).

**Strength labels**

- **primary test:** a pre-registered paired test with a stated multiplicity correction. Several are inconclusive; "inconclusive" is not "no effect".
- **descriptive:** counts or rates on a frozen set without a pre-registered test, or tests that were not corrected.
- **development only:** run on development or screening data used to design a later arm; not evidence for the effect.

**Common caveats (apply to every row)**

- All cases were written by one agent family (Claude). Labels were checked by a blind agent (mostly terra), not by humans. There is no human-written or naturally occurring input except a small set of public items in set3 p2 (CLINC150, Banking77, VitaminC, SQuAD 2.0), which are probably in training data.
- Systems differ in weights, precision, runtime, readout and native prompt. A between-system difference is a system difference, not a controlled effect of one design choice. The within-system rows (same system, one design change) are the cleaner evidence.
- Repeat noise is zero for the local deterministic systems (checked), so a changed answer under a changed input is caused by the change. Hosted Jev is not bit-deterministic (probabilities differ by up to 0.12; same choice 60/60).
- Case classes: W1 routing/matching, W2 sufficiency, W3 policy and priority application (p3 definitions). "Fallback" means a case whose gold is an abstain-type option (out of scope, no matching candidate, missing evidence, conflicting evidence, explicit defer).

## Questions for the external search

For each question, what to check against published work, and the lab fact that prompts the question.

**Q1 Instructions**
- Does published work separate effects of instruction length, structure and terseness for classification-style or multiple-choice decisions, and does the direction depend on model type? Lab fact: terse F1 helped K27 on W3 (15/20 to 18/20; E-fb 4/9 to 7/9) but hurt A27 on W2 (19/20 to 16/20), and the dev pilot saw terse push A27 and K27 towards over-rejection (Q1-1 to Q1-4).
- Evidence on "decisiveness", "do not assume defaults" and "if none, clarify" clauses, and on a data-boundary sentence; the lab confounded the sentence with base model, readout and training (Q1-9, Q1-10).
- Distractor and irrelevant-context effects for scorers (the lab saw 23 new failures on adding visible state to J, Q1-8).
- How prompt-sensitivity is measured and reported (spread statistics inflate with number of variants and small n; the lab's F1-F3 are unreviewed).

**Q2 Options**
- Guidance on key naming (keys that name actions invite override text), description length and concreteness, "choose this when" forms, near-neighbour options, number of options, and how to express "none of these", "need more information" and hand-off (Q2-1, Q2-2, Q2-6).
- Effect of a separate fallback option versus a threshold or abstain head, and of fallback position in the list (W1 fallback options sit last).
- Whether encoders and cross-encoders have label-description guidance (GLiNER, GLiClass) that explains J's and G's failures to abstain (Q5-1, Q6-6).

**Q3 Order and format**
- Typical size of option-order and answer-symbol effects for letter-logit readers and for pointer or encoder scorers; known countermeasures (permutation averaging, prior estimation, calibration of the symbol prior) and their cost. Lab fact: W12, K27, A27, D35 change 1-3 of 60 under reversal, J 21, G 14, L27 6.
- Whether letter versus key-text symbols matter (not varied in the lab, Q3-7).

**Q4 Second passes**
- Conditions under which self-verification, self-correction, re-asking, elimination and top-k re-ranking work; known failure without external feedback; whether any result transfers to scorers that do not generate. Lab fact: no narrowed second pass helped K27; probabilities after dropping options are the first pass renormalised (Q4-6 to Q4-8).
- Voting over prompts versus over models, and the effect of diversity (wordings agreed on 107 of 120 W3 cases; the three-model vote was worse than the base, Q4-1, Q4-4).
- Order disagreement as an uncertainty or error signal (6 fired, 5 wrong, descriptive); is this a known quantity under another name (inconsistency under permutation, consistency-based confidence)? (Q4-9)
- Whether a verifier of the same family inherits the generator's error (Q4-12).

**Q5 Abstention**
- Results on "none of the above" penalties, unanswerable questions and missing-premise detection, and wording that raises abstention at the cost of coverage. Lab fact: the shared failure is an unstated decisive fact with a plausible default, and a blind check of whether a decisive fact is unknown said yes on 0 of 17 false executions (Q4-2, Q5-3, Q5-6).
- Selective-prediction or conformal methods for scorers whose errors are high-confidence (Q4-10).
- Explicit "not stated" markers and two-stage sufficiency gates (planned in set3, not run; Q4-11).

**Q6 Readout dependence**
- Which of Q1-Q5 findings are known to hold only for generative models; evidence for encoder, cross-encoder, option-logit and pointer-head scorers specifically.

**Q7 Reinvention (experiments to compare with the literature)**
- **p3 F0-F3 wording family and order diagnostics (D1, D2, D3):** Q1-1 to Q1-3, Q3-1, Q3-2, Q1-11. Check against prompt-sensitivity and option-order literature, permutation-based debiasing, and the position-recall measure (RStd).
- **set4 WV (wording vote), VB (blind verification), VS (shown verification), MV (model vote) and abstention (unanimity rules and random-review reference):** Q4-1 to Q4-5, Q4-12.
- **set5 D2 (top two, both orders), Dex (first choice vs best fallback), T3 (top three), F2 (re-ask naming the first choice):** Q4-6 to Q4-8.
- **set5 D2 order-disagreement flag:** Q4-9. Compare with permutation consistency measures and with PriDe-style debiasing, which the lab did not run (D2 was used as a re-ask, not as a debiaser).
- **Proposed terse-wording experiment** (plan.md, from p3 F1 on K27 W3): Q1-2, Q1-3, Q1-4. Check whether a terse-instruction effect on option-scoring models is reported, and whether the opposite signs in Q1-3 and Q5-10 are expected.

**Methodological cautions to verify when comparing**
- The p3 F1-F3 wordings were authored after the freeze and not meaning-reviewed; F1 is 0.77 of F0 length (design asked 40-60%).
- set4 and set5 share one fresh set (second use); n of fallback cases is 60; the primary tests had 2-4 discordant pairs and are inconclusive.
- The set5 D2 and set4 comparisons are on one base model (K27); the lab states they say nothing about generative reasoning models.
