# Evidence note Q1/Q2: instruction wording, distractors, compression, option keys, descriptions, option count, near-neighbours, "none of these"

Written for `devdocs/episodes/study/prompt_best_practice/p1` step2 and step3. Access date for every link: **2026-10-03**. All items below are **external sources**, kept apart from the lab's own results in [../lab-evidence.md](../lab-evidence.md). Overlapping topics are covered in [q3-order-and-format.md](q3-order-and-format.md) (order, option-ID bias, permutation methods), [q5-abstention.md](q5-abstention.md) and [q6-readout-and-vendor.md](q6-readout-and-vendor.md); where a source also appears there, only the Q1/Q2 facts are recorded here.

## How to read this note

**Readout match to the lab's systems.** Lab systems: option-letter logits (W12, A27), pointer-head span scores (K27), encoders (J, G), short generative output (L27).
- **matching**: the source reads next-token or option-ID probabilities over a closed option list, or scores labels with an encoder.
- **partial**: the source reads probabilities over label words or a verbaliser, trains the system itself, or uses a related-but-different scorer (for example a bilinear scorer over type embeddings).
- **not matching**: the source reads free generation (including chain-of-thought) and parses the answer.
No source in this note used a pointer-head scorer like K27; the closest items are marked "partial".

**Grades.** peer-reviewed controlled (venue verified in the paper header, listing, or abstract page) / preprint with code or data / vendor or author evaluation / practitioner / guide. "Venue not checked" means I did not verify publication; the item is treated as a preprint.

**Depth of reading.** "Read (full text)" means I downloaded the PDF and read the passage that supports the claim. "Abstract only" means the claim rests on the abstract or on a fetch summary and the supporting passage was not read. "Fetch summary" means a web page was condensed by the fetch tool and numbers were not independently checked. Claims from abstract-only or summary-only sources are weaker.

**Ground rule applied.** Averages over tasks or models are recorded as averages. Task-dependent and negative results sit next to the positive ones.

## Summary by topic (what the evidence supports, with scope)

| Topic | What the sources support | Readout scope | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Format and paraphrase sensitivity | Large spreads from meaning-preserving changes in formatting or instruction wording are well documented in several independent controlled studies. Spread persists with scale and instruction tuning in some studies (Sclar), shrinks with scale in others (ProSA, He et al.). Single-prompt comparisons can reverse model rankings. Recommended reporting: a distribution or interval over many plausible formats, plus instance-level inconsistency. | Mostly generation or base-model probability ranking; partial for letter logits | Established as a phenomenon; effect sizes are task- and model-specific |
| Instruction length and structure for classification | One controlled study (PLOS ONE 2026, generation) shows task-dependent value of longer category definitions: no gain where labels are self-explanatory, +17 to +37 points where the task needs the definitions. No source found that isolates terse versus full instruction for option-logit, pointer or encoder scorers. | not matching for the only controlled source | Open for the lab's readouts |
| "Meaning of the instruction" | Models often do about as well with irrelevant or misleading templates as with instructive ones, at least for NLI with small or older models; target (verbaliser) words matter more than template semantics. | partial (rank classification of target words; encoder and older LLMs) | Plausible; dated models |
| Distractors and irrelevant context | Degradation from irrelevant text is documented on generative models (arithmetic, multi-hop QA, RAG), including relevance-neutral padding. One RAG study found random documents helped, which contradicts a simple "irrelevant text hurts" rule. No source on encoders or option-logit scorers. | not matching | Established for generation; open for lab readouts |
| Prompt compression | Compression papers target long contexts and demonstrations. Short-context quality falls as compression rises; moderate compression helps only for long contexts. Nothing found on compressing short classification instructions or option lists. | not matching | Gap |
| Option keys / answer symbols | For option-ID readers, replacing A/B/C/D with other symbols does not remove the symbol bias; removing IDs and scoring option text reduces it. Tokenisation of the space before the letter changes accuracy by up to 11 points on small base models and little on large instruct models. Label-word choice moves zero-shot F1 by more than 0.1 on every dataset tested (stance, 8B models). | matching (Zheng, Sanz-Guerrero, Alzahrani), partial (LOADS) | Established for letter-logit readers on base and chat models up to 70B |
| Label and option descriptions | Descriptions or definitions improve results in several settings (LLM zero-shot labelling, zero-shot NER with a bilinear scorer, label-description training for an MLM encoder). Short elaborations of single label words gave limited average effect. No ablation was found for GLiNER2 or GLiClass descriptions. | partial | Plausible; effect depends on how non-obvious the label is |
| Number of options | High option counts hurt when all options and descriptions are in one prompt (Banking77, 77 labels: 4.0 F1 for Mistral-7B). MMLU-Pro's drop from 4 to 10 options is confounded with harder questions. Encoder authors report degradation with very large label sets. | partial | Plausible; confounded evidence |
| Near-neighbour options | Preprint (abstract only): top-K retrieval does not help the model tell similar labels apart; adding known confusable partners plus pairwise rules gave up to +10.0 points macro-F1. Vendor docs recommend `not_for` fields. | not matching / vendor | Plausible; thin |
| "None of these" and hand-off | Post-trained chat models often pick an invalid option even when told none may be correct; base models do better and improve with size. An explicit alert helped some models and did not help others. Vendor guidance recommends an `other` option. | not matching | Established for chat models on arithmetic; open for scorers |

## Q1. Sensitivity to format and paraphrase, and how to report it

### Sclar et al. 2023/2024, "Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324 (ICLR 2024, header verified). Read (full text), sections 4.1-4.3 and the introduction.
- Models/sizes: LLaMA-2-7B/13B/70B, Falcon-7B and 7B-Instruct, GPT-3.5-Turbo. Readout: ranking accuracy over valid options by output probability (default); exact prefix match as second metric. Task: 53 Super-NaturalInstructions tasks (19 multiple-choice, 34 classification), English, 1,000 samples per task, few-shot formats that vary separators, casing and enumeration style (not instruction wording).
- Effect: spread up to 76 accuracy points on LLaMA-2-13B between formats of one task; median spread 7.5 points across tasks (10 randomly sampled formats, which the authors call a lower bound); 20% of tasks at least 15 points for all LLaMA-2 settings (section 4.2). With 320 formats GPT-3.5 reaches 56 points, median 6.4 (introduction). Spread persists with model size, instruction tuning and more shots (Figure 2). Model comparisons reverse under a different format with probability 0.141 (LLaMA-2-13B vs 70B, margin 0.02), and both paired comparisons were significant in 76% and 47% of cases (McNemar, section 4.2). 11% of single-constant changes shift probability-ranking accuracy by 5 points or more, 24% under exact match (Figure 6). Format preferences correlate only weakly between models (abstract).
- Significance: McNemar tests for the reversal analysis; spread itself is descriptive. Replicated in direction by the sources below.
- Grade: peer-reviewed controlled. Match: partial (probability ranking over options, but base and early chat models, few-shot template features).
- Caution: "up to 76" is the maximum over 53 tasks; the median is 7.5.

### Mizrahi et al. 2024, "State of What Art? A Call for Multi-Prompt LLM Evaluation"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00595 (TACL per abstract page). Read (full text), section 4 and Table 3-4.
- Models: 20 LLMs (16 instruction-tuned evaluated per task; 11 on BBH). Tasks: 39 tasks from LMentry, BIG-bench Hard and BIG-bench Lite (analysis on 25 tasks, incl. classification and multiple-choice). Readout: generation with exact string match (BBH) or the official LMentry script. n = 100 samples per template; about 175-200 automatic paraphrases of the instruction template per task, then manually filtered (human check: about 85% of 375 sampled paraphrases valid, Table 3).
- Effect: Friedman test finds significant ranking differences across paraphrases on 21 of 25 tasks; 15 of 25 tasks have negative average Kendall's tau between paraphrase rankings (section 4.2). One word edit ("excludes" to "lacks") changed Flan-T5-large by -28% and Flan-T5-XL by +46% (section 4.2). Proposes reporting max, average, median and a quantile over paraphrases instead of one prompt.
- Grade: peer-reviewed controlled. Match: not matching (generation). Caution: paraphrases are machine-made and about 15% were invalid before filtering; ranking instability is across models, not accuracy of one model.

### Voronov, Wolf, Ryabinin 2024, "Mind Your Format"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06766 (arXiv v3, Jun 2024; venue not checked). Read (partial: abstract, section 4.1, Table 3, section 4.2-4.3).
- Models: 21 models, 770M to 70B (19 base, plus instruction-tuned in Appendix L). Tasks: four classification datasets (SST-2, DBPedia, AG News, TREC, English). 10 random templates times 3 demonstration sets, 30 runs per cell.
- Effect: a poor template can reduce the strongest models to random-guess level; the standard deviation across templates reaches up to 35% of the mean even for the largest models (section 4.1). No specific template component (verbalisers, separators) could be excluded (Appendix D); a combination of "suboptimal" parts can be optimal. Best templates do not transfer between models of the same family. Calibration and channel prediction methods raise mean accuracy, but calibration was more template-sensitive than channel (Table 10 per text). Template ensembling is proposed as a test-time fix.
- Grade: preprint (venue not checked). Match: partial (in-context classification, base models; readout not extracted).

### Other sensitivity and reporting sources
- **PromptEval** (Polo et al., 2024): https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17202. Abstract and introduction only. Estimates the performance distribution over about 100 prompt templates on MMLU, BBH, LMentry for a budget of about two single-prompt evaluations; quantile metrics instead of a single prompt. Preprint with code (venue not checked). Match: not matching (method for evaluation design).
- **POSIX** (Chatterjee et al., 2024): https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02185, Findings of EMNLP 2024. Read (Table 1, section 5). Eight 7-8B models (LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3, Mistral, OLMo; base and instruct) on MMLU multiple-choice and Alpaca. Sensitivity is the relative change in response log-likelihood under paraphrase, template or spelling changes. Result: prompt template is the most sensitive variation type for multiple choice, paraphrase for open-ended; parameter count and instruction tuning do not necessarily lower sensitivity, few-shot helps somewhat. Peer-reviewed. Match: partial (log-likelihood readout, small models).
- **ProSA** (Zhuo et al., 2024): https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12405. Read (abstract, Figure 1-2, model list). Eight instruct models (including Llama3-70B-Instruct, Qwen1.5-7B to 72B, InternLM2, Mistral-7B) on ARC-Challenge, CommonsenseQA, MATH, HumanEval, 12 prompt variants, greedy decoding. Introduces an instance-level sensitivity score (PromptSensiScore): the expected inconsistency in correctness between two prompts for the same item. Findings: sensitivity varies by dataset and model; larger models more robust; few-shot examples reduce sensitivity; higher decoding confidence goes with higher robustness. Preprint with code. Match: not matching (generation); the instance-level inconsistency idea is relevant to any "disagreement under rewording" flag.
- **MMLU-Pro** (Wang et al., 2024): https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01574. Read (abstract, section 6). With 24 different prompt styles, score range under prompt changes is typically 4-5% on MMLU (peak 10.98%) and about 2% on MMLU-Pro (max 3.74%). Confounded: MMLU-Pro also changes the questions, uses 10 options, and is scored with 5-shot chain-of-thought. Preprint (venue not checked). Match: not matching.
- **Alzahrani et al. 2024**, "When Benchmarks are Targets": https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01781. Read (sections 1, 5.2-5.4). Minor perturbations (choice order, symbols, scoring method) change MMLU rankings by up to 8 positions. Section 5.4 is a negative result: informative changes to few-shot context, such as trivial or off-subject examples, change performance only slightly and reduce bias. Preprint (arXiv v2, venue not checked). Match: partial (symbol scoring versus cloze versus hybrid, open models up to 70B).
- **He et al. 2024**, "Does Prompt Formatting Have Any Impact on LLM Performance?": https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10541. Abstract only. GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4; plain text, Markdown, JSON, YAML; up to 40% variation for GPT-3.5 on code translation; GPT-4 more robust. Preprint. Match: not matching.

## Q1. Instruction content: what the wording needs to say

### Webson and Pavlick 2022, "Do Prompt-Based Models Really Understand the Meaning of their Prompts?"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01247 (arXiv v2, Apr 2022; venue not checked). Read (full text), sections 4-5, Table 2, Figures 3-8.
- Models: ALBERT (235M), T5 LM-adapted (770M, 3B, 11B), T0 (3B, 11B, T0++), GPT-3 175B. Task: NLI (RTE; ANLI also tried), English, rank classification of target words, 4-256 shots plus zero-shot. More than 30 hand-written templates in five categories (instructive, misleading-moderate, misleading-extreme, irrelevant, null), all written before evaluation.
- Effect: models learn about as fast with irrelevant or misleading templates as with instructive ones, with the exception of T0++ at zero shots and some model and category pairs (Table 2). Scale does not fix it (GPT-3). Section 5: the choice of target words matters much more than the template (yes/no >> yes-no-like >> arbitrary >> reversed targets); Figure 8.
- Significance: independent two-sample t-test and Wilcoxon rank-sum with Bonferroni correction (Table 2).
- Grade: controlled study, older models. Match: partial (rank classification over label words, includes the encoder ALBERT). Caution: pre-2023 models and a single task family; it shows that these models do not use the template semantics, not that wording never matters for current instruction-tuned models.

### Celebi and Penczynski 2026, "Much Ado about Prompting: LLM classification of text messages from experiments"
- Link: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0354757 (PLOS ONE; also https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13436748/). **Fetch summary only**; numbers below were not checked against the PDF.
- Models: GPT 5.4 and 5.4-nano, Qwen 3.5 397B and 9B. Tasks: promise classification (1,494 utterances), two level-k reasoning classification tasks (493 and 851 messages); experimental-economics chat text, two human annotators. Readout: generation of structured JSON. Conditions: minimal one-line category descriptions, verbatim codebook, worked examples, content compression levels (25-100% of verbatim detail).
- Effect (Table 3): promise task minimal to verbatim: GPT 5.4 73.2% to 74.2% (not significant), GPT 5.4-nano 60.9 to 64.3, Qwen 397B 70.5 to 66.2, Qwen 9B 64.1 to 63.3. Level-k I: GPT 5.4 47.4 to 81.3, nano 52.5 to 70.2, Qwen 397B 50.1 to 83.0, Qwen 9B 53.1 to 70.8. Level-k II: GPT 5.4 49.6 to 81.0, nano 53.1 to 68.9, Qwen 397B 43.6 to 74.6, Qwen 9B 38.8 to 64.4. Removing detail from the codebook "drops accuracy sharply on theory-dependent tasks"; frontier models plateau at high detail; smaller models do best with instructions but little context and extra context can hurt them. McNemar tests, paired design.
- Grade: peer-reviewed, controlled, one domain. Match: not matching (generation). The pattern is task-dependent: detailed instructions matter where the category is not self-explanatory.
- Caution: the authors call experimental-economics text a best-case setting; results may not transfer.

### Shi et al. 2023, "Large Language Models Can Be Easily Distracted by Irrelevant Context"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093 (arXiv v3; venue not checked). Read (sections 1, 5.1, Table 3).
- Models: code-davinci-002 mainly, text-davinci-003; arithmetic word problems (GSM-IC, 4,000 sampled problems; 100 base problems). Readout: generated reasoning plus answer.
- Effect: accuracy drops sharply with an irrelevant sentence added; among base problems solved by baseline prompts, no more than 18% are solved consistently for all distractor types. Adding the instruction "feel free to ignore irrelevant information in the problem description" raised overall micro accuracy for chain-of-thought from 72.4 to 77.8 (code-davinci-002, exemplars without distractors); for text-davinci-003 from 68.4 to 71.3; for least-to-most 77.5 to 80.6 and 76.3 to 76.7 (Table 3). Exemplars with distractors helped more. Self-consistency raised accuracy further.
- Grade: preprint (peer review not checked). Match: not matching. Effect of the instruction is a few points and not significance-tested.

### Data-boundary sentences and delimiters
- **Hines et al. 2024, "Defending Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks With Spotlighting"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720. Read (section 5.1). Microsoft preprint. GPT-3.5-Turbo and text-davinci-003, document summarisation and Q&A. A defensive instruction alone had negligible effect on attack success rate with GPT-3.5-Turbo; adding delimiters roughly halved it; datamarking cut it from about 50% to below 3% (GPT-3.5-Turbo) and from 40% to 0% (text-davinci-003). Generation, older models, author-chosen attacks. Match: not matching.
- **Jia et al. 2025, "A Critical Evaluation of Defenses against Prompt Injection Attacks"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18333. Read (introduction and related work only). Preprint. Its related-work section states, citing a prior benchmark, that earlier prevention methods such as wrapping data in delimiters or XML tags "are largely ineffective" in utility or effectiveness. This is second-hand characterisation, not a result I checked. Contradicts the spotlighting numbers in part; the two papers use different models and attack sets.
- Net: delimiters and datamarking have measured effects on generative models; effect on logit-readout, pointer or encoder scorers: not found.

### Principled instruction lists
- **Bsharat et al. 2023, "Principled Instructions Are All You Need"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16171. Read (abstract, sections 1, 4). 26 principles; 20 human-selected questions per principle with and without the principle; LLaMA-1/2 7B-70B, GPT-3.5, GPT-4; a single response per question, human-compared. Reported average boost 57.7% in quality and 36.4% in correctness for GPT-4. Averages across principles, author-built benchmark, generation. Grade: preprint, low weight. Match: not matching. Do not read as evidence for any single wording.

## Q1. Irrelevant context and distractors

- **Levy, Jacoby, Goldberg 2024, "Same Task, More Tokens"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14848 (arXiv v2; venue not checked). Read (abstract, sections 1-4). GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Gemini Pro, Mistral Medium, Mixtral 8x7B on a reasoning QA set with padding of 250-3,000 tokens (about 600 samples per point). Average accuracy over models falls from 0.92 to 0.68 at 3,000 tokens; the drop appears even with duplicated relevant text, and padding drawn from a books corpus hurt more than similar-task padding (Figure 5). Chain-of-thought did not remove the trend. Match: not matching. Note for scale: the lab's long-context axis used about 2,800 characters, below the range where this study sees most of the decline.
- **Cuconasu et al. 2024, "The Power of Noise"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14887 (SIGIR 2024, verified in paper header). Read (abstract, section 5). Llama2-7B, Falcon-7B, Phi-2, MPT-7B on NQ-open with 5-token answers. One distracting (related, non-answering) document lowers accuracy by up to 0.24 (25%); more distractors reduce accuracy by up to 0.38 (67%). **Contradicting result:** adding random unrelated documents raised accuracy by up to 0.07 (+35%) in some settings (abstract, section 5), and the authors present it as counter-intuitive. Match: not matching. Do not assume that unrelated text always hurts.
- **Yoran et al. 2024, "Making Retrieval-Augmented Language Models Robust to Irrelevant Context"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01558 (ICLR 2024, header). Read (abstract, sections 1-2). Llama-2-13B few-shot on five QA tasks; retrieval can reduce accuracy; an NLI filter (BART-large-MNLI, 407M) prevents harm at the cost of discarding relevant passages; fine-tuning on a mix of relevant and irrelevant contexts (1,000 examples suffice) restores robustness. Peer-reviewed. Match: not matching for the main result; the NLI filter is an encoder used as a gate, not a wording result.
- **Lost in the Middle** (Liu et al. 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172) was downloaded but not read for this note; not used.
- No source found on irrelevant-context effects for encoder, cross-encoder, option-logit or pointer scorers. Gap.

## Q1. Prompt compression and removing text

- **Jiang et al. 2023, LLMLingua**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05736 (EMNLP 2023 per preprint format; not verified). Read (abstract and Table 1). GSM8K, BBH, ShareGPT, Arxiv-March23; GPT-3.5-Turbo and Claude as target; up to 20x compression of long prompts with little loss on reasoning and in-context learning tasks (GSM8K exact match 77.33 at 20x in Table 1). Targets demonstrations and long contexts, not short instructions. Match: not matching.
- **Zhang et al. 2025, "An Empirical Study on Prompt Compression for LLMs"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00019 (ICLR 2025 workshop, header). Read (abstract and the bullet findings). Six methods, 13 datasets, Claude-3-Haiku and others. Findings: for short contexts all methods lose performance as the ratio rises; for long contexts moderate compression can improve performance (LongBench); compression changes response length and increases hallucination. Match: not matching. Workshop paper.
- **Pre-registered randomised trial of prompt compression**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23525 (abstract and introduction only). Claude Sonnet 4.5, 358 successful runs (59-61 per arm) on production task-orchestration instructions; retention r = 0.8, 0.5, 0.2. r about 0.5 cut total cost 27.9%; r about 0.2 raised cost by 1.8% because output tokens grew; quality measured by embedding similarity of the response, not task accuracy. Preprint. Match: not matching. A negative result against "compress more".
- **Prompt compression in the wild**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02985 (abstract only). LLMLingua gives up to 18% end-to-end speed-up when prompt length, ratio and hardware match; response quality statistically unchanged on summarisation, code generation and QA. Preprint.
- Net: no source studies compressing a short classification instruction or option text, or the effect on logit-readout scorers. The only guidance is to compress long contexts moderately and to measure quality. Gap.
- Downloaded but not read for this note: LLMLingua-2 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12968), a prompt-compression survey (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12388).

## Q2. Option keys, answer symbols and label words

### Zheng et al. 2023/2024, "Large Language Models Are Not Robust Multiple Choice Selectors"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882 (ICLR 2024, header verified). Read (sections 1, 2.3-3.3, 4.3, Table 2). Covered in depth in [q3-order-and-format.md](q3-order-and-format.md); Q2 facts only.
- Models: llama-7B to 65B, llama-2 7B/13B/70B (base and chat), vicuna, falcon, gpt-3.5-turbo; 20 models in total. Readout: for open models, probability of the option-ID tokens A/B/C/D(/E), argmax; for gpt-3.5-turbo, first generated token. Tasks: MMLU, ARC-Challenge (4 options), CommonsenseQA (5 options), 0-shot and 5-shot, English.
- Effect: moving the gold answer to position D lowers gpt-3.5-turbo from 67.2 to 60.9; moving it to A raises llama-30B by 15.2. Replacing A/B/C/D by a/b/c/d, 1/2/3/4 or (A)/(B)/(C)/(D) did not reduce the standard deviation of per-position recall (RStd) remarkably (Table 2, gpt-3.5-turbo, MMLU: default 5.5; a/b/c/d 6.8; 1/2/3/4 3.8; (A)(B)(C)(D) 8.1; ARC 3.3, 2.1, 2.1, 4.0). Removing option IDs and scoring option text lowers RStd (MMLU 5.5 to 1.0) with accuracy about unchanged (67.2 to 66.7). The authors conclude token bias on option IDs is the main intrinsic cause and position bias is smaller and irregular. Shuffling the ID order did not reduce RStd either. Simple debiasing instructions and chain-of-thought gave little (RStd 6.1 and 4.5).
- Grade: peer-reviewed controlled. Match: **matching** for W12 and A27 (ID-token logits), but base and chat models of 2023, 4-5 options.
- Caution: the number of options varied only from two to five.

### Alzahrani et al. 2024 (see above, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01781), section 5.3
- Replacing the default symbols with rare symbols and shuffling them: models always show bias toward symbols, and even after shuffling the symbols the bias changes in unpredictable ways (Figure 6, Table A.8). The authors recommend hybrid scoring (cloze plus symbol) over symbol scoring on MMLU and ARC-C (Figure 7). Preprint. Match: partial.

### Sanz-Guerrero, Bui, von der Wense 2025, "Mind the Gap: A Closer Look at Tokenization for MCQA"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15020 (arXiv, Sep 2025; venue not checked). Read (abstract, Table 1-2, sections 4-5).
- Fifteen LLMs (Llama 2/3.1, Gemma 3, Mistral, Qwen 2.5/3, GPT-Neo; base and instruct, 2.7B to 72B). Readout: next-token logits restricted to the option letters, zero-shot, English. MMLU plus five other datasets.
- Effect: tokenising the space before the letter together with the letter (token "space-X" instead of "X") changes accuracy by up to 11% and calibration; on MMLU zero-shot: Llama-3.1-8B 61.47 to 63.93 (significant), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 67.28 to 68.73, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct 82.31 to 82.60, Qwen 2.5 72B 81.93 to 83.24, Gemma 3 12B 71.17 to 71.31 (not significant). Model rankings change with the choice (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct first with one convention, Qwen 2.5 72B first with the other). With chain-of-thought the gain shrinks (Llama-3.1-8B 69.64 to 70.11, not significant).
- Significance: McNemar for accuracy, paired bootstrap for ECE, p < 0.05 marked.
- Grade: preprint with controlled analysis. Match: **matching** (letter-logit readout). Large instruct models show small effects; the 11% maximum is on a small model.

### Robinson and Wingate 2023, "Leveraging LLMs for Multiple Choice Question Answering"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12353 (ICLR 2023, header). Read (abstract, section 4-5, Table 1).
- Models: GPT-3 davinci, Instruct davinci, Codex davinci, others; 20 datasets. Multiple-choice prompting (symbol readout) versus cloze scoring. Effect: for models with high "multiple choice symbol binding" ability, multiple-choice prompting beats cloze: OpenBookQA Codex 83.0 (MCP) vs 65.6 (best cloze normalisation), Instruct 77.4 vs 58.4; for GPT-3, which has low symbol binding, cloze wins (OpenBookQA best cloze 57.4 vs MCP 41.4; Table 1). Symbol binding depends on model, with Codex and Instruct much stronger than GPT-3. Peer-reviewed. Match: matching in readout, but 2022 base-family models. Implication: whether a letter-key interface works at all depends on the model's symbol binding, which the fine-tuned W12 and A27 were presumably trained for. (Inference, not tested.)

### "Answer text versus first token"
- **Wang et al. 2024, "My Answer is C"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14499 (Findings of ACL 2024 per abstract page). Abstract only. Instruction-tuned models, especially heavily fine-tuned on conversational or safety data: first-token probabilities and text answers mismatch in over 60% of cases, even when prompts are constrained to start with an option letter. Match: matching readout, with a warning that first-token readout may not reflect the model's text answer for chat-tuned models. The lab's W12 and A27 are fine-tuned decision models (not the models studied); not tested here.
- **Wang et al. 2024, "Look at the Text"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08382. Abstract only. Text answers are more robust to question perturbations than first-token probability when the two disagree; at mismatch above 50%, text answers are more robust to option order than debiased first-token probabilities (PriDe included). Preprint.
- **ABCD: All Biases Come Disguised** (2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17445. Read (abstract, sections 4-5, Table). 13 open models 8B-32B (Qwen3, Llama-3.1, Gemma-3, Ministral-3, Nemotron, Phi-4, GPT-OSS), five benchmarks plus a synthetic random-word set (NonsenseQA, 1,000 questions, 4 options). Proposed protocol: uniform unordered labels ("-"), full-text answer generation, sentence-similarity matching. Mean accuracy variance across answer permutations falls about 3x (geometric mean variance ratio 0.33) with a small drop in mean accuracy; Llama-3.1-8B did not improve (variance ratio 0.96). Preprint. Match: not matching (generation plus embedding matching). It adds evidence that label symbols, not just positions, carry bias, and that labelless options can reduce it for generative readers.

### Holtzman et al. 2021, "Surface Form Competition"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08315. Read (abstract and introduction). GPT-2 and GPT-3 families; zero-shot multiple choice scored by string probability; different surface forms compete for probability mass; domain-conditional PMI reweighting removes much of it and gives consistent gains over calibrated and uncalibrated scoring on all GPT-2/GPT-3 models. Cloze scoring of option text, not letters. Peer-reviewed (header not verified). Match: partial (explains why option wording matters when option text is scored).

### Label-word choice for zero-shot classification
- **Li, Zhao, Scarton, LOADS (2024/2025)**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19195 (arXiv v2, Aug 2025; venue not checked). Read (abstract, section 3, Tables 2-4). Flan-T5-xl (3B), Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Gemma-2-9B and Flan-T5-xxl for generalisation); stance classification on 10 datasets (macro F-score); greedy decoding, zero-shot, prompt lists the label words. Effect: changing only the label words (for example agree/disagree versus support/oppose) moves F-score by more than 0.1 for every dataset and model, by more than 0.2 on more than half the datasets (Table 2: Llama 3 on `emergent`: max 0.740, min 0.387, original 0.467; on `semeval`: 0.688 / 0.544 / 0.643). Some label sets cause degenerate outputs (Flan-T5 always outputs "support" with support/deny/neither). Label order has limited average influence; elaborating a label word with task details has limited average impact (changes above 0.2 on certain dataset/model pairs, in both directions). Their kurtosis-based selection raised F-score in several cases (for example from 0.54 to 0.76 at best). Match: partial (first generated token of the label word; small models).
- **Gao, Ghosh, Gimpel 2023, "The Benefits of Label-Description Training for Zero-Shot Text Classification"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02239 (EMNLP 2023 main, listing verified). Read (abstract, sections 1, 3-4, Tables 2-3). RoBERTa-base and -large with pattern-verbaliser masked-LM scoring (14 patterns for topic, 16 for sentiment; Schick and Schuetze patterns). Fine-tuning on a few minutes of curated label descriptions (related terms, dictionary entries, short templates; no input texts) raises zero-shot accuracy by about 17% (base) and 19% (large) absolute across topic and sentiment datasets, and the standard deviation of accuracy over the 14 patterns is smaller than for zero-shot on every dataset (Table 3). Grade: peer-reviewed controlled. Match: **partial** (encoder with a masked-LM head and verbalisers, not label-in-input encoders like GLiNER, and it trains on the descriptions).
- **Webson and Pavlick** (above): target-word choice matters more than template (Figure 8).
- **Wei et al. 2023, "Larger Language Models Do In-Context Learning Differently"**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03846. Read (abstract). With semantically unrelated labels (foo/bar instead of negative/positive) small models fail and the ability emerges with scale; instruction tuning strengthens reliance on semantic priors more than on in-context mappings. Implication: label names with real semantics carry prior weight that arbitrary keys do not, more so for instruction-tuned models. Preprint (venue not checked). Match: not matching (in-context learning).
- **Yousefiramandi and Cooney 2026**, verbaliser ablation inside a fine-tuned-LLM paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12677. Read (section 4.10, Table 11). Instruction-tuned Mistral-7B-v0.3 with LoRA on a 5-class patent set (CLV) and a 14-class multi-label patent set (WIPO), single seed: single-letter IDs F1 0.753, full names 0.785, JSON 0.850 (CLV); 0.262, 0.277, 0.838 (WIPO). The WIPO spread is mostly parse failures of the strict answer regex, which the authors say. Preprint, one seed, so only a pointer that output format can matter for a fine-tuned generative classifier. Match: not matching for scorers; partial for L27's JSON output.

### Entity-type and label naming for NLI-style classifiers
- **Ghilene et al. 2024**, SUD zero-shot with entailment models: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13735 (conference paper, CMC and Social Media Corpora 2024). Read (section 3.2.1, Table 3). Four NLI models (RoBERTa, BART, mDeBERTa, XLM-RoBERTa) on hate and offensive-speech datasets. Changing the verb in the hypothesis template changed average F1 by about +/-20 (section 3.2.1). The best template per model was chosen on the evaluation data and then reused, so the effect size is not a clean held-out estimate. Grade: workshop-level controlled. Match: partial (NLI cross-encoders; closest to what a J- or G-style label-in-input encoder is).
- **Laurer et al. 2024**, "Building Efficient Universal Classifiers with NLI": https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17543. Read (sections 2-3 and Figure 2). Guide for verbalising each class as an NLI hypothesis ("This text is about {}"). It states the setup and trains on a harmonised mix of NLI and classification data; it does not report a template ablation. The Hugging Face card for the released model (https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/deberta-v3-large-zeroshot-v2.0, fetch summary) says to test different `hypothesis_template` and verbalised class formulations and gives no sensitivity numbers. Grade: guide and author documentation.
- **Aarab 2026, BTZSC**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11991 (ICLR 2026, header). Read (abstract, sections 4-5). 22 English datasets (sentiment, topic, intent, emotion), 38 checkpoints in four families, each label verbalised as a short description by a fixed template ("The overall sentiment within the Amazon product review is {label}"). Instruction-tuned LLMs were scored as multiple choice by the next-token probability of the enumerated options. Results: Qwen3-Reranker-8B macro F1 0.72; best LLM (Mistral-Nemo-12B) 0.67; NLI cross-encoders plateau with size; gemma-3-270m and -1b-it only 0.28 and 0.36 F1. Verbalisation was fixed per dataset, so the paper does not measure the effect of label wording. Match: matching for letter-logit LLMs and for encoders.

## Q2. Label and option descriptions

- **Walshe et al. 2025**, automatic labelling with open LLMs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12332 (preprint, JPMorgan; venue not checked). Read (sections 2.1-2.3, Table 1-3). Mistral-7B zero-shot, macro accuracy, labels only (L, about five words) versus label plus description (L+D, about 50 words, generated by Mistral-7B from a few training examples): AG News 76.2 to 83.4, Banking77 81.6 to 88.8, DBpedia 78.0 to 84.6, Amazon 85.5 to 88.9 (without chain-of-thought; with CoT 80.0 to 84.4, 79.2 to 83.7, 83.1 to 88.8, 86.0 to 89.2). The same paper reports that putting all 77 Banking77 labels with descriptions into one prompt collapses macro F1 to 4.0, and proposes one-label-at-a-time scoring with retrieval. Match: not matching (generation). Caution: descriptions were generated from training examples, the datasets are public, so the gain may include dataset-specific leakage.
- **Ewais et al. 2026, Just Pass Twice (JPT)**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05158 (preprint v3). Read (section 5.1, Table 4 and Table 8). Qwen3-4B with LoRA and a bilinear classifier over entity-type embeddings; zero-shot NER on CrossNER and MIT benchmarks. Dual-channel definitions (embedding and prompt) give +12.6 F1 over no definitions on average; one channel alone limited. Precise definitions with boundary cases improved MIT-Restaurant per-type F1 by +32.6 (LOCATION) and +34.9 (PRICE) against generic definitions (Table 8; the per-type figures are examples the authors chose). Match: **partial** (type embeddings scored by a bilinear head, closest to K27's pointer scoring and to GLiNER; model is a LoRA-adapted LLM trained by the authors).
- **Zamai et al. 2024, SLIMER**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01272 (arXiv v3, Sep 2024; venue not checked). Read (abstract, Table 4 excerpt). Instruction-tuned LLaMA-2-based generative NER; definitions and guidelines in the prompt give better zero-shot performance on unseen entity tags; per-tag gains include +35.18 F1 for "trailer" in Movie. Average gain not extracted. Match: not matching.
- **GLiNER2 paper**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18546. Read (feature table, section 3). States that entity-type descriptions are supported ("richer semantic understanding") and lists "label description" as a GLiNER2 feature absent in GLiNER; **no ablation or effect size for descriptions was found**. The original GLiNER paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08526, read sections 3.2 and 5.3) trains with negative entity types sampled from other examples (50% negative ratio best: F1 60.9 versus 53.3 with none, Table 5), caps entity types at 25 per sentence in training, and shuffles and drops types as regularisation. It evaluated no natural-language label descriptions. Peer-reviewed (GLiNER, NAACL 2024 per usual citation, not verified in the text). Match: matching for encoders (J, G family).
- **GLiClass paper**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07662. Read (parts: data, model overview, scaling discussion; searched the text for label-description content). Uni-encoder with labels prefixed by a label token and concatenated with the text; reports that performance becomes harder with very large label sets and attributes it to positional encoding and attention limits. No label-description evidence. The README and Hugging Face card (https://github.com/Knowledgator/GLiClass, https://huggingface.co/knowledgator/gliclass-instruct-base-v1.0, fetch summaries) mention optional label descriptions and task prompts but give no ablation or guidance on label wording, number of labels or "other" labels. Vendor docs, no effect sizes.
- **Vendor guidance for Jev-type interfaces** (author documentation, fetch summaries, no measured evidence):
  - https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/choice.md: "Write descriptions that separate the options from each other"; descriptions can be null when option names are self-explanatory; up to 255 options; give the full list rather than a shortlist; add an `other` or `none of the above` option when the list may not cover every input; use structured descriptions with `what`, `not_for`, `examples` when options are easily confused; a flat probability shape means low confidence and below 0.3-0.5 should go to manual review.
  - https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/how-to-build-with-system-one.md: ask atomic, narrow questions; make criteria contrastive; use consistent field names across options; test confidence thresholds by plotting confidence against accuracy on your own data.
  - https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/advanced.md: option descriptions may be objects; the `not_for` example "sharpens the boundary between options".
  - https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev: cardinality up to 255; for higher cardinality a two-stage system of scoring independently, then choosing; numbers on latency and cost only.
  - Laya README, https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya (fetch summary): options share a fixed token budget (`head_max_len` 192 or 256 tokens); Banking77 with 77 labels accuracy 0.425 versus 0.870 for Jev (author comparison); "Jev leads on >20 options"; for more than 20 options narrow with a shortlist first; per-slot logits show a position spread decided by position alone (+1.68 to -1.91 across five slots).
  - OpenAI cookbook, https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/using_logprobs (fetch summary): prompt "Classify the article into one of ... Return only the name of the category"; use logprobs as a guardrail, but "validate its classifications and thresholds with task-specific evals". Practitioner guidance.
  - Anthropic guidance (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/use-xml-tags resolved to the general prompting best-practices page): wrap instructions, context and inputs in separate tags to reduce misinterpretation; place long documents before the query, claimed up to 30% quality gain in tests (no data shown). Vendor guide for generative Claude models. A classification use-case page I tried (https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/about-claude/use-case-guides/classification) redirected to a 404.

## Q2. Number of options

- **MMLU-Pro** (above, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01574): moving from four to ten options, together with harder questions and chain-of-thought evaluation, lowers accuracy by 16% to 33% and prompt sensitivity from 4-5% to about 2%. The paper does not isolate option count. Do not read as an option-count effect.
- **Zheng et al.** (above): selection bias present at two to five options (Appendix B, Table 3); the study does not go beyond five.
- **Walshe et al.** (above): 77 labels with descriptions in a single prompt collapse a 7B model (macro F1 4.0).
- **Li et al. 2024, LongICLBench**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02060 (abstract and Table 1-2 of tasks). 15 long-context LLMs on in-context classification with 28 to 174 classes; models cope with fewer classes and shorter demonstrations and fail on Discovery (174 classes); bias toward labels late in the sequence. Labels are shown through demonstrations, not as a one-shot option list. Preprint. Match: not matching.
- **GLiClass** limitation (above) and **Laya** README (above) on large label sets, from the authors of the encoders and the clone.
- No controlled study found that varies the number of options from 2 to about 50 at fixed task difficulty for option-logit, pointer or encoder scorers. Gap. The lab's own sets (4, 8, 16) are small relative to the 77-label failures reported above.

## Q2. Near-neighbour options and "none of these"

- **Confusable labels**: "From Confusion to Clarity" (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01564. Abstract only. Three benchmarks (WOS, Flipkart, LEDGAR); top-K retrieval narrows candidates but does not help the model separate similar labels; adding known confusable partners and generated pairwise disambiguation rules raised macro F1 by up to 10.0 points over retrieval baselines, and rules transferred to smaller models (2B-20B, up to +11.5). Preprint. Match: not matching (generative LLM).
- **Wait, that's not an option** (Goral et al. 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00113; arXiv v3 Jun 2025, venue not checked; also summarised in [q5-abstention.md](q5-abstention.md)). Read (abstract, Table 1, Table 3, Figure 6). BAD dataset (arithmetic multiple-choice with no valid option), MMLU and MedMCQA variants; models: GPT-4o, o1-mini, Llama 3.1-8B/70B/405B, Qwen2/2.5 base and instruct, others. Conditions: Easy (the prompt alerts that neither option may be correct), Standard, Hard (told to pick one regardless). Share of reflective responses (declines or gives the right answer), Table 1: GPT-4o 0.9 / 0.0 / 0.0; GPT-4o-mini 37.0 / 58.0 / 14.0 (alert lower than standard); Llama 3.1-8B 0 / 0 / 0; Llama 3.1-70B 86.4 / 60.0 / 50.0; Llama 3.1-405B 100 / 42.5 / 91.5; Qwen2-Math-7B base 100 / 99 / 95.5 versus its RLHF version 53 / 16 / 16; Qwen2.5-7B base 49 / 40.9 / 33.6 versus Instruct 1.8 / 0 / 0. Post-trained aligned models often default to an invalid option; base models do better and improve with size; an explicit alert helps some models and not others (non-monotone); warning prompts did not lower the model's confidence in its (invalid) answer (section 3.4). Grade: preprint (peer review not verified), code released. Match: not matching (generation on arithmetic with two options).
- **LLMs may perform MCQA by selecting the least incorrect option**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01349. Abstract only. LLMs may regard multiple options as correct; performance degrades when "none of the above" substitutes for the correct answer (search summary reports drops up to about 70.9% from 74.44%, not verified in the text). Preprint.
- **UA-Bench** (2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17293. Abstract and introduction only. 18 frontier LLMs and over 3,500 questions; even strong models struggle to distinguish "the question is under-specified" (data uncertainty) from "beyond my capability" (model uncertainty), which matters for choosing between a clarify and a hand-off option. Preprint. Match: not matching.
- **Answer matching versus multiple choice** (Chandak et al. 2025): https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02856. Abstract only. Many multiple-choice items can be answered without seeing the question; shortcuts arise from discriminative evaluation; free-form generation plus a matching grader aligns better with humans. Relevance to the lab: it supports the lab's own options-only shortcut check. Preprint.
- **Option-level psychometrics** (2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02966. Abstract only. Across 189 LLMs and 31,554 items from 14 benchmarks, distractor identity adds information beyond correctness. Preprint. Relevance: near-neighbour distractors carry model-specific signal; not an authoring guideline.
- **GLiNER** negative-type training (above) is the encoder-side analogue of "none of these": 0% negatives gave precision 49.3 and recall 58.1; 50% gave 62.3 and 59.7; 75% gave 61.1 and 56.5 (Table 5).
- **Vendor docs** recommend an `other`/`none of the above` option when the list may not cover every input (typesafe choice page, above). No measured evidence.

## Coverage and gaps for Q1/Q2

- No source isolates terse versus full instruction wording for option-letter-logit, pointer-head or encoder readouts. The nearest controlled instruction-length result (PLOS ONE 2026) is generative and task-dependent.
- No source tests irrelevant-context or distractor effects on encoders, cross-encoders or pointer-head scorers. The lab's "23 new failures from adding state to J" has no published counterpart found.
- No evidence on decisiveness or abstention clauses in instructions for scorers; for chat LLMs see [q5-abstention.md](q5-abstention.md).
- No published ablation for GLiNER2 or GLiClass label descriptions; only author documentation that they are supported.
- No controlled study of number of options from 2 to 50 for logit-readout scorers; the MMLU-Pro option-count evidence is confounded.
- Prompt compression has no evidence for short classification instructions.
- Most of the sensitivity evidence is on 2022-2024 base and chat models up to 70B. The lab's decision models are fine-tuned for a fixed option interface, so the size of the letter-symbol and paraphrase effects for them is not known from these sources.
- Several 2026 preprints (arXiv IDs 2602 to 2609) had no peer review at the time of access; each is labelled preprint.
