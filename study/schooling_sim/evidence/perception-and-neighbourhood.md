# Evidence: perception and neighbourhood (searcher B, area 2)

- Areas: metric vs topological neighbourhoods and number of neighbours; limited field of view / blind angle; binary or discrete-choice flocking; visual-projection models; whether coarse perception still gives schooling and escape.
- Accessed: 2026-10-06.
- Read-level legend: **full text** = the PDF/HTML of the paper was opened and the named sections read; **partial** = abstract plus named sections only; **abstract only**; **fetch summary** = small-model summary of a web page; **search summary** = search-engine snippet only.
- Card IDs: PN-01 to PN-04. Cards cited from the existing set: C-04 (Vicsek), C-11 (random neighbour choice), C-12 (predator evasion and criticality).

## Summary: positive and negative/qualifying results

Positive (supports the p2 design):
- A small, fixed number of topological neighbours is empirically grounded and works in simulation. Starlings interact with nc = 6.5 ± 0.9 (SE) neighbours regardless of density; in the same paper's 2D predator-attack simulation, a topological model with **nc = 3** stays cohesive far more often than a metric one (stragglers 5% vs 43% of connected components) (PN-01). k = 3 in p2 sits in the range the authors cite as optimal in 2D (3–5) (PN-01, quoting another model; not verified by us).
- Binary heading choice is enough for collective motion. The active Ising model (spins ±1, left/right biased hopping, local majority-like alignment) has a genuine ordered flocking phase (PN-03). Coarse black-and-white visual input (where neighbours block the sky) plus alignment with 4 topological neighbours gives cohesive, polarised swarms (PN-04).
- Restricted view helps up to a point: in a Vicsek model with a vision sector, consensus is fastest at a view angle below 2π (3π/2 in the main example) (Tian et al. 2009, read in full, recorded in the search log; no separate card).

Negative / qualifying:
- In golden shiner fish, neither metric nor topological neighbourhoods best explain how behaviour spreads; visual (occlusion plus angular-size threshold) and Voronoi networks do better, and visual networks have lower transitivity at equal degree (PN-02). A "k nearest, regardless of occlusion" view is therefore a modelling choice, not the empirically best fit for fish.
- Ballerini et al. stress that the shape of the interaction, not only a cut-off, is topological (PN-01). p2 uses topology only to choose the 3 neighbours; distance to them is dropped. That matches the topological case.
- Binary-symmetry flocking differs from continuous-heading flocking. In the active Ising model the transition is a liquid–gas phase separation with travelling bands, and its critical point is in the Ising class (PN-03). Results from Vicsek-type models (C-04) do not carry over unchanged to binary or discrete headings.
- A narrow field of view can destroy order: one later Vicsek-type study reports a phase transition only for half-angle ≥ 0.5π (small N, 40–500; read but low weight, search log #9). Different studies disagree on which view angle is optimal and on the direction of the effect.
- Lead not carded (search summary only): Ginelli & Chaté 2010 (PRL 105, 168103) report that Voronoi (metric-free) neighbours change the collective properties qualitatively compared with metric Vicsek. Katz et al. 2011 (PNAS) and Herbert-Read et al. 2011 infer fish rules from data; they report that responses are not simple averaging over many neighbours (not verified).
- No source found that tests a **discretised egocentric sector view** (for example 8 sectors × 3 distance bands) against continuous perception in a predator–prey school. That is the main gap for p2.

## Cards

### PN-01 Ballerini et al. 2008: topological interaction (6–7 neighbours) and predator-attack cohesion
1. **Source.** Ballerini M., Cabibbo N., Candelier R., Cavagna A., Cisbani E., Giardina I., Lecomte V., Orlandi A., Parisi G., Procaccini A., Viale M., Zdravkovic V. (2008). "Interaction ruling animal collective behavior depends on topological rather than metric distance: Evidence from a field study." PNAS 105(4):1232–1237. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0711437105 ; arXiv:0709.1916 (https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.1916). Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text of the arXiv version (Results, Discussion, Fig. 4 caption with simulation parameters, Methods). The SI was not read.
2. **Local view and representation.** Empirical: 3D positions of starlings in 10 flocking events (flocks up to 2600 birds). A nearest-neighbour anisotropy measure decays with neighbour rank n and gives the interaction range. In units of birds this range, nc, is constant across densities (nc⁻¹ᐟ³ vs sparseness r1: R² = 0.00021, P = 0.97). The metric range rc scales with r1 (R² = 0.78, P = 0.00072). Mean **nc = 6.5 ± 0.9 SE**. The authors attribute the anisotropy (lack of neighbours along the direction of motion) to vision and suggest a subitizing limit of about 7. They cite 3–5 for 2D fish shoals. Simulation: 2D SPP (Vicsek-type) model. In the metric version birds align with all neighbours within rc = 0.15; in the topological version with **nc = 3** nearest neighbours. Continuous positions, no sectors, no blind zone.
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** Continuous heading: θi(t+1) = [θi(t) + Σj θj(t)]/(Ni+1), plus a predator term.
4. **Predator model and interaction.** A predator moves past the flock with vertical offset d = 0.9. It exerts a repulsive 1/r force on each bird's heading with strength F0 = 0.05 relative to alignment. A second set-up uses an obstacle: velocity is reassigned at random when a bird gets too close.
5. **Noise or error.** No explicit noise term is described for the simulation (none is given in the Fig. 4 equation). Error spread is not measured.
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** N = 200, T = 2000 steps, 5000 initial conditions (metric) and 2000 (topological). Metric: the number of connected components (CC; birds within 3rc) M after the attack. Metric flocks most often split into M = 5; topological flocks most often stay at M = 1. Stragglers make up 43% of CCs (metric) vs 5% (topological); in the obstacle set-up 24% vs 0.7%. The same qualitative result holds with 1/r or 1/n weighting (Fig. S6, not read).
7. **Evidence type.** Empirical field study plus simulation demonstration. No code or data link in the arXiv text.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Supports a topological k = 3 for a 2D predator set-up: the authors' own topological simulation used nc = 3. Copy the outcome metric: connected components and straggler fraction after a predator pass. This is a cohesion measure robust to the torus that p2 can report next to polarisation. Not tested: discrete perception, binary turns, decision errors.

### PN-02 Strandburg-Peshkin et al. 2013: visual networks vs metric, topological and Voronoi
1. **Source.** Strandburg-Peshkin A., Twomey C.R., Bode N.W.F., Kao A.B., Katz Y., Ioannou C.C., Rosenthal S.B., Torney C.J., Wu H.S., Levin S.A., Couzin I.D. (2013). "Visual sensory networks and effective information transfer in animal groups." Current Biology 23(17):R709–R711. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.07.059 ; PMC4780851. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text of the PMC author manuscript (main text and figure legends). Supplemental information not read.
2. **Local view and representation.** Four candidate neighbourhoods were compared: metric (all within radius), topological (k nearest), Voronoi (shared tessellation boundary) and visual (all individuals subtending an angular area on the focal fish's retina above a threshold, with occlusion from ray-casting the visual field).
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** Binary response event: each uninformed fish "responds" (accelerates toward the target) or not. P(s) = 1/(1+e^(−s)), where s depends on the **fraction of an individual's neighbours that have already responded**.
4. **Predator model and interaction.** None. Leadership events: trained "informed" fish move toward a stimulus.
5. **Noise or error.** Not modelled as error. The response is stochastic (logistic). Spread was measured as a wave of responses from the first responder (LMM p < 0.0001).
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** Groups of 70 golden shiners, 25 leadership events, tracked at 30 fps. Models were compared by marginal likelihood (mean of 10 runs, 10,000 parameter samples each), including nonsocial and constant-probability null models. The visual model fits best, Voronoi second; both beat metric and topological. At equal average degree, visual networks have similar network efficiency but **substantially lower transitivity** than metric and topological networks. The authors interpret this as less redundant information per neighbour.
7. **Evidence type.** Empirical (lab fish) plus model comparison. Data and code availability not stated in the read text.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Qualifying. For fish, "k nearest" is not the best-supported neighbourhood, and its neighbours overlap (high transitivity), which should raise correlated input among neighbours. In p2 that could make neighbours err together through shared input rather than through contagion. The fractional-response logistic model gives a ready formula to fit to p2's simulated spread data.

### PN-03 Solon & Tailleur 2013/2015: active Ising model, flocking with binary (left/right) headings
1. **Source.** Solon A.P., Tailleur J. (2013). "Revisiting the flocking transition using active spins." arXiv:1303.4427. Solon A.P., Tailleur J. (2015). "Flocking with discrete symmetry: the 2d Active Ising Model." arXiv:1506.05749. Journal versions (PRL 2013, PRE 2015) were not verified; cite the arXiv IDs (https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.05749). Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: 2015 paper, full text of the abstract, Introduction, Sec. II (model definition) and Sec. VI B (off-lattice version) heads. Other sections were scanned for parameters. The 2013 paper was downloaded but only its abstract was read.
2. **Local view and representation.** Lattice gas. Alignment is purely on-site: a particle sees only the magnetisation mi and density ρi of its own lattice site (a metric neighbourhood of zero range). No sectors, no field of view. An off-lattice version uses local density and magnetisation within a range.
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** **Binary**: spin s = ±1 (move right or left). The spin flips at rate W(s→−s) = exp(−s β mi/ρi), a soft local-majority rule with temperature T = 1/β. Hopping is biased: rate D(1+sε) in the spin direction and D(1−sε) against it, D for up and down. ε = 0.9 in the figures.
4. **Predator model and interaction.** None.
5. **Noise or error.** Thermal noise only (temperature β), independent across particles. Error spread is not measured.
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** The order parameter is the magnetisation. The phase diagram is in temperature–density and velocity–density planes. The flocking transition is a **liquid–gas phase separation**, with ordered bands moving through a disordered gas between two coexistence lines. The critical point at zero velocity is in the Ising universality class. Example system size 800 × 100. The off-lattice version reproduces the phenomenology (Sec. VI B).
7. **Evidence type.** Theory (coarse-grained hydrodynamics) plus simulation. Code not linked in the text read.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Positive: a binary left/right decision with a probabilistic local-majority rule produces collective motion. Difference: in p2 the binary choice is a *turn* (change of heading), not the heading itself, so p2 is closer to a discretised Vicsek model than to the AIM. Expect band-like or phase-separated states at the edge of order (as in AIM and Vicsek), and expect the order to depend on density. Report density and torus size. Not tested: predator, perception error, topological neighbours.

### PN-04 Pearce et al. 2014: coarse black-and-white visual projection model
1. **Source.** Pearce D.J.G., Miller A.M., Rowlands G., Turner M.S. (2014). "Role of projection in the control of bird flocks." PNAS 111(29):10422–10426. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1402202111 ; arXiv:1407.2414. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text of the arXiv version (Introduction, Hybrid Projection Model, opening of Results). The SI (3D, blind angles, steric interactions) was not read.
2. **Local view and representation.** Each agent registers only a **binary dark/light projection** around it: the angles θij of the boundaries between blocked (bird) and open (sky) sectors, with occlusion. It also sees the headings of its **σ = 4 nearest visible neighbours** (topological, with line of sight). The authors say "incomplete angular vision corresponding to blind angles" is explored in the SI (not read).
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** Continuous heading: v(t+1) ∝ φp δi + φa ⟨v̂k⟩ + φn ηi, with δi the mean unit vector to all boundaries and φp + φa + φn = 1.
4. **Predator model and interaction.** None in the read sections. The authors argue that marginal opacity lets individuals see predators, or the response of distant birds to them.
5. **Noise or error.** η is a unit random vector, independent across individuals and steps. Error spread is not measured.
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** The model gives "robustly cohesive swarms" and marginal opacity. Empirical starling flock opacity Θ0 is roughly 0.25–0.6. Several phenotypes (bird-, fish- and insect-like) arise depending on {φp, φa}. Exact N and replicate counts were not read.
7. **Evidence type.** Simulation plus empirical comparison (photographs of flocks). Code availability not stated in the read text.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Positive: a very coarse, low-dimensional perception (binary occupancy by angle) plus a few topological neighbours is enough for cohesion. This supports the idea that a sector-based discretised view can sustain schooling. Contrast: p2 has no global or long-range cue such as projection, so cohesion must come from 3 neighbours alone. PN-01's topological result suggests that is feasible, but p2 has no explicit attraction term (only turn left/right), so cohesion is not guaranteed and should be checked first in the rule-only baseline.
