# Evidence: learned and language-based agents in swarms (cards LA-xx)

- Searcher areas: (3) learned and language-based agents in swarms (LLM-driven Boids/flocking, LLM swarm robotics, MARL/evolved predator avoidance, discretised observations).
- Access date: 2026-10-06.
- Read-level legend: **full text** = PDF text read via pdftotext (sections named); **abstract only** = abstract/landing page; **fetch summary** = WebFetch summary by a small model; **search summary** = web-search snippet only.
- All "Relevance to p2" fields are inference by the searcher, not claims of the sources.

## Summary: positive and negative/qualifying results

Positive (feasibility):
- An LLM (GPT-4o) can replace the heading rule of a subset of NetLogo Boids and give flocking that the authors call "comparable" to the rule-only model, but only after five rounds of prompt repair (LA-02). Five LLM birds in a flock of 30 is a hybrid, not an LLM-only school.
- Evolved agents with a coarse, egocentric, sector-based retina (12 sectors of 15 degrees, presence bits only) and a discrete left/right/forward/stay action evolve swarming under predation, provided the predator suffers confusion (LA-03). This is the closest published analogue of the p2 view and decision format, and it is pre-LLM (2013).
- RL agents with an egocentric view of the predator and n nearest neighbours (distance, bearing, orientation) and five discrete turn options form swarms (LA-04).

Negative / qualifying:
- With raw coordinates in the prompt, GPT-3.5-Turbo agents did not flock: about 60% of five-agent runs collapsed to one point; a single agent asked to keep 10 units from a fixed agent moved away, and its stated reasoning had the other agent on the wrong side (sign of an x-coordinate ignored) (LA-01).
- LLM heading outputs "often pointed in opposite directions" from the rule-based heading until the compass convention and "shortest rotation" were spelled out; counterclockwise small turns stayed error-prone (LA-02). This is a direction (sign) error that is fixed to input type, the same class as the lab's known input-fixed errors.
- The RL-learned school was beaten on survival by a trivial "turn away from the predator" rule, both per agent and for the whole group (LA-04). Schooling is not automatically the survival-optimal policy; in LA-03 it evolved only when the predator was confused by nearby prey.
- No source found reports agent-level agreement with the rule for each decision, the spatial or temporal correlation of agent errors, or an injected-random-error baseline. LA-02 compares at the collective level only; LA-01 has no rule baseline at all.
- Not carded (demonstrations without comparison or too small): LLM2Swarm (Strobel, Dorigo, Fritz 2024, arXiv 2410.11387: "showcases" only, no quantitative comparison); Rahman, Schranz, Hayat 2025/2026 "LLM-Powered Swarms: A New Frontier or a Conceptual Stretch?" (arXiv 2506.14496, full text read): classical vs LLM Boids, 3 boids x 5 iterations x 30 trials; classic Boids higher in cohesion and separation (values only in a figure); LLM run 1054.41 s vs 3.53 s total. It is a comparison, but too small to inform collective outcomes.

### LA-01 LLM agents fail at decentralised flocking (Li et al. 2024)
1. **Source.** Peihan Li, Vishnu Menon, Bhavanaraj Gudiguntla, Daniel Ting, Lifeng Zhou, 2024, "Challenges Faced by Large Language Models in Solving Multi-Agent Flocking", DARS 2024; arXiv:2404.04752v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04752. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text (Sections 1, 3.2, 4, 5).
2. **Local view and representation.** Continuous, global and allocentric: each agent's prompt lists its own (x, y) and the positions of all other agents as a list of coordinate pairs; no sectors, no field of view.
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** Each agent has its own LLM call and outputs free text "Reasoning: ..., Position: [x, y]" (a continuous 2D target position), with a max velocity stated but not enforced in the prompt.
4. **Predator model and interaction.** None.
5. **Noise or error.** Errors come from the model. The paper reports format failures (GPT-4-Turbo about 80% failure to follow the output format; GPT-3.5-Turbo about 5%) and reasoning errors, shown by example: the active agent at x = -16.96 "thinks the stationary agent is to the left" of it (it is at x = 17.04) and moves away, then oscillates. Errors are not quantified per decision; correlation between agents is not measured.
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** Metric: MAE of nearest-neighbour distance from the desired distance. N = 5 (circle, alpha-lattice, V-shape), 3, 2, and 1 active + 1 stationary; 10 tests per configuration; about 15-25 rounds (from figures). "Nearly 60% of the tests fail and gather to the same point" for five agents. No rule-based (Olfati-Saber / Boids) baseline was run; the paper describes Olfati-Saber's law but does not simulate it.
7. **Evidence type.** Simulation (negative demonstration). Code: https://github.com/Zhourobotics/llms-for-flocking-pub (stated, not checked).
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Supports the p2 choice to hand the model a simulator-computed egocentric, discretised view instead of raw coordinates: the documented failure is exactly frame/sign handling of coordinates. It also shows why a rule baseline is needed: the paper cannot say how far the LLM is from a working rule. It does not test a binary choice, so it does not tell how a model performs once the geometry is precomputed.

### LA-02 LLM-steered Boids inside NetLogo, with a rule-based reference (Jimenez-Romero et al. 2025)
1. **Source.** Cristian Jimenez-Romero, Alper Yegenoglu, Christian Blum, 2025, "Multi-Agent Systems Powered by Large Language Models: Applications in Swarm Intelligence", arXiv:2503.03800v1 (also on PMC, PMC12135685, journal not checked). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03800. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text (abstract, toolchain, flocking prompt iterations 1-5, flocking results Sec. 5.2); ant-foraging part skimmed.
2. **Local view and representation.** Continuous, metric: current heading in degrees plus a list of neighbours within the vision radius, each as relative x, y and heading in degrees (example: "neighbor_1: x: 0.53, y: -3.69, heading: 248 deg"). Compass convention (0 = north, 90 = east) had to be stated explicitly.
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** GPT-4o returns JSON with a "rationale" and a "new-heading" (continuous angle), bounded by NetLogo max-turn parameters (separate 1.5, align 5, cohere 3 degrees).
4. **Predator model and interaction.** None.
5. **Noise or error.** Model errors are described per prompt iteration, qualitatively: iteration 2 headings "often pointed in opposite directions" from a rule-based model's; iteration 4 "some agents still moved in the opposite direction when making small adjustments, particularly for counterclockwise turns"; fixed by adding "always choose the shortest path (clockwise or counterclockwise)". Errors without a written rationale were worse. No error rate per decision is given; spread between agents is not measured.
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** Baseline = original rule-based NetLogo Flocking. Hybrid = 25 rule birds + 5 LLM birds. N = 30, 800 steps, 5 seeds each. Metric: heading differences and distances between birds over time (figures; no numeric table read). Reported: rule-only model shows "somewhat lower heading differences"; LLM birds sit at the flock periphery; collisions also analysed. No all-LLM flock was evaluated quantitatively for flocking.
7. **Evidence type.** Simulation with a rule baseline (collective level only). Code and data: https://github.com/crjimene/swarm_gpt (stated).
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** The closest LLM-Boids study with a rule comparison, and its main documented error is a turn-direction (sign) error tied to a class of inputs (small counterclockwise turns), i.e. a systematic, input-fixed error like the lab's. p2 can copy the hybrid design (k model agents among rule agents) as an extra condition and should measure where model agents end up in the school (periphery effect). p2 goes further by scoring each decision against the rule; LA-02 never did, so p2 would be new on that point.

### LA-03 Evolved Markov-network prey with a 12-sector retina evolve swarming under predator confusion (Olson et al. 2013)

> **Cross-reference.** Same source as [SP-01](schooling-and-predation.md#sp-01-predator-confusion-is-sufficient-to-evolve-swarming-olson-et-al-2013), the primary card; counted once in the catalogue. Kept here for its learned-agent reading.

1. **Source.** Randal S. Olson, Arend Hintze, Fred C. Dyer, David B. Knoester, Christoph Adami, 2013, "Predator confusion is sufficient to evolve swarming behaviour", Journal of the Royal Society Interface 10: 20130305; arXiv:1209.3330v3. https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3330. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text (Methods 2.1-2.4, Results 3.1); later results sections skimmed.
2. **Local view and representation.** Discrete, egocentric, sector-based. Frontal 180 degree retina of 12 sectors of 15 degrees each; prey have two layers (conspecifics, predator), range 100 virtual m (predator 200 m). Each sector reports presence only, not count or distance. Rear 180 degrees is a blind zone.
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** Four discrete actions: stay, forward, turn left 8 degrees + forward, turn right 8 degrees + forward. Controller: an evolved Markov network (with memory).
4. **Predator model and interaction.** One coevolving predator, 3x faster, turns 6 degrees per step, one attack per 10 steps. Confusion: P(capture) = 1/ANV, where ANV is the number of prey visible to the predator within 30 m of the target.
5. **Noise or error.** Not modelled as error. Important design: all 50 prey in a trial are clones of one genome ("50 identical prey agents from the single prey genome"), chosen to remove individual-level selection. Agent-level correctness is undefined (no reference rule).
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** 50 prey, 1 predator, 1,200 generations, 180 replicates per experiment. With confusion, cohesive swarms evolved in 70% of replicates; without confusion, none (prey dispersed). Metrics: swarm density and swarm dispersion along the line of descent (numbers in figures, not read).
7. **Evidence type.** Simulation (evolutionary). Code https://github.com/adamilab/eos and data https://github.com/adamilab/eos-data (stated).
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Strong precedent for p2's view (angular sectors, presence-type input, a predator layer and a neighbour layer) and for a near-binary left/right turn, so p2's discretisation is not exotic. It also gives a clear test: whether schooling pays depends on the predator model (confusion). A p2 predator without confusion may make dispersal, not schooling, the better collective outcome, and capture rate would then penalise a school. Clonal prey are the evolutionary analogue of "copies of one decision model": identical policies, so input-fixed errors would be shared. Leaves untested: errors relative to a reference rule.

### LA-04 RL-learned escape flocking loses to a "turn away" rule (Hahn et al. 2019, SELFish)
1. **Source.** Carsten Hahn, Thomy Phan, Thomas Gabor, Lenz Belzner, Claudia Linnhoff-Popien, 2019, "Emergent Escape-based Flocking Behavior using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning", ALIFE 2019 (venue from the arXiv metadata, not checked); arXiv:1905.04077. https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04077. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text (Approach, Simulations and Results, Agent Survival).
2. **Local view and representation.** Continuous, egocentric, topological: for the predator, itself and the n nearest neighbours, a 3-tuple (distance, angle the agent would have to turn to face the entity in (-180, 180], absolute orientation), normalised to [0, 1]. n was tuned (best value in Table 1, not read). Torus 40 x 40.
3. **Per-agent decision and dimensionality.** Turn angle only, constant speed. DQN: 5 discrete turns {-90, -45, 0, +45, +90}; DDPG: continuous turn.
4. **Predator model and interaction.** One scripted predator, same base speed with occasional bursts; chases the nearest agent and, when several agents are equally near, picks a target at random (a distraction/confusion effect); turns limited to +-45 degrees per step.
5. **Noise or error.** No error model. Training: one learning agent; its network is copied to all other agents after each episode (all agents share one policy).
6. **Collective metrics, baselines, N, horizon, replicates, effect sizes.** Baselines: Boids with an added predator-avoidance force (weights tuned for survival) and "TurnAway" (turn directly away from the predator). Training with 10 agents; evaluation with varied N (e.g. 40). Metrics: DBSCAN cluster sizes, mean deviation from cluster mean heading, mean pairwise distance, episode length, caught agents per time. Reported: Boids gives the most aligned groups; "the best strategy to survive is to simply turn away from the predator", for each agent and for the whole swarm. The authors suggest RL got stuck in a stay-with-the-swarm equilibrium. Numbers are in figures only.
7. **Evidence type.** Simulation with rule baselines. Video link given; code not stated.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Directly relevant to the p2 reference rule ("turn away from the predator; else follow neighbours"): a pure turn-away rule can beat flocking on capture rate when the predator is not confused by groups, so a capture-rate comparison against the rule may reward dispersal. p2 should report polarisation and cohesion next to capture rate, and should include a TurnAway-only baseline to separate "schooling helps" from "fleeing helps". Shared-policy copies again mirror the lab's model copies.

## Also noted (not carded)
- PettingZoo/MPE2 simple_tag (fetch summary of https://mpe2.farama.org/environments/simple_tag/): continuous observations [self_vel, self_pos, landmark_rel_positions, other_agent_rel_positions, other_agent_velocities], 5 discrete moves (none, left, right, down, up in world frame, i.e. allocentric), default 1 good agent and 3 adversaries, 25 cycles. Not discretised and not egocentric; it is a pursuit benchmark, not a schooling model.
- Ward, Gobet, Kendall 2001, "Evolving collective behavior in an artificial ecology", Artificial Life 7(2):191-209 (search summary only): neural-network prey and predators coevolve schooling without a schooling fitness term. Pre-2010 precursor of LA-03; not read.
- Authors not verified, 2025, "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models", arXiv:2506.07962 (search summary only): errors are more correlated for same-developer / same-architecture models. Relevant to the lab fact about fixed errors, but it is not a swarm paper; it belongs with S-11 if anyone carries it forward.
