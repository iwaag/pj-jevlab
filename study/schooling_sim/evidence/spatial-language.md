## Summary: positive and negative/qualifying results

Positive:
- Single-step relation questions in a fixed (allocentric, map-like) frame are easy for strong models: GPT-4 scored 100% on 1-hop StepGame questions (n = 20) (SL-01). Mapping text to a relation is reliable; multi-step composition is the weak point.
- When the geometry is precomputed and the model only has to name or use a relation, errors drop sharply (StepGame: GPT-3 parsing + logic solver, 2 errors in 1000; SL-01). This supports p2's design, where the simulator computes sector and band and the model only chooses.
- Small models can be fixed cheaply: LoRA fine-tuning of Llama-3.1-8B with reasoning traces gave large gains on egocentric navigation, and a fully fine-tuned FlanT5 (<1B) matched some frontier models (SL-03).

Negative / qualifying (most relevant to p2):
- Egocentric frames are harder than fixed frames, and the failures are systematic, not random: models drift back to the default "+Y is forward" frame mid-reasoning (SL-03); Llama3-70B "systematically reverses left and right" when converting perspective (SL-02); many models flip front/back when the reference is not the viewer (SL-03). These are input-fixed errors, consistent with the lab fact.
- Accuracy depends on the text format of the same space by a factor of 2-5 (adjacency list vs visual character grid, SL-04); presenting a global map can be harder than local step-by-step text (Yamada et al., noted below).
- Accuracy falls with the number of reasoning hops and with distracting entities (SL-01). For p2 each question is one hop, but a list of k = 3 neighbours plus predator is a small multi-entity context; the "majority of neighbours' previous moves" step is a small aggregation that these benchmarks do not test.
- Benchmarks themselves had label errors (14 of 214 StepGame templates wrong, SL-01). The p2 reference rule should be checked the same way before model errors are counted.
- Very small models: Llama2-7B and 13B scored zero or near zero on zero-shot map navigation (Yamada et al.); 7-8B models score low on egocentric localisation (SL-03). No source tested a classifier-style model answering one binary left/right question from a structured state; that is the p2 setting and it is untested in this literature.
- Pre-2010 background: Levinson (2003) frame-of-reference classes (relative, intrinsic, absolute) and Hill (1982) on front/back reversal in English, both cited in SL-02; not read directly.

### SL-01 StepGame benchmark and its LLM re-evaluation (Shi et al. 2022; Li, Hogg, Cohn 2024)
1. **Source.** (a) Zhengxiang Shi, Qiang Zhang, Aldo Lipani, 2022, "StepGame: A New Benchmark for Robust Multi-Hop Spatial Reasoning in Texts", AAAI 2022; arXiv:2204.08292. https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08292. (b) Fangjun Li, David C. Hogg, Anthony G. Cohn, 2024, "Advancing Spatial Reasoning in Large Language Models: An In-Depth Evaluation and Enhancement Using the StepGame Benchmark", AAAI 2024 (copyright line in the PDF); arXiv:2401.03991. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03991. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: (a) full text, dataset construction and noise sections; results tables skimmed. (b) full text (template errors, Table 4, discussion).
2. **How space is encoded in text.** Natural-language story of k pairwise relations among k+1 entities on an implied grid, 8 directions (top, down, left, right and four diagonals) plus overlap, from crowd-sourced templates (e.g. "If E is the center of a clock face, H is located between 2 and 3"). Fixed, allocentric (map) frame. Test sets add distracting noise: disconnected, irrelevant and supporting chains.
3. **Question format.** "What is the relation of A to B?", one of 9 labels; k = 1 to 10 hops.
4. **Predator.** None.
5. **Error types reported.** (a) Models trained on clean data lose accuracy with noise and with more hops. (b) 14 of 214 templates in the benchmark were wrong (only "above" and "left" mappings were error-free); one parsing error example maps "between 2 and 3 on a clock face" to "right" instead of "upper-right". Model errors grow with k. Only 2 errors in 1000 when GPT-3 (Davinci) parses and an ASP logic program reasons.
6. **Metrics and numbers (as read).** (b) Table 4 accuracy, base prompting: GPT-4 100 at k = 1 falling to 25 at k = 10 (20 test items per k); gpt-3.5-turbo 62 at k = 1, 20 at k = 10 (100 items per k); Davinci 77 at k = 1, 22 at k = 10. CoT / tree-of-thought prompting raised GPT-4 to 60-100 for k = 2-10. (a) Leakage: 23.2% of bAbI task 17 test samples appear in training; StepGame designed for about 0% leakage beyond k = 1.
7. **Evidence type.** Benchmark. Data and code public (StepGame; corrected templates at the repo cited in (b), not checked).
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** p2 asks a 1-hop question with the geometry already computed, which is the easy end of this curve; the evidence predicts low error from geometry and puts the risk in rule composition (predator case vs neighbour-majority case). The template-error finding is a direct warning: audit the p2 reference rule and its labels (e.g. what "ahead" and "behind" mean at sector boundaries) before scoring model errors.

### SL-02 FoREST: frame-of-reference comprehension; systematic left/right reversal (Premsri & Kordjamshidi 2025)
1. **Source.** Tanawan Premsri, Parisa Kordjamshidi, 2025, "FoREST: Frame of Reference Evaluation in Spatial Reasoning Tasks", arXiv:2502.17775v3. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17775. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text (Sections 1-3, 5 results RQ1-RQ3); tables partly garbled in text extraction, so only numbers quoted in the prose are used.
2. **How space is encoded in text.** Short natural-language scene sentences with a locatum and relatum ("A cat is to the left of the car"), with or without explicit perspective ("from the car itself", "from my perspective") and topology (inside/outside). Four FoR classes after Tenbrink (2011): external/internal x intrinsic/relative.
3. **Question format.** QA about the locatum's relation to the relatum, possibly from a different perspective than the context; also layout generation for text-to-image.
4. **Predator.** None.
5. **Error types reported.** In ambiguous cases models show a preference (bias) for one frame (e.g. Qwen2 assumes the context and question share a perspective; GPT-4o shifts to intrinsic with CoT). In perspective conversion, "pure-text LLM, Llama3-70B, systematically reverses left and right", unlike humans who in English reverse front and back (Hill 1982). Models also tend to assume a fixed facing direction and to ignore orientation tokens (attention analysis).
6. **Metrics and numbers (as read).** Models: Llama3-70B, Qwen2-72B, Qwen2VL-72B, GPT-4o at temperature 0. Converting from camera to relatum perspective: best only 55.5% (Llama3-70B, CoT); GPT-4o up to 82.4% for relatum-to-camera but 53% for camera-to-relatum (CoT). Human baseline collected (numbers not read).
7. **Evidence type.** Benchmark. Data release not checked.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** The strongest documented case that left/right errors in text models can be systematic reversals tied to a frame conversion, not noise. p2 removes most of the conversion because the simulator gives egocentric sectors. The remaining risk is in how sectors are named: if a sector is labelled with words that need a frame ("left-behind", "north-east") the model may apply a default frame. Inference: use labels that need no conversion and test both mirror images of each view to detect a left/right bias.

### SL-03 GSU: text-only grid tasks; egocentric frames and small models (Sidhu & Hockenmaier 2026)
1. **Source.** Risham Sidhu, Julia Hockenmaier, 2026, "Grid Spatial Understanding: A Dataset for Textual Spatial Reasoning over Grids, Embodied Settings, and Coordinate Structures", arXiv:2603.17333v1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17333. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text (Sections 2, 4, 4.1, 4.3, 4.4, Appendix A direction distribution); Table 1 numbers garbled in extraction and not quoted.
2. **How space is encoded in text.** Integer grid coordinates in text, with a viewer location and heading (axis-aligned). Two frames: Cardinal (fixed axes) and Egocentric (directions rotate with the agent's heading). Also Card2Ego (translate N/S/E/W to left/right/forward/backward).
3. **Question format.** Navigation (follow egocentric instructions to a final coordinate, or generate instructions); object localisation ("Where is the red block relative to you?" answer such as "to my front left"; allocentric variant relative to a reference object); structure composition from coordinate lists.
4. **Predator.** None.
5. **Error types reported.** Egocentric harder than Cardinal. Models lose the axes ("we are facing -Y with +Y to our right"), or compute the new heading then "forget" to use it; deductions fall back to the default +Y heading (e.g. facing -Y: "-X is to my right" correct, then "x values smaller than me are to my left" wrong). Front/back flipping is the main allocentric error. Smaller models also name relations on axes where the two points do not differ.
6. **Metrics and numbers (as read).** Models: Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, VLM counterparts, GPT-OSS-20B, Olmo-32B, Qwen-32B, Llama-70B, Qwen-72B, GPT-4o, Gemini-3-Pro. "In the Egocentric case ... smaller models receive scores below 50" on object localisation (term-overlap score). Gemini-3-Pro solved several tasks fully. LoRA on Llama-8B with synthetic reasoning traces more than doubled Navigation Follower and increased Egocentric Instructor about tenfold; fully fine-tuned FlanT5 (<1B) matched some frontier models. No vision benefit for small VLMs.
7. **Evidence type.** Benchmark. Dataset release not checked.
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** Supports two p2 choices: precompute egocentric relations in the simulator (the frame update is where models fail), and use a small trained model rather than a prompted one (fine-tuned small models close the gap). The "fall back to the default frame" error predicts errors fixed to particular headings or sectors; p2's per-view error table should be read for such patterns (e.g. errors concentrated in rear sectors).

### SL-04 Same maze, different text format: 2-5x accuracy change (Zhu et al. 2026)
1. **Source.** Yilin Zhu, Weijiang Li, Parijat Dube, Rajarshi Das (author list as read on page 1; possibly incomplete), 2026, "Do LLMs Build Spatial World Models? Evidence from Grid-World Maze Tasks", ICLR 2026; arXiv:2604.10690. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10690. Accessed 2026-10-06. Read level: full text of abstract and introduction only; methods and results sections not read.
2. **How space is encoded in text.** Two encodings of the same maze: tokenized adjacency lists with special tokens (<ADJLIST START>, <ORIGIN START>) vs a visual character grid. Grids 5x5 to 9x9.
3. **Question format.** Maze solving (path), plus sequential proximity questions and distance comparisons.
4. **Predator.** None.
5. **Error types reported.** Representation dependence; without CoT most models fail; models treat sequential spatial questions as independent and do not reuse earlier computation even when their reasoning covers 96-99% of the needed content.
6. **Metrics and numbers (as read).** Gemini-2.5-Flash with CoT: 80-86% on 5x5 to 7x7 with adjacency lists vs 16-34% with visual grids; 86% vs 34% on 5x5. Claude-Haiku-4.5 nearly 0 on 5x5 tokenized mazes with base prompting, 78% with CoT. Models: Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-5-mini, Claude-Haiku-4.5, DeepSeek-Chat.
7. **Evidence type.** Benchmark. A "SpatialBench" HuggingFace Space is mentioned (not checked).
8. **Relevance to p2 (inference).** The encoding of the p2 state is itself a factor: the same view in a different text layout can change accuracy several-fold. p2 should freeze one structured encoding, and could add a small check (same views, two encodings) to make sure errors belong to the view, not to the format. It also suggests that errors are a property of input format x content, consistent with input-fixed errors.

## Also noted (not carded)
- Yamada, Bao, Lampinen, Kasai, Yildirim 2024, "Evaluating Spatial Understanding of Large Language Models", TMLR; arXiv:2310.14540 (full text, Sections 2-3 and error-analysis opening). Local step-by-step navigation text over square, triangle, hexagon, ring, tree maps. GPT-4 above chance (1/8) on square, ring, triangle but not hexagon; GPT-3.5-turbo below chance on all; Llama2-7B and 13B near zero accuracy; giving the global map up front was harder than local narration. Errors analysed as spatial-topology bias vs temporal (prompt-order) bias. Temperature 1.0, 5 runs.
- SpartQA (Mirzaee et al. 2021) and bAbI tasks 17/19 (Weston et al. 2016): cited in SL-01 as precursors; bAbI criticised for leakage and for not needing multi-hop reasoning. Not read directly.
- REM, SpatialBench (2511.21471), LRR-Bench, SpinBench, "Egocentric bias in VLMs" (2602.15892): multimodal (image) benchmarks; search summaries report egocentric bias and below-chance left/right scores. Out of scope for a text-only decision model; not read.
- "Learning to Draw ASCII Improves Spatial Reasoning in Language Models" (arXiv 2604.14641, search summary only): training on layout construction improves text-only spatial reasoning. Not read.
