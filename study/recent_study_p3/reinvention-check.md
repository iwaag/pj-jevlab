## New to `study/`, no matching lab experiment

- Output-interface retention: 2610.16191 Choice2Seq (abstract-level): correct selection does not carry to sequential construction (Jev 97.1% → 33.0% retained).
- Privacy leakage through the probability fields: 2610.23668 (abstract-level).
- Reasoning on demand inside a decision model: 2610.19644, 2610.17309, 2610.15501, 2610.20951 (all abstract-level; the authors of the first two build and rank their own models).
- Letter-logit readout against hosted Jev: 2610.17205 independent measure: after temperature scaling 79.6 vs 80.1, short LoRA 84.4 — the first outside number for the lab's class-D baseline idea (raw readout 72.9; calibration matters).

## Should the lab stop or change anything?

- **Stop** designing new missing-evidence demonstrations; cite 2610.18529, 2610.14631, 2610.21412.
- **Do not claim as new:** order sensitivity on decision models; option-set dependence; fallback unreliability; label-channel attack on label-rendering models.
- **Change:** fix one fallback wording before measuring thresholds (2610.19856); add a re-ask/duplicate-question floor to every flip count (2610.22066); report coverage at an accuracy target, not only accuracy (2610.20778, 2610.21412).
- **Still open for the lab:** key-naming-in-state on K27/W12/A27; mixed panels on shared hard states; Japanese; the W2 vs W3 split.

## Contradictions

None outright. Two tensions: 2610.19536 finds Jev/Clef/Kev-27B robust to renaming, while 2610.12292 finds label-rendering models catastrophically exposed — consistent once the rendering and the model class are separated. 2610.17820 finds hosted Jev the most robust system on input moderation yet bypassed 19–29% on phishing forgeries, so "robust" is task-specific.
