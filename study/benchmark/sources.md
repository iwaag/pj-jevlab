# Sources

Index of every source used in `study/benchmark/`, compiled 2026-10-02 in step2 of [benchmark/p1](../../devdocs/episodes/benchmark/p1/plan.md). Each ID links to its entry in [evidence/](evidence/), which records:
- title, authors or organisation, date, URL/DOI and source type;
- **how far it was read** and the specific claims it supports, with section, equation or table numbers;
- caveats and disagreements.

How the sources were found is in [search-log.md](search-log.md).

## How to read the "Read to" column
| Level | Meaning | What may be cited |
|---|---|---|
| **full text** | The relevant sections were read in raw text: PDF via `pdftotext`, arXiv HTML, PMC HTML, official docs, or code | The claims listed in the evidence entry, at the section given |
| **partial** | Only some sections were read in full | Only those sections; numbers elsewhere are abstract-level |
| **abstract** | Only the abstract or landing page was read | Only what the abstract states. **An abstract is not evidence for a paper's methods or detailed results** |
| **unverified** | Bibliographic record only; full text not accessible | Nothing directly. Any formula is attributed through a secondary source that *was* read, and the evidence entry names it |

## Source types
- **Textbooks/tutorials and method papers** are the main methodological evidence.
- **Empirical studies** are evidence for what they measured, in their setting.
- **Benchmark/dataset papers** are primary documentation of their own artefact. Their performance claims about systems are **benchmark-author claims**.
- **Official docs and code** document a tool's own conventions, which are not universal.
- **Vendor claims** (a developer reporting on its own model or product) are marked in the evidence entries and in the text where they are used.

Several sources appear in more than one cluster, read for different purposes. Examples: SQuAD 2.0, CLINC150, BEIR, WILDS, CheckList, HELM, Datasheets. Each entry states what was read for that cluster.
