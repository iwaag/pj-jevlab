# Missing information, abstention, rejection and "I don't know" in Jev-style decision models

Theme summary. Seven recent papers (25 Sep to 6 Oct 2026) test what typed decision models do when the decisive fact is absent, when no listed option is right, or when the input is underspecified. The common finding matches the lab's unsolved failure: the argmax and its top probability do not signal absence (Jev gives 0.5 to 0.8 to a salient guess with no information; it rejects 7% of answer-absent menus), but a separately asked, targeted question ("is the evidence sufficient?", "is each candidate correct?") or a score threshold on an existing "none" option recovers much of the signal, and the thresholds do not transfer across tasks or candidate sets. Three of the ideas below (a sufficiency yes/no that does not name an answer, a threshold on the fallback-option probability instead of argmax, and counterfactual value-of-information probing of the missing slot) are not among what set4 and set5 tried.

All papers were read from the pdftotext files (full text), accessed 2026-10-08. Numbers are from the body unless marked "abstract only" (nothing below is abstract-only, except where noted).

## 2610.01006 — Beyond Answer Confidence: A Controlled Audit of Self-Knowledge in a Black-Box Decision Model
- Authors: Sharath M Shankaranarayana, Davor Runje, Jan Jannink (Synthpop.AI). Submitted 1 Oct 2026 (v1). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01006. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts: hosted Jev (jev-latest, every response reported jev-1.13.0), Choice probabilities plus Noul (yes/no) follow-up questions in the same call. Small side comparisons: open GLiNER2.5-Decide (encoder) and a 5-member MiniLM deep ensemble. No open LLM was run through the full protocol (author-stated).
- Design: about 575,000 paid calls, 15 public datasets and 6 generated families; every cell queried 3 times and averaged (replicate SD about 0.04 near p=0.5, probabilities quantised to 0.01). Paired interventions hold the item fixed and vary supplied information: Banking77/CLINC150 with opaque intent codes plus 0 to 8 examples, names, irrelevant text, swapped examples; 480 generated knowledge-by-chance scenarios with known ideal distributions; HotpotQA with 0/1/2 supporting paragraphs; 600 generated policy cases with a fact edited, deleted, contradicted or irrelevant; PopQA by popularity, 1,600 fabricated-entity questions; Daily Oracle yes/no news (3,360 balanced questions; 6,320 total) Jan 2020 to Jul 2026. English. Item-clustered bootstrap 95% intervals. Main plan frozen in a git tag (intent study); later studies "planned, plan committed afterwards" (not externally verifiable); the surface-cue controls (look-alike names, date removal) and recalibration bounds are exploratory, added after the main studies (Table 13, Sec. 4).
- Key results:
  - Confidence is calibrated on familiar closed-choice tasks (SmoothECE 0.015 to 0.033 on CLINC150, PopQA, TriviaQA, MMLU-Redux) and supports abstention (p_max>=0.9 keeps 8,132 of 9,960 TriviaQA questions with 20 errors) (Sec. 4.1).
  - No information (Table 3): opaque codes give p_max 0.32 (Banking77) and 0.36 (CLINC150) at chance accuracy, a third of the mass on the first numbered code; fixed-but-unknown fact 0.49 (ideal 0.25); fabricated entities 0.52; made-up future event winner 0.76; fair die 0.80 on "one" in all 720 option orders. GLiNER2.5-Decide on the same opaque-code inputs gives p_max 0.017/0.010, essentially uniform (Sec. 4.1, App. C).
  - Misleading context: related non-supporting paragraphs drop HotpotQA accuracy 0.84 to 0.75 while confidence stays 0.83; P(enough)=0.13 for those, 0.86 with both supporting paragraphs (Sec. 4.2).
  - "enough" question: separates complete from incomplete evidence, AUROC 0.95 (wording range 0.95 to 0.97 over 14 rewordings) versus 0.85 for confidence on the same items (+0.10 [0.09, 0.11]); text length alone 0.65 and within 10-word length strata enough keeps 0.95 while length falls to 0.48; adding enough to length features lifts a gradient-boosted model from 0.60 to 0.94 (Table 1, Sec. 4.2). On policy cases, deleting the deciding fact drops P(enough) from 0.83 to 0.10 and a "cannot tell" option gets 0.91.
  - "settled" question (is the answer already fixed): AUROC 1.00 (0.99 to 1.00) separating fixed-but-unknown from future chance events, mean P(yes) 0.87 vs 0.13; answer uncertainty alone reaches 0.96 only because Jev concentrates on the mode of chance events.
  - "known" question ("do you know the answer for certain?"): 0.88 to 0.91 on the original contrasts, but a char n-gram text classifier gets 1.00 (pseudo-word names) and 0.997 (date alone). With look-alike names known = 0.74 vs answer uncertainty 0.76 (paired diff -0.02 [-0.05, 0.01]); with dates removed 0.58 vs 0.56 (+0.02 [-0.01, 0.06]). Conclusion: no demonstrated memory-gap detection beyond confidence (Table 1, Fig. 3, Table 8).
  - Knowledge boundary: after an accuracy change point (about Nov 2024) Jev answers "no" to 92% of news questions (true share 51 to 52%), confidence rises 0.75 to 0.82; accuracy 0.511 at confidence 0.816 (gap +0.305). Recalibrating on earlier months leaves +0.19 (expanding window) to +0.23 (frozen map) overconfidence; no monotone map gets below 0.047 period-conditional error (Sec. 4.1, App. B.4).
  - Explicit "not known" option: chosen for 93% of post-boundary questions (coverage 6.6%) and 35.7% of earlier ones; confident errors 6 of 1,191 answers vs 330. Matched-coverage accuracy vs a confidence threshold: -0.034 at 5% post-boundary share, +0.010 [-0.002, 0.043] at the panel's 27%, +0.051 at 50% (App. C, Table 9). A rule skipping questions with the latest dates comes within 0.01 to 0.02.
  - Out-of-scope: explicit OOS option flags 89% of off-topic CLINC150 requests at 3.5% false alarms (AUROC 0.982) but only 36% of held-out Banking77 intents that have close relatives in the schema (App. C).
  - Unanswerable/ambiguous: P(answerable) separates SelfAware unanswerable questions AUROC 0.882; P(single answer) flags AmbigQA ambiguity only 0.661.
  - Stating "paragraphs may be irrelevant" restores HotpotQA accuracy 0.75 to 0.84 (SmoothECE 0.02).
- Method / recipe and how it is implemented (for replication):
  - Same call, extra Noul questions. Request: `state` = JSON of facts + question; `questions` = {"answer": choice with criteria; "settled": noul; "enough": noul}. Verbatim wordings given in the paper: settled = "is the answer already fixed, so that someone with more information could know it?" (App. A uses "Is the answer to the question already fixed at this moment, so that someone with more information than is given here could know it for certain?"); enough = "do the paragraphs contain enough information to answer for certain?"; known = "do you know the answer for certain, rather than having to guess?". Policy-case example in App. A also carries "do the facts determine the answer with certainty?" (P(yes)=0.04 there). Answer instruction: "Answer the question using the facts given."
  - Readout: P(yes) used as a ranking score (AUROC), validated as ranking, not as a probability of an epistemic state. Thresholds "must be set per wording on held-out data" (absolute levels vary across 14 rewordings, ranking is stable). "cannot tell" option: added as an extra Choice criterion; chosen by Jev 0.91 when the fact is deleted.
  - Control design to copy: for each contrast, build a text-only baseline (char 2 to 5-gram TF-IDF + logistic regression, out of fold), length-matched strata, and paired item variants (complete vs incomplete, same paragraph count).
  - Cost: Jev priced $0.042 per million input tokens, outputs free; extra Noul questions in the same call change answers only within replicate noise.
- Authors' stated limitations: one version of one closed model, no claim about other decision models or LLMs; known contrasts are proxies (fabricated vs real, period); enough was checked only against length features; temporal findings not confirmed on a fresh panel; date removal changes propositions (30 of 112 "yes" pairs lost event identity; annotation by a single AI assistant, human check pending); scores are rankings, not calibrated epistemic probabilities; no cost-based deployment policy evaluated.
- My caveats: the "enough" evidence is on deleted-paragraph HotpotQA and generated policy cases, where absence is created by deleting a stated fact, so a surface mismatch (question mentions something not in the text) may help; that is close to, but not the same as, a lab W2 case where a plausible default exists. The "enough" AUROC of 0.95 is a discrimination number on curated contrasts, not an operating point (no precision/recall at a threshold for the policy cases is given in the body); accepting by enough was 2 to 6 points worse than accepting by confidence at the same coverage because Jev often answers correctly without the paragraphs (Sec. 4.2). Hosted Jev only.
- Relevance to the lab: bears on lab item 2 (W2 sufficiency; set3, set4, set5) and on the J/G encoders (item 3). Verdict: extends the lab's finding (confirms "decides anyway" for hosted Jev: 0.49 to 0.80 with nothing to decide on) and supplies a new idea worth trying (a separately asked, answer-free sufficiency question). Concrete reuse:
  1. Correction by the survey editor (checked against `lab/experiments/set4-advanced_method/p1/frozen_methods.json` and `out/fresh_analysis.md`): set4's check C1 on K27 was already an answer-free sufficiency question ("does the outcome depend on a fact that the input leaves unknown...?"). As a hard yes/no it said yes on 14/16 W2 missing-evidence vs 6/36 W2 substantive cases, but only 6/20 W3 missing-evidence cases and 0/17 W3 false executions. What remains untried is to read C1's P(yes) as a score (AUROC per class, held-out threshold), and to pose it on W12/A27/J/G. Original suggestion, kept for the per-readout details: add an answer-free sufficiency question to the W2 cases and read its P(yes) as a missing-fact score. For W12/A27 (letter logits): pose "Does the record contain enough information to decide for certain? A. yes B. no" as a 2-option choice and read P(A). For K27 (pointer head): offer two option spans "enough" / "not enough". For J/G encoders: noul/score with the same sentence. Report AUROC on paired complete/incomplete W2 cases, with a text-length-matched baseline and a char n-gram TF-IDF baseline, choose the threshold on a held-out split per wording.
  2. Use their "may be irrelevant" style preamble ("the facts may be incomplete") and an explicit "cannot tell" option, and measure how often the fallback is chosen at the lab's actual W2 prevalence (their option helped only when the unanswerable share was around 27% or more).
  3. G (GLiNER2.5-Decide) was near-uniform with no information (p_max 0.01 to 0.02 on opaque codes) while hosted Jev was not; test G's normalised entropy as a missing-evidence detector on W2 (it was underconfident once informed).

## 2609.35342 — Jev thinks "I don't know", but doesn't say it: Introducing Sys1Cal-v1 Dataset for Probability Calibration
- Authors: Riccardo Porcedda (little-g.ai; Sant'Anna School of Advanced Studies; University of Pisa). Submitted 28 Sep 2026 (v1). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35342. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts: hosted Jev through its three primitives (Noul, Choice, Score) and SemIf (an open-source Choice-style baseline; size not stated in the body).
- Design: Sys1Cal-v1, 365 rendered True/False items from 92 problems (6 state families: explicit probabilities, frequencies from counts, compound, conditional, Bayes rule, sequential Bayes; rendered as direct, counts, ratios, tables, prose, nested state, distractor-augmented). Exact P(A) known by construction. Each item queried 10 times and averaged; metric OVL = 1 - |p_hat - p*| (soft accuracy). English, no preregistration; the T/U/F model is fitted post hoc.
- Key results: Mean OVL Jev-Choice 0.764 (TV 0.236), Jev-Noul 0.918, Jev-Score 0.886, SemIf-Choice 0.629 (Table 1). Choice is pushed to high True probabilities when P(A) >= 0.5 and fuzzier below (Fig. 1). Fitting a latent third value from the Score expectation mu_S: pi_U = lambda * mu^alpha * (1-mu)^beta, giving alpha = 0.530, beta = 1.011, R2 = 0.834 for predicting Choice, mean absolute-error reduction 0.110 (CI excludes 0); global lambda_hat 0.978 [0.789, 1.000]; per-problem lambda_j 0.821 [0.760, 0.882] (Table 2). Inverse correction raises Choice OVL mean 0.764 to 0.880 and median 0.771 to 0.903; keeping the interval P(A) in [T, T+U] gives mean OVL 0.931, median 0.978, with mean width 0.292 (Table 3). Abstract states median 0.771 to 0.978 and the conclusion states "median interval OVL 0.971": the body (Table 3) says 0.978, the conclusion 0.971, an internal inconsistency.
- Proposed method and cost: T/U/F representation: T = mu_S, U = lambda mu^alpha (1-mu)^beta, F = 1-T-U, read from one Score call (10 levels "Completely false" ... "Completely true", z_j = j/9) plus one Choice call. Decision rule in the worked example (Cerr=100, defer cost 45): robust Gamma-minimax picks "defer" when the interval [T, T+U] = [0.400, 0.767] makes both commits costlier than 45. Cost is one extra Score call.
- Authors' stated limitations: synthetic, templated, limited ecological coverage; one-dimensional projection of Score; the U model is an observational account, "indicates but does not prove" that Jev represents a hidden third value.
- My caveats: the task is numerical probability semantics (stated probabilities), not missing evidence; U is whatever mass the Score and Choice readouts disagree on, fitted with 3 parameters on the same 365 items with no held-out split, so 0.978 is in-sample; wider intervals are scored more leniently (OVL_TUF is not comparable to binary OVL, the authors say so). Whether Choice vs Score disagreement signals "evidence is missing" is not tested at all. Hosted Jev only.
- Relevance to the lab: background only for item 2 (it does not test missing information); at most a new idea. Verdict: background only, with one speculative idea. Idea: on W2 cases ask the same sufficiency proposition once as a binary Choice (yes/no) and once as a graded Score (10 ordered levels, mapped to [0,1]); a large Choice-minus-Score gap (the paper's U) could flag "decided anyway" items. Readout: W12/A27 letter logits (the Score as 10 lettered levels, expectation computed from the letter distribution); J/G encoders have a score primitive natively. K27 pointer head: score levels as 10 option spans. Untested for missing evidence; try only as an offline diagnostic.

## 2609.39496 — When the Right Answer Is Missing: An Arithmetic-Dependent Rejection Bottleneck in Jev
- Authors: Jike Zhong, Ming Li, Yuxiang Lai (USC; Florida; Emory), equal contribution. Submitted 30 Sep 2026 (v1). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39496. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts: hosted Jev (Choice and native Boolean types); a generative control Qwen3.5-9B with reasoning. Three formulations: Menu Choice {A, B, C, NONE}; candidate-level true/false Choice ("T/F Choice", one proposition per candidate, categorical type); native Boolean (same questions, only the type field changed).
- Design: paired answer-present vs answer-absent menus with exact ground truth. Main: 100 problems each for two-operation arithmetic (a+b-c, a in [300,999], b,c in [10,99]), numerical lookup, purchase totals (qp+s); present menus use answer offsets {-2, 0, +3}, absent {-2, +1, +3}; candidate order shuffled within each coverage condition. Time and counting: 20 screening + 80 fresh cases. Contextual: 128 inventory problems, 64 each refund-policy and logical-eligibility. Further: depth 0 to 5 (50 paired cases per depth), magnitude bands (100 fresh cases per band), candidate distance (50 cases), operator patterns (10 per pattern), 15 pilot families (20 paired cases each). Paired bootstrap, 20,000 resamples. Rejection means "the supplied candidates are incorrect", not that the problem is unanswerable. English.
- Key results:
  - Arithmetic: Menu 99% answer-present accuracy, 7% correct rejection; T/F Choice 61%/27%; Boolean 97%/99%. Purchase: Menu 94/24, T/F 45/17, Boolean 73/76. Time: 100/63 (Menu), 100/99 (Boolean). Object counting 97/87. Lookup 100/100 for all (Fig. 3).
  - Qwen3.5-9B (reasoning) menu accuracy 100%/100% on arithmetic and purchase (the 66 of 67 errors it makes are token-limit truncations).
  - Supplying the computed result in the input restores Menu rejection 7% to 100%. Contextual inventory: 127/128 correct selections, 14/128 correct rejections; refund-policy and logical-eligibility controls 64/64 under all interfaces (Sec. 4.2 and 4.3).
  - Rejection collapses at two operations (Menu present/absent 100/66 at one op, 98/4 at two); at 1 to 9 digit operands rejection is 56%, then 8, 14, 11, 14% for bigger bands. Larger distractor error helps Menu rejection (20% to 88%) and T/F (16% to 84%) but Boolean declines 100% to 84% (Fig. 4). Multiplication/division chains reject well (98%, 96% single; 9/10 and 10/10 for a*b*c), mixed with +/- badly (0 to 3 of 10).
  - Label wording: "Other" 99/9, "None of the above" 99/6, vs NONE 99/7; moving NONE across four positions recovers 5, 0, 2, 0 of 40 failures (Sec. 4.5).
  - Score separation: T/F arithmetic AUC 0.985 while only 88/200 exact-match menus; purchase AUC 0.942 with 62/200 exact (Sec. 5.1). At the default 0.5 threshold T/F accepts multiple candidates on 55/200 arithmetic menus vs 3/200 for Boolean.
  - Threshold repair (Table 2, Fig. 8), original to calibrated correct counts, held-out: Arithmetic Menu tau=.03: 99->97 present, 7->79 absent. T/F tau=.74: 61->86, 27->90. Purchase Menu tau=.03: 94->86, 24->79; T/F tau=.80: 45->73, 17->82; Boolean tau=.90: 73->87, 76->97. Time Menu tau=.13: 80->80, 49->72. Counting Menu tau=.09: 78->76, 73->78. Balanced accuracy for arithmetic rises 53% to 88%.
- Method / recipe (implementation):
  - Menu rule (Eq. 2): reject if p_NONE >= tau, checked BEFORE argmax; otherwise take the arg max over the three candidates. NONE need not be the largest option. Candidate rule: accept candidate i if its truth score s_i >= tau, with all three judgments required correct; one positive selects that candidate, no positive rejects, several positives count as an error.
  - Threshold choice: search tau in {0, .01, ..., 1} separately per task and interface on 20 development problems each with a present and an absent menu; objective = mean case accuracy over the two balanced conditions; ties prefer the threshold closest to 0.5, then the smaller threshold. Development sets are disjoint from the 100-case (or 80 fresh) evaluation sets; no evaluation labels used. Offline on saved scores, no retraining, no additional inference.
  - Alternative that works without thresholds: native Boolean verification per candidate (changing only the type field), at the cost of one yes/no per candidate in the same request.
- Authors' stated limitations: hosted API only, no weights; mechanism not identified; synthetic numerical/symbolic data; future work on real retrieval settings, cross-task calibration and training for rejection.
- My caveats: only 20 development problems per task choose the threshold (tiny; the neighbouring paper 2610.03387 shows such thresholds fail to transfer); the failing tasks are exact-computation tasks where the correct answer exists but is not offered; nothing is claimed for the lab's case where the decisive fact (not a candidate) is missing. Failure is task-dependent (lookup, refund policy and logical eligibility show no rejection gap), so it does not by itself predict the lab's W2 results.
- Relevance to the lab: item 2 (fallback options missing_evidence/no_matching_candidate, item 3) and item 4 (set4 gates). Verdict: extends the lab's finding (an explicit rejection option does not get chosen; NONE relabelling or repositioning does not help) and offers a new idea (threshold on p(fallback) instead of argmax). Concrete reuse: the lab already has fallback classes and logged per-option probabilities; compute p(missing_evidence) (K27 pointer-head score for that span; W12/A27 letter logit of the fallback letter; J/G encoder score) and apply "reject if p_fallback >= tau" with tau chosen on a small dev split, using the same objective (mean of present/absent accuracy, ties toward the smaller threshold). This needs no new inference (offline on set3/set4 logs). Check AUROC of p(fallback) between paired complete/incomplete W2 cases first; the paper's Fig. 7 shows the fallback score separates classes although it is not the argmax. Also try their native-yes/no-per-candidate verification: one pointer-head/letter query per candidate "is this candidate correct?" (set4's blind verification asked a similar kind of question; the new point is that per-candidate verification with a native boolean-type readout rejects much better than the same question as a categorical choice).

## 2610.03387 — Benchmarking candidate coverage and rejection policy transfer in typed decision models
- Authors: Jiawen Lu, Tongtong Wu (Monash University). Submitted October 2026; this is v2 dated 6 Oct 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03387. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts: Laya 0.3.21 (open encoder, English checkpoint pinned; per-option-count temperatures 1.906/1.760/1.000/0.5), hosted Jev (jev-1.13.0, Choice with option probabilities), and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (greedy BF16, generated key, discrete only; 1,369 interface failures in the core run, kept in the denominators).
- Design: public reference labels define coverage (G=1 if the reference label is offered). Five datasets: AG News (4 classes), DBpedia (14), Emotion (6), TREC (6, supplemental), CLINC150 (150 intents + OOS). 1,456 classification texts + 750 CLINC queries; calibration texts 80/280/120/120/350, test texts 120/420/166/150/400; 224,964 neural-model responses. Three missing-answer sources: artificial omission (reference label swapped for a wrong label at equal k), natural retrieval misses (BM25 top-k intents, k=2, 5, 10, no gold repair), global OOS queries. Metrics: D = P(reject | missing), F = P(reject | covered). Text-level bootstrap (2,000 draws). Seeds and revisions pinned. English.
- Key results:
  - Policy transfer with 80 calibration pairs per source task at k=2 (Fig. 2): six of twelve task transfers exceed 5% false rejection for each of Laya and Jev (three of six without supplemental TREC). Jev's DBpedia threshold (tau=0.06) on Emotion: D=92.2% [88.0, 95.8] but F=69.3% [62.0, 76.5] (n=166). Laya AG News policy on TREC: D=98.7%, F=66.7%. Jev's Emotion-calibrated threshold is tau=1.00, which disables rejection (D/F=0/0 on all targets); recalibrating on 80 AG News pairs restores D/F=82.5%/3.3%. Even target recalibration can exceed 5% F (both DBpedia diagonal entries).
  - CLINC (Table 2): as k goes 2 to 10, Laya OOS detection 83.0% to 99.0% but covered false rejection 15.2% to 48.8%; Jev 84.0% to 75.0% OOS detection with F 3.3% to 3.1%; Qwen 85.0% to 63.0% with F 6.3% to 2.4%. Artificial omission at k=5: Laya D/F 86.0/27.7, Jev 63.7/3.3, Qwen 45.0/3.7. Natural-miss denominators are tiny (31, 11, 5 queries at k=2, 5, 10). The BM25 scope gate rejects 0% of retrieved menus by construction (it shares the retriever's winner) but 82.3%/17.7% on artificial omission.
  - Score quality (Table 5), balanced pairs, DBpedia k=13: Jev none-score AUROC 0.987, Brier 0.068, local calibrated D/F 98.8/3.6; Laya 0.865/0.191, 34.0/2.4. On Emotion k=5: AUROC Laya 0.636, Jev 0.704; calibrated D/F 19.3/7.8 and 25.3/7.8. Native Emotion k=5: Jev correct 45.8%, wrong 28.9%, rejected 25.3%, D=53.0%.
  - Wording: neutral option identifiers lower Qwen detection by 20.0 points on AG News k=3 and 30.6 on DBpedia k=13 (n=20 texts per task); task-aligned NONE wording raises Laya detection by 25.0 points on Emotion k=5 (10.0 to 45.0), no change for Jev or Qwen.
  - Jev and Qwen both select the reference label on 97.4%/96.9% of covered DBpedia (k=13) inputs but detect only 82.4%/25.0% of missing references.
- Method / recipe (implementation): none-score policies from three absence scores: negative maximum probability and entropy normalised by log k (from plain requests), and raw p(none) (from explicit-NONE requests). For each configuration and score, choose the threshold that maximises calibration detection subject to false rejection <= 5%; thresholds are observed calibration scores; ties prefer lower false rejection, then higher threshold; reject if s > tau (strict). Source thresholds applied unchanged to targets; target-local reference uses its own 80 labelled pairs. Exact NONE wordings (e.g. "None of the listed emotions best describes the emotion expressed in this text.") are in App. A.2; NONE is inserted at a seeded slot. BM25 reference: k1=1.2, b=0.75, max document score per intent. Costs: only labelled calibration pairs; no retraining.
- Authors' stated limitations: coverage follows public labels (overlapping descriptions leave acceptable alternatives after removal); balanced samples differ from deployment frequencies; one checkpoint per model; natural-miss estimates rest on few cases; model pretraining overlap unknown; empirical constraint gives no population risk guarantee.
- My caveats: Qwen2.5-7B is an old, small control; its failures counted as non-answers; Table 6 controls use only 20 texts per task; the policy transfer matrix mixes distribution and rubric shifts. Jev here is hosted; Laya open. The quantisation of Jev probabilities (two decimals) can matter for saturated scores (App. B: AUROC changes up to 0.069 for Laya when quantised).
- Relevance to the lab: item 2 and 3 (fallback options, calibrated thresholds, benchmark p3 screening); item 8 (margin gates). Verdict: extends the lab's finding (rejection options are brittle; selection accuracy conceals rejection failure) and warns about the threshold recipe in 2609.39496. Concrete reuse: when applying a p(fallback) threshold on K27/W12/A27, calibrate per candidate configuration (option count and wording) with a stated false-rejection budget (they use F <= 5%), and report D and F for each W-class together; do not carry a threshold between W1/W2/W3 or between option counts. Their task-aligned "None of the listed ... " wording is a cheap factor to test on the fallback span (it moved Laya D by +25 points but did nothing for Jev/Qwen). Do a transfer matrix W1->W2->W3 with 80 pairs.

## 2609.30706 — LAVOIR: Teaching a Single-Pass Decision Encoder When and What to Ask with Amortized Value of Information
- Authors: Furkan Yilmaz, Habibe Aleyna Tasdemir, Muhammed Faruk Gozay. Submitted 25 Sep 2026 (v1). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30706. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts: LAVOIR = open encoder (Laya head on ModernBERT-large, about 0.4B) with an added slot block and a VOI head; one forward pass returns the option distribution and a predicted value per candidate missing slot. Jev and Laya numbers are copied from Laya's reports (apart from Fig. 1 the Laya checkpoints were not run); one seed.
- Design: 12 hand-written customer-service schemas (8 train, 4 held-out zero-shot), each with 5 to 6 units, 3 to 4 decisive slots and 1 non-decisive slot; exact Bayes posterior by enumeration (Eq. 1) gives the Bayes ceiling. Messages written by Qwen3.6-35B-A3B from partial profiles, checked by Gemma-4-26B-A4B (leakage and fidelity gates; 21,601 training, 5,379 seen-schema and 5,432 zero-shot test examples). Each message has 2 to 4 complete profiles so the regression estimates an expectation. Answers simulated: clean 0.675, partial 0.125, "I don't know" 0.10, over-informative 0.10. Real data: 918 ABCD test dialogues (ten flows as units) and 3,812 SGD first turns. Policies capped at 2 questions; baselines B2 (never ask), B3 (random unresolved slot when max p < tau), B5 (ask the top-VOI slot when a conformal-like prediction set has more than one unit), ORACLE (greedy exact VOI). English, one seed, no preregistration.
- Key results: Seen schemas: decision accuracy 0.653 vs Bayes ceiling 0.659 (gap -0.006 [-0.016, 0.004]); question-policy AUC 0.799 to 0.800 vs ORACLE 0.800 to 0.803 (Table 1); with <= 0.5 questions per conversation, 12.9 to 14.4 points above never asking and 7.2 to 9.5 above random. Zero-shot schemas: accuracy 0.51 to 0.52 vs ceiling 0.667; VOI policy still ahead of baselines (AUC 0.02 to 0.05 above B5). Without the cap the model asks in 93% of SGD and 92% of ABCD cases although SGD arguments cannot change the intent (SGD median max VOI 0.133 among 3,526 cases with confidence >= 0.99, where the bound allows about 0.02); with the Gini cap the SGD question rate falls to 8.6% (c=.02/.05/.1: .086/.063/.048), SGD AUROC(VOI to error) 0.84. ABCD: the real exchange raises accuracy +8.3 [4.9, 11.7] where LAVOIR asks (529 cases) and -0.3 [-1.5, 1.0] where it does not (389); the uncapped model gains +2.7 in both groups (Table 2). On the 12 Laya benchmarks, LAVOIR is above Laya on 7 of 12; a yes/no collapse in the controlled model (spam: "false" on 398/400) was fixed by question-form yes/no items (spam 0.480 to 0.993). Calibration remains weak on real dialogues: ABCD ECE 0.186, and the model is wrong in 19% of the cases with confidence >= 0.99, in which the cap correctly stays silent.
- Method / recipe (implementation): input = [CLS] question type + instruction, one [MASK] per option, then a block headed "missing information:" with one [MASK] per candidate slot, then the state; a zero-initialised segment embedding makes it bit-identical to Laya when no slots are given. VOI head: 0.27M-parameter MLP over the slot-marker output and features of the option distribution (max prob, top-2 margin, entropy/log m, m/255). Gini cap: v_hat_k = G(p) * sigmoid(h_k), G(p)=1 - sum p_y^2 detached. Training target: t_k = p_hat_k(y*) - p_hat_0(y*), the gain in the probability of the GOLD unit after a sampled answer is appended (not self-referential confidence), MSE on target-standardised values, weight lambda=1, targets recomputed from a frozen warm snapshot. Policy: ask k* = arg max v_hat_k if v_hat_k* > c (c=0.02, 0.05, 0.1 swept) and fewer than Q=2 questions asked, otherwise decide, or hand off if 1 - max p > c_h (c_h=0.3 hands off 18% and gives 0.973 accuracy on the rest). Question content is a template per slot, not generated. Cost: 28 to 48 minutes on 8 to 16 GH200 GPUs; 31 ms median per question (GH200).
- Authors' stated limitations: synthetic dialogues from a simulator whose answer kinds and rates they chose; LLM-written text may be unusually cooperative; real-data tests are single exchanges; zero-shot decisions 15 points under the ceiling; calibration out of distribution is weak; privacy schema overlap caused 22% message rejection (selection effect); missing baselines (slow VOI that re-encodes every possible answer, an LLM agent, an ablation of the self-referential target); English only, one seed, questions are slot templates.
- My caveats: the slots are defined by the schema author, and the gold is rule-defined; in the lab's W2 cases the decisive fact is not enumerated in the input, so the model would have to be given candidate slots. Training needs thousands of synthetic cases and a trained encoder; zero-shot schemas fail, so the recipe is not usable on new W2 tasks without retraining. The Gini cap is not a missing-information detector: it silences asking when the model is already confident, so it would stay silent on exactly the lab's confident-wrong W2 failures (ABCD 19% wrong at confidence >= 0.99).
- Relevance to the lab: item 2 and set5 (re-asking). Verdict: new idea worth trying, in a training-free form. Concrete reuse: implement the paper's "slow VOI" baseline (missing from the paper) by counterfactual probing at inference: for each plausible value v of the candidate missing fact (the lab knows the W2 decisive fact and its values by construction), append "Fact: <v>" to the state and re-run; if the decision changes across plausible values while the original input had p>=0.8, the fact was decisive yet unmentioned, so the case should abstain/ask. VOI estimate = mean over v of the gain in the max or gold-option probability. This differs from set5 (which narrowed the option list; second-pass probabilities were just renormalised) because it changes the information, not the option set. Readout: any one-pass scorer is cheap here (K27 pointer head or W12/A27 letter logits, one forward pass per value); it also gives a ceiling for the lab: if K27 reads an appended fact correctly (the paper's decision-accuracy-at-the-Bayes-ceiling shows a trained encoder does), the failure is in the absence signal, not in reading. J/G encoders: could be given a slot block only if retrained (LAVOIR used Laya/ModernBERT; J = GLiFormer-large would need the same).

## 2609.34024 — Jev in Medicine: A Benchmark Evaluation
- Authors: Alfredo Madrid-Garcia, Beatriz Merino-Barbancho (independent researchers, Madrid). Submitted Sep 2026; this is v3 dated 4 Oct 2026 (first-submission date not shown in the text). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34024. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts: hosted Jev 1.13 via OpenRouter, a single Choice question per item (native probabilities per option); reference GPT-6 Sol (medium reasoning, no reasoning), JSON-schema-constrained option plus verbalised probabilities. Each model/condition run once, 26 to 27 Sep 2026, options in original order.
- Design: four benchmarks: MetaMedQA (1,373 USMLE-style items, 6 options A-D, "None of the above", "I don't know or cannot answer"; 277 items without a correct substantive option: 115 NOTA, 162 IDK, including 100 on a fictional organ), PubMedQA (500; yes/no/maybe; conclusions removed), DiagnosisArena-MCQ (915), NEJM Case Challenges (34). Wilson CIs, paired bootstrap (2,000), exact McNemar with Holm correction, ECE with ten bins, AUROC, AURC. Thresholds 0.5/0.8/0.9 fixed in advance, not tuned. English. No preregistration; results labelled preliminary.
- Key results: Accuracy Jev vs GPT-6 Sol medium: MetaMedQA 74.8 vs 82.7; PubMedQA 78.4 vs 78.2; DiagnosisArena 59.8 vs 82.4; NEJM 61.8 vs 82.4 (Holm P=0.078) (Table 2). MetaMedQA calibration ECE 0.063 vs 0.146; Jev answers with p>=0.9 (52.9% of items) are 93.4% right, but at similar coverage GPT-6 Sol is as accurate and AURC is worse for Jev (0.087 vs 0.070). DiagnosisArena AUROC 0.645 vs 0.768; only 17.3% of Jev answers reach 0.9 and these are right 75.9% of the time (Table 3). Unanswerable (Sec. 3.4): Jev answered the 1,096 answerable-with-substantive-option questions correctly 86.5% (948); selected "None of the above" for 53.9% (62/115) of NOTA items; selected "I don't know" for only 10.5% (17/162; CI 6.7 to 16.2) of IDK-correct items and a substantive option for 64.2% (104); on the 100 fictional-organ questions: 52 substantive, 38 NOTA, 10 IDK; when Jev did select IDK, it was right 17 of 18 times. GPT-6 Sol (medium): NOTA recall 73.9%, IDK recall 8.6% (4.3% without reasoning). Context from the cited MetaMedQA authors: GPT-4o reached unknown recall 3.7% with the baseline prompt and 44.4 to 51.8% with prompts that warned that questions might be unanswerable (cited, not run here). PubMedQA "maybe" recall 27.3% (15/55) for Jev vs 85.1% yes and 84.0% no. Cost for all 2,823 items US$0.082; median latency 0.27 to 0.31 s.
- Proposed method: none. The authors suggest (untested) two remedies: tell the model that some questions may be unanswerable, or ask a separate question about whether the supplied information is sufficient; they also did not test whether low probabilities flag unanswerable items by threshold deferral.
- Authors' stated limitations: text only; contamination cannot be excluded; each condition run once, option order and repeatability not assessed; GPT-6 Sol probabilities verbalised not native; intermediary adds latency; not designed for equivalence; reference answers not re-adjudicated; findings preliminary.
- My caveats: Jev's IDK option is just one of six options in a fixed position; recall of IDK is low but precision is high (17/18), which means IDK works as a rare-and-reliable output, not a detector; no threshold analysis of p(IDK) was done, so the 10.5% is an argmax number. Mixed categories (NOTA vs IDK vs fictional organ) have different semantics.
- Relevance to the lab: item 2 (and item 3 fallback classes). Verdict: already shows what the lab found (prior/parallel work): a plausible-substantive-answer is chosen for most unanswerable cases (64.2% of IDK-correct, 52% of fictional-organ questions), with the same weakness in a frontier LLM (IDK recall 8.6%). Concrete reuse: (a) report recall and precision of the explicit fallback option separately (the lab's missing_evidence class), because high precision/low recall is a different failure from low precision; (b) re-score the lab logs by p(fallback) instead of argmax (as in 2609.39496), the very analysis this paper says it did not do; (c) test an instruction preamble warning that the record may lack the decisive fact on W12/A27 (letter-logit models take an instruction), since the cited unknown-recall jump for GPT-4o (3.7% to 44.4-51.8%) suggests a cheap lever; K27 pointer head: put the warning in the question text. MetaMedQA (277 unanswerable items with labelled NOTA/IDK) could serve as an external test set.

## 2609.33843 — Laya as a Typed Probabilistic Assessor: An Independent Reproduction and a Preregistered Study of Calibration and Selective Escalation
- Authors: Gowthamkumar Nandakishore (independent; no affiliation with Convai Innovations, LocalLLaMA or TypeSafe). Submitted 27 Sep 2026 (v1). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33843. Read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08. I read Sections 1 to 4, 6 (heading only), 7, 9.1 and the abstract; Sections 5, 6 detail, 8, appendices were skimmed or not read.
- Systems and readouts: Laya Typed-Decisions (open, 421M-parameter ModernBERT-large encoder; choice/noul/score outputs; shipped per-option-count temperature table), a clean re-trained Track B replication, laya base, laya-multilingual (mmBERT-base), a ModernBERT-base specialist. Hosted Jev was NOT run (no API access); its numbers on the Typed Decisions benchmark are third-party (dataset card: accuracy 0.727, ECE 0.144, p50 710 ms). A hosted zero-shot Decider 1 is also listed third-party (0.768).
- Design: Typed Decisions benchmark, 4 workflow subsets, 300 train and 100 test cases each, 5 typed questions per case (6,000 train / 2,000 test decisions); gold = mean of 3 samples from a roughly 4B-class teacher. Preregistration frozen 2026-09-26 15:50 UTC; E1 (reproduction) retrospective, E2 to E8 prospectively registered (22 executed confirmatory tests, 20 reject under BH FDR q=0.05; two descoped). Track A: 400 test cases split 200 calibration / 200 eval at case level (seed 20260926); Track B: re-train on 960 / 240 validation. Case-level cluster bootstrap, 10,000 resamples. English.
- Key results (relevant to calibration and escalation): the shipped checkpoint is uniformly under-confident (signed gap -0.214; ECE 0.214; every occupied bin's accuracy exceeds its confidence); one disjointly fitted temperature T=0.469 (sharpening) cuts held-out ECE 0.204 to 0.037; the shipped table softens (T=1.25 to 1.98), directionally backwards here. Accuracy reproduces the card (0.767 vs 0.766). Selective escalation (Sec. 7): gate q = max p >= tau chosen on calibration as the smallest tau with accepted-set error <= 0.10: tau=0.70 (calibration coverage 0.644, error 0.0916); on eval coverage 0.672 [0.637, 0.704], accepted accuracy 0.8601, accepted error 0.1399, so the target is missed (53% relative degradation); on Track B 0.0866 to 0.1028. Confidence does beat random escalation at matched rate (+0.0646 [0.0502, 0.0785]). Cause: isotonic calibrator and threshold fitted on the same 1,000 decisions; single temperature lands at the target (tau=0.75, eval 0.100, coverage 0.598); an upper-confidence-bound rule (Wilson or cluster bootstrap 95% bound on calibration error below target) picks tau=0.80 and delivers 0.101 at coverage 0.534. Case-level gating (all five decisions confident) collapses to 0.055 coverage. High-confidence errors persist: security_incidents 26/177 accepted decisions wrong (14.7%), invoice 16/187 (8.6%). Exploratory OOD probe (E10, own design, 24 fictional documents, 120 claims, 60 supported/60 not): noul accuracy 0.617 [0.533, 0.683] vs 0.858 in-distribution; false acceptance of unsupported claims 0.333 [0.218, 0.439]; confidence AUROC 0.589; no grid threshold reaches the 10% error target at nonzero coverage; entity swaps and one-step inference at chance (0.500); retrieval recall@5 0.84.
- Method / recipe: confidence q = max_k p_k after calibration; threshold chosen on a frozen twelve-point grid from calibration data; the paper's own fix is a single temperature plus a threshold chosen by an upper confidence bound, evaluated once. Cost: Laya about 18.4 ms per decision (batch 1, 2,000 decisions in 36.8 s) on this author's Apple-silicon machine.
- Authors' stated limitations: all scores measure agreement with a synthetic teacher whose self-agreement ceiling (0.735) the specialist exceeds, so accuracy is not correctness; no runnable strong judge, so cascade accuracy is only an oracle upper bound; E9 external-validity set descoped; Jev never run.
- My caveats: the unsupported-claim probe is small (120 claims, 24 documents) and self-designed; and it is a claim-verification task, not option selection with a missing fact. The calibration direction (under-confident) is specific to this checkpoint and benchmark.
- Relevance to the lab: items 2, 3, 8 (margin gates), and set4's unanimity/review rules. Verdict: extends the lab's finding in a way that matters for the J/G encoders: confidence gates beat random but miss their error target out-of-sample, confident errors persist at the top of the scale, and a threshold fitted on one split is optimistic. Reuse: (a) fit gates with an upper-confidence-bound rule rather than the point estimate (lab thresholds from small W2 dev sets are likely optimistic in the same way, as set4's "no better than random review" suggests); (b) report accepted-set error on a split not used for calibration; (c) use the claim-verification probe design (supported vs not-supported claims, with claim types) as a template for a missing-evidence noul test on J/G; entity-swap and inference claims were at chance, so expect J/G noul to be near chance on subtle absences.

## Cross-paper notes

What the lab has done (per the episode reports): set4 used blind yes/no verification (3 checks, caught 4/17 errors, all conflicting-records, 0 of 11 missing-evidence), shown-answer verification, wording vote, and model votes; set5 re-asked K27 with narrowed options (changed at most 3/120 answers, because second-pass probabilities are the first-pass probabilities renormalised).

Ideas the lab has NOT tried, ranked by how promising they look for missing-evidence detection. (Editor's note: item 1 was in fact tried on K27 as set4 check C1, as a hard yes/no; it worked on W2 (14/16 vs 6/36) and not on W3 (6/20 missing-evidence, 0/17 false executions). The untried part is using its probability as a thresholded score and asking it of other systems. See [../reinvention-check.md](../reinvention-check.md).)

1. Answer-free sufficiency question (2610.01006). "Does the text contain enough information to decide for certain?" asked as a separate yes/no, with a paired complete/incomplete W2 design, length-matched and n-gram text baselines, a per-wording threshold. It is the only method in this set with a reported detection AUROC on deleted-fact cases (0.95 on HotpotQA, P(enough) 0.83 to 0.10 on policy cases with the deciding fact deleted). set4's C1 already asked an answer-free question of this kind on K27 (see the editor's note above). Cost: one extra query per case, no training. Caveat: shown only on hosted Jev; check on K27/W12/A27 in an inexpensive 2-option form.
2. Threshold on the fallback option's probability, not on argmax (2609.39496 with the transfer warning of 2610.03387). Needs only logged probabilities from set3/set4 runs, so it can be evaluated offline for free. Calibrate per configuration with a false-rejection budget (F <= 5%), choose the threshold on a dev split and apply the upper-confidence-bound rule of 2609.33843 to avoid the optimism they measured; evaluate on a fresh split; report D and F together. Warning: Jev's DBpedia threshold gave F=69.3% on Emotion.
3. Counterfactual value-of-information probing (adapted from 2609.30706's oracle VOI, Eq. 2). Append each plausible value of the missing fact, re-run, and flag cases where the decision flips although the original p>=0.8. It changes the information rather than the option set (set5 changed only the option set and found nothing). Costs one forward pass per value on one-pass scorers. Not validated by the paper (the paper's learned head and its slow-VOI baseline are different things); the Gini-capped learned head would stay silent on confident errors, so only the probing form is relevant.
4. Warning/permission preamble and explicit "cannot tell" option (2609.34024 citing 3.7% to 44.4-51.8% unknown recall; 2610.01006 "paragraphs may be irrelevant" 0.75 to 0.84 accuracy; 2610.03387 task-aligned NONE wording +25.0 points for Laya). Cheap, and the lab has not reported it; but 2609.39496 shows relabelling/repositioning NONE did nothing for Jev, so expect model-dependent effects.
5. Native boolean per-candidate verification (2609.39496: 99% absent-case exact match vs 27% for the same questions as a categorical choice). Lab's set4 blind checks may have been categorical; checking whether a binary-typed readout (noul/yes-no logits) changes the result is a small experiment.
6. Choice-vs-Score disagreement as a third truth value (2609.35342). Speculative; the paper does not test missing information.

Parallel prior work (already shows what the lab found): 2609.34024 (IDK recall 10.5%, 64.2% substantive answers on IDK-correct items), 2610.01006 (up to 0.80 on a salient guess with no information; related but non-supporting text lowers accuracy without lowering confidence), 2609.39496 (7% rejection with an explicit NONE option).

Contradiction/nuance to keep in view: rejection failure is task-dependent in 2609.39496 (lookup, refund policy and logical eligibility showed no rejection gap), and the explicit "not known" option helps only when unanswerable items are common (2610.01006: -0.034 at 5%, +0.051 at 50%). The lab's W2 prevalence therefore decides whether a fallback-option approach is net positive. The authors of 2610.01006 and 2609.35342 and 2609.39496 all evaluated hosted Jev only, and only 2610.03387 and 2609.30706/2609.33843 run open models (Laya); none ran a pointer-head or letter-logit LLM like K27/W12/A27, so all transfers to the lab's systems are untested.
