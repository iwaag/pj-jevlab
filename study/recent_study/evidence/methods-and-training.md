# Methods for open decision models: readouts, order invariance, training objectives, ordinal scales, calibration

Theme summary. The ten papers here (plus the two evaluation papers in `evaluation-methodology.md`) ask what has to be fixed around a one-pass typed decision readout. Three answers recur. (1) Position and label bias are properties of the readout and can be reduced at inference by permutation averaging (AnyJev), or removed by construction only if the model is trained with an order-blind mask or head (block-causal CI, ufakzeka-karar, prior-fused SBERT2S1). (2) For one-pass scorers, the RLCD-style policy-gradient objective gives no accuracy or post-temperature calibration advantage over plain cross-entropy; the RL advantage reported by OpenJev-RLCD is confined to models that generate a rationale first. (3) Accuracy hides distribution-level failures (ordinal scale compression, question-blindness of fine-tuned specialists), so evaluation must include counterfactual and population-level checks.

## 2610.00831 — AnyJev Technical Report: Typed Decisions from One Prefill of Any Open LLM
- Authors: Jiamu Zhang, Tianze Yang, Yucheng Shi, Evan Chen, Zixiang Nie, Kelly Wan, Liangjie Hong, Ninghao Liu, Liang Wu (Nokia Applied Research, Tencent Hunyuan, PolyU). Submitted 30 Sep 2026 (v1). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00831 ; read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08. Appendices A–E (answer-mass table, hidden-state head, BEV evaluation protocol) not read; only their summary in the body.
- Systems and readouts tested: 13 frozen instruction-tuned open LLMs in bf16 (Qwen3 1.7B/4B/8B/32B/30B-A3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B, Granite-3.3-8B, Phi-4-mini, SmolLM2-1.7B, OLMo-2-7B, gpt-oss-20b low reasoning). Readout: one prefill, next-token distribution at the answer position restricted to the K single-token option labels (letters, Yes/No, digits) and renormalised (Eq. 1). Variants: L0 (label prior + rotations), L1 (+temperature), L2-mono (truncated forward + affine translator to the final layer, hidden-state access needed). Also compared on BEV Decision Mix with Bespoke-Nimble-9B and four published interfaces.
- Design: three own tasks, 300 held-out items per cell: banking20 and newsgroups (20 options each), injection (binary); an 18-option MASSIVE routing question for timing; public JevBench subset (231 items, 213 scored, mean 3.6 options, 8 models); full BEV Decision Mix test split, 46,320 decisions, 2 AnyJev base models. Stats: exact sign test over 11 models (p = 4.9e-4 for 11/11); paired bootstrap (2,000 resamples) on JevBench; Clopper–Pearson bound for the stopping rule. The 20-option runs record aggregates only (no per-item intervals).
- Key results:
  - Answer-mass gate (Sec. 3): median normaliser m >= 0.995 on every reported row; three binary-injection rows excluded (SmolLM2-1.7B 0.408, OLMo-2-7B 0.710, Granite-3.3-8B 0.976). gpt-oss harmony template gave m = 0.000 while answers still looked plausible.
  - Reversing the option list changes the argmax on 32.8% (banking20) and 33.4% (newsgroups) of items on average over 11 models (Sec. 3).
  - Table 1 (means over models passing the gate): banking20 accuracy 0.665 raw -> 0.737 rotations -> 0.752 rotations + label prior; flip rate 0.328 -> 0.138. Newsgroups accuracy 0.609 -> 0.668 -> 0.675; flip 0.334 -> 0.183. Injection (K=2): flip 0.180 -> 0.000 but accuracy up on only 3/7 models (p = 0.77). Rotations lower flip rate and raise accuracy on 11/11 models on both 20-option tasks (+7.2 and +5.9 points on the means).
  - JevBench public (Table 2, Fig. 3): accuracy spans 0.549 (Qwen3-1.7B) to 0.798 (Qwen3-32B); debiasing changes accuracy by +0.006 pooled, 95% CI [-0.004, +0.018]; no per-model interval excludes zero. Trend of effect with K: +0.0059 per option, CI [-0.0012, +0.0132] (not resolved). Temperature lowers ECE on 7/8 models (four intervals exclude zero, all improvements), raises it on Granite (0.270 -> 0.301).
  - BEV Decision Mix, 46,320 decisions (Sec. 7.3): rotation average without label prior 68.01% (Qwen3.5-9B) and 67.59% (Qwen3-32B) vs raw 67.85% and 67.03%; adding the batch prior at alpha = 0.75 lowers accuracy to 65.01% and 65.55%, though 9B ECE improves 0.0490 -> 0.0418. Bespoke-Nimble-9B 70.57% (a trained model, 2.56 points above the 9B rotation average). L2-mono at block 29 of 32 scores 67.24%, 0.61 below the full-depth readout.
  - Serving (Table 3): 18-option question, 300 decisions, H100: all 18 rotations 16.7 decisions/s on vLLM; stopping rule wave 2 reads 7.3 of 18 rotations, 37.2 decisions/s (2.22x), agreement with full-rotation decision 0.987, accuracy 0.703 vs 0.697. On Transformers 2.34x.
  - "What did not work" (Sec. 8): a question-agnostic closed-form head fitted on 19 questions fails on the held-out one (0.580 vs 0.635 debiased readout); an order-blind attention mask on the pretrained model (options see only prefix and themselves) makes options exchangeable (shuffle effect 3.7e-6 vs 2.2e-3 causal) but accuracy falls to chance: 0.042 vs 0.633 for the rotation average on Qwen2.5-7B, 18-option routing, chance 0.056. Masking options from each other with positions kept already gives 0.092. Packing all K rotations in one forward: 5.9x vs K independent prefills, 1.20x vs a shared-prefix cache on a 10-token state.
- Proposed method or recipe and its cost:
  1. Prompt: state first, then question, then one line `label. option` per position, then "answer with the label only" (so a backend can reuse the state prefix across layouts).
  2. Label prior: q_hat = running mean of the restricted distribution over inputs already scored (batch calibration), kept per question and per layout, no gold labels. Divide out: p_dbs proportional to p / q_hat^alpha, alpha = 0.75 chosen once on a separate sweep of 230 (model, question) pairs scored against gold labels (so alpha is a labelled quantity). Content-free-probe estimator is offered but not used. The paper warns it lowers accuracy when the label marginal is extreme.
  3. Position bias: K cyclic rotations (pi_t(j) = (j+t) mod K), average of log restricted probabilities (before the label prior), softmax. Exact under an additive model logit = c_i + b_position; reversal flips still 0.138/0.183 afterwards because the additive model is approximate.
  4. Optional canonical order: sort the option list by text before rotating, so the prompt set is a function of the option set (exact order invariance at any budget). Stated by construction but NOT measured in the paper (all runs rotate the caller's listing).
  5. Confidence: one temperature fitted on 100-500 labelled examples of the same question (argmax unchanged); the "+temperature" level also re-estimates q_hat from the calibration set, so its accuracy can differ by up to 0.013.
  6. Cost: K prefills per decision (18 for the 18-option question). Stopping rule: read rotations in van der Corput order (0, K/2, K/4, 3K/4, K/8, ...), compute the log-space margin mu = z(1) - z(2) of the running average, stop at the first rotation where mu >= tau after a minimum of 2. Choose tau without labels: read every rotation for n unlabelled states, take the smallest tau on a grid such that the Clopper–Pearson upper bound (delta = 0.05) on the share of states whose stopped decision differs from the full-rotation decision is <= epsilon (epsilon = 0.01 in the report). To make the bound valid, select on one third of an unlabelled batch and verify on the other two thirds. With one disagreement, a bound of about 4.7/n means roughly 500 verification states for a 1% target. Verified: Qwen2.5-7B bound 0.0079 at 10.61 of 18 rotations (1.70x fewer) and 9.01 of 20 (2.22x); Qwen3-8B on newsgroups misses at 0.0105; on massive_route no threshold clears the target. The serving run (tau = 6.0 from 600 states, 7.3 rotations) selected and bounded on the same states, so its bound does not cover the selection; held-out disagreement was 0.013 (4 of 300, 95% CI [0.004, 0.034]). Log-odds margin certified all four cells at epsilon = 0.01; the probability gap certified two.
- Authors' stated limitations (Sec. 9): single prefill cannot reason (hardest JevBench tier 0.343-0.590 across the 8 models); JevBench has few options (3.6 mean), where rotation has least to remove; 18 items unscored (12 ordinal); the 20-option results have no intervals; intervals are over items not seeds/hosts; threshold selected and certified on the same sample for the serving run; efficiency and accuracy measured on different tasks; hidden-state variants need a local backend. My caveats: the headline flip reduction is on weak-to-mid 1.7B-32B base models with 20 options; on a trained 9B-class comparison (BEV) rotations gain only +0.16 and +0.56 points over raw, so for strong models the benefit is mostly consistency, not accuracy. The label prior hurt accuracy on BEV (about -3 points), so alpha = 0.75 should not be applied blindly. The weak spot for the lab: tasks with 3-6 options and skewed label marginals are exactly where the prior is least safe.
- Relevance to the lab: experiments benchmark p3 (order reversal 21/60 J, 14/60 G), set5 (re-asking one-pass scorers), study/prompt_best_practice (permutation averaging / PriDe listed as untried). Verdict: **new idea worth trying** (extends the lab's order-flip finding with a quantified cure). Concrete reusable ideas: (a) permutation averaging in log space for W12/A27 letter logits (relabel letters per rotation, average log restricted probabilities, K = number of options so 3-6 passes, state prefix cacheable); (b) canonical option sorting as a free exact-invariance step for J/G and for any readout (caveat: answers then depend on option names, so it trades order dependence for name dependence); (c) the answer-mass check m as a template/readout sanity gate for W12/A27 before reporting accuracy; (d) the unlabelled-state Clopper–Pearson stopping rule if rotation cost becomes a problem for K27. Not applicable: the order-blind mask without training (it destroys accuracy on a pretrained model, Sec. 8), and the label prior without checking the label marginal of the lab's workloads (the missing_evidence/fallback classes are rare, which is the extreme-marginal case).

## 2609.35865 — PACT: Pairwise-Anchored Calibrated Tuning for Single-Token Typed Decisions
- Author: Yida Lin (Victoria University of Wellington). Submitted 26 Sep 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35865 ; read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08. Appendix A tables only skimmed; limitations section partly truncated in the extract.
- Systems and readouts tested: Qwen3.5-9B with LoRA (rank 16, rsLoRA, LoRA+ ratio 8) reading one-letter answer-code logits at one position (Choice, Boolean, Score fields, C from 2 to 26). Arms: base model, "Nimble recipe" (the published cross-entropy recipe: 1 epoch, plain LoRA), CE-only (PACT's optimiser and schedule, cross-entropy only), PACT (CE + four terms).
- Design: training data = 1,338 two-record families (2,676 records, 34 source families, 10 domains), each pair differing by an edit of at most 8 words that flips the answer, each with a machine-checked certificate that deleting the decisive sentence makes the fact unknown. Disjoint families: train 30, select 2, calibration 2, frozen holdout 6 families (324 records, 162 pairs; 146 Choice, 114 Boolean, 64 Score). Three seeds (17/18/19) for main arms; one seed for each leave-one-out ablation. Exact McNemar and 10,000-resample paired bootstrap per seed. Hypotheses H1-H5 written before the runs.
- Key results (Tables III-VII): single pass accuracy Nimble 85.2 ± 2.1, PACT 84.6 ± 2.8, CE-only 82.2 ± 6.0, base 66.4; McNemar p >= 0.50 PACT vs Nimble at every seed. PACT vs CE-only: significant at 2 of 3 seeds (p = 0.008, 0.035; seed 17 favours CE-only, p = 0.099), halves seed s.d. (6.0 -> 2.8), lowers NLL by 26% and ECE by 25%. Score-field MAE 0.232 (PACT) vs 0.311 / 0.322. Position bias with K=4 canonical relabellings: flip rate 9.8% (PACT) vs 13.8% (Nimble), 12.1% (CE-only), 23.5% (base); mean pairwise TV 5.6% vs 6.9% / 7.4% / 12.0%; gain from K=1 to K=4 ensembling: PACT -0.7 pp, CE-only +2.4, Nimble +0.5, base +1.2. Contextual temperature lowers holdout ECE in 9/9 runs (PACT 10.8 -> 7.9%), accuracy unchanged by construction, but selective-risk AURC slightly worse (3.95 -> 4.21); fitted slopes unstable across seeds (slope on log C ranges -1.60 to +2.73). Pair accuracy 73.3% vs single-member accuracy 84.6%; in 39 of 162 pairs (24%) the model gives the same answer to both members (ignores the edited fact), 74% of all imperfect pairs. 28% of errors (16 of 58) carry confidence >= 0.9. Leave-one-out ablations (seed 17): no term raises raw accuracy; removing the necessity term hurts most (accuracy -3.1 pp, pair accuracy -8.0 pp, TV 5.0 -> 11.7%). Per-kind effects: PACT +6.2 to +15.6 pp on Score fields but -3.4 to -4.8 on Choice and -0.9 to -4.4 on Boolean vs Nimble, same sign at all seeds. Verdicts on pre-specified hypotheses: H2 and H5 supported, H3 and H4 partly, H1 inconclusive.
- Proposed method or recipe and its cost: loss = CE + 0.5 L_CF + 0.5 L_PC + 0.2 L_NR + 0.3 L_EMD (weights not tuned on evaluation data; ramped in over first 10% of steps). L_CF = softplus(2 - d), d = difference-in-differences of the ya:yb log-odds across the pair, exactly invariant to any shared logit offset (Proposition 1). L_PC = Jensen–Shannon divergence between the canonical distributions of two answer-code permutations. L_NR = relu(|z_ya - z_yb| - 0)^2 on the certificate-ablated context. L_EMD = squared earth-mover (CDF) distance for ordinal Score fields. Contextual temperature T(x) = softplus(a + b log C + c log(L/1000)), 3 parameters fitted by NLL on a held-out calibration split. Cost: each pair contributes 5 views (2.5x the rows); 1.37 h and 28.8 GiB per run vs 0.50 h (CE-only) and 0.26 h (Nimble, 1 epoch) on 48 GB GPUs; serving cost unchanged. Dropping the permuted views saves 45% of time.
- Authors' stated limitations: holdout is 324 synthetic, model-labelled items from 6 families (labels from the same model family as training, so shared errors are undetectable); L_NR and L_CF trust the certificate; the necessity term is applied to one ablated view per pair; ablations are single-seed so per-term claims are directional; checkpoint selection on 2 families / 116 records is as noisy as the effects (the lowest-selection-NLL CE-only run had the lowest holdout accuracy, 75.6%). My caveats: the headline "lowest position bias" (9.8 vs 13.8%) rests on 3 seeds and a 324-item set; the paper itself reports that the objective does not beat the well-tuned published CE recipe on accuracy.
- Relevance to the lab: set3 (missing evidence: decisive fact absent but every system decides anyway), set4 (verification), set5, benchmark p3 (flips). Verdict: **extends the lab's finding** (a documented training-side analogue of the missing-evidence failure and of order flips) and a **new idea worth trying** for evaluation. Reusable: (a) pair accuracy (both members of a minimally edited pair correct) as the headline metric for W2 sufficiency cases: PACT shows 84.6% single-member accuracy but 73.3% pair accuracy and 24% "ignores the edit" pairs; the lab's swap pairs for priority orders are the same construction; (b) evidence-necessity probe: delete the decisive sentence and require near-equal logits for the competing answers; apply as a test on W12/A27 letter logits and K27 pointer scores (logit gap |z_a - z_b| on the ablated context); (c) the finding that a model with the invariance term gains nothing from K=4 ensembling is a diagnostic: use the K=1 vs K=4 gain as a measure of residual position bias in W12/A27. Training recipe itself is not applicable unless the lab fine-tunes.

## 2610.01601 — Permutation-Robust Decision Modeling with Candidate-Independent Block-Causal Attention
- Author: Guy Amit (independent researcher). Submitted 1 Oct 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01601 ; read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts tested: decoder backbones Gemma 3 1B, Qwen3 1.7B, Qwen3 4B with a trainable scoring head on one hidden state per candidate block (score token at the end of each candidate), softmax over candidates. Compared: causal cross-encoder, candidate-independent (CI) block-causal, frozen-backbone dual encoder (historical, not optimisation-matched).
- Design: Open-Jev typed-decision dataset with identical train/val/test/OOD partitions; test 5,000 examples (Gemma, Qwen 1.7B) and 10,208 (Qwen 4B), shared OOD 15,539. Two epochs, effective batch 64, checkpoint chosen by validation NLL. Each held-out example scored in original order and under five fixed permutations, mapped back to canonical candidate IDs. Paired group-level bootstrap, 5,000 resamples (seed 2026). One training seed per condition.
- Key results: Table I: test flip rate falls 8.30 -> 0.11% (Gemma 1B), 18.16 -> 1.20% (Qwen 1.7B), 7.19 -> 0.67% (Qwen 4B). Test accuracy 84.38 -> 84.66, 81.84 -> 84.96, 91.05 -> 92.22. OOD accuracy within 0.72 points (Gemma 80.44 vs 80.71; Qwen 1.7B 75.09 vs 74.37; Qwen 4B 83.43 vs 83.83), OOD NLL worse for CI on all three (e.g. Gemma 0.640 vs 0.723). Qwen 1.7B paired: accuracy +3.12 points [+0.82, +5.63]; all OOD intervals cross zero. Mechanism ablation (Gemma 1B, full data): isolation only 3.82% test flips (from 8.30%); position-reset only 12.16% flips (worse than causal) with 79.64% accuracy; no permutation augmentation still 0.04% flips. Dual encoder 65.68% test accuracy. Qwen3-4B 250K-example artifact (six sources): balanced validation 95.61% accuracy, NLL 0.1014, ECE 0.0085, flip 0.183%; OOD 83.84% accuracy, flip 0.0044; 28.57% on the eight-candidate slice and 76.86% / 72.69% at 3 / 16 candidates.
- Proposed method or recipe and its cost: mask M: prefix causal; candidate block i attends to the prefix and causally to itself, never to another block; each block restarts at the same logical positions (position = q - s_i), the prefix keeps its positions. Training with this mask (and candidate permutation augmentation, optional). Cost: needs fine-tuning; implemented with dense kernels, so no speed gain; block-sparse kernels not done.
- Authors' stated limitations: one training seed per condition; dense kernels; dual-encoder not matched; 250K study is not a matched causal-vs-CI data-scale comparison; slices are descriptive only; causal remains useful when candidates must interact. My caveats: OOD NLL consistently worse for CI; the eight-candidate slice is 28.57%, which the paper does not explain; the invariant mask costs candidate interaction, and AnyJev's untrained-mask test shows interaction matters for pretrained models.
- Relevance to the lab: benchmark p3 order-flip result. Verdict: **background only** for the lab's J/G encoders (cannot be applied without training), but **extends the lab's finding** that order dependence is structural (flip rates 7-18% in causal baselines; the lab's J/G flips are 35% and 23%). Reusable: the evaluation protocol (original + 5 fixed permutations mapped back to canonical IDs, group-level paired bootstrap) for any readout, and the mechanism result that both isolation and position reset are required (position reset alone made flips worse).

## 2610.06744 — ufakzeka-karar: An Open Turkish Typed-Decision Model with Order-Invariant Option Scoring
- Author: Sait Furkan Teke (ufak AI). Dated October 2026; arXiv submitted 5 Oct 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06744 ; read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08.
- Systems and readouts tested: 182,494,466-parameter Turkish causal encoder-style backbone (ufakzeka-1-base, 24 layers, hidden 768); head = each option segment attends to the prefix and itself only, all options start at the same position after the prefix, mean-pooled option states scored by one linear layer, softmax over options. Compared with a sequential head trained with shuffled options and with REINFORCE. Board comparison with 15 other rows (Gemini 3.8 Flash, GPT-5.6 Sol, GLM 5.3, Jev 1.13, Kev 4B/9B, Qwen3.5-4B, DeepSeek V4 Pro, decider-2b, Laya variants, open-jev DeBERTa, a surface-cue baseline).
- Design: HakemBench v1.0 open set, 4,275 questions in 7 tracks (most gold from AI passes, 260 questions human gold); three runs, three seeds per recipe; ablations pre-specified with paired bootstrap over items and Holm correction; development set of 255 support questions with human answers (single annotator).
- Key results: order sensitivity 0.000 by construction; sequential head with shuffled options was about as accurate (Run 1 -1.7 points [-4.3, +0.9]; second round -4.0 [-7.0, -1.0]) but changed 2.3-2.8% of answers under reordering. REINFORCE (8 samples, leave-one-out) lost 10.2 macro-F1 points [-13.5, -6.2] in Run 1 and 12.7 [-15.5, -9.5] in the second round to cross-entropy, with every calibration measure worse. Released model: composite 0.660 [0.642, 0.677], rank 7 of 16 (Kev 4B 0.688, Kev 9B 0.666 above it); 0.678, rank 6, on the four unflagged tracks. Order sensitivity of other measured models on HakemBench ranges 0.039 (Jev 1.13) to 0.590 (Laya) (cited from the companion paper). Temperature scaling raises smooth ECE on held-out support questions: 0.027 -> 0.045 (Run 1, never trained on them); for the released run 0.036 -> 0.064 (not an unseen-question test). Dev set: 0.050 -> 0.038. Not-sure threshold 0.9 passes 29.2% of questions with 6.2% wrong; at 0.99, 12.2% of 148 guardrail answers wrong. Triage priority scale accuracy 0.500, macro F1 0.268; support choice/score accuracy 0.490/0.481, below the surface-cue baseline (0.516/0.507); on 84 hand-answered support questions the model scores 0.476 vs baseline 0.619.
- Proposed method or recipe and its cost: order-invariant head as above, with options laid out sorted by encoded text and scores returned in caller order (bit-for-bit permutation test; more than ten options are split into passes of ten under one softmax). Loss: cross-entropy against soft targets plus ranked probability score on ordinal questions; no RL. Temperature chosen among four forms (none, global, per type, per type and option-count bucket) on a fit/selection split by selection-set cross-entropy; isotonic map from raw max probability to expected error as the "not sure" signal. Cost: $236.90 total GPU and API; 427.8 s training on one H200; 102.5 ms median per question on one CPU thread.
- Authors' stated limitations (Sec. 8): six models score higher; support routing weak (below the surface baseline); one human annotator; calibration does not transfer everywhere; not-sure signal misses guardrail errors; every number of the released model comes after reading earlier runs' test results (flagged); moderation may be inflated by style likeness; weak world knowledge; intervals likely too narrow because items from one generation request are correlated. My caveats: the sequential-head accuracy loss "did not reproduce" in Run 1, so only "as accurate, never depends on order" is claimed; the REINFORCE arm is a plain policy gradient, not the RLCD recipe, and its optimum is by construction a one-hot answer (the authors' own explanation).
- Relevance to the lab: benchmark p3 (J/G flips), study of calibration. Verdict: **background only** for order invariance (requires training) but **extends the lab's finding** on the RL-vs-CE question and on temperature scaling (it can hurt on unseen question types). Reusable: (a) the sorted-layout trick (sort options, return in caller order) is the cheap, training-free half of the idea and applies to J/G/W12/A27 as canonical ordering (see AnyJev); (b) the evidence that fitting a temperature on one question distribution can worsen ECE on new question types: if the lab ever fits T for W12/A27/K27 it should be fitted per workload class (W1/W2/W3) and checked on held-out classes.

## 2609.38850 — OpenJev-RLCD: A Working RLCD Implementation
- Authors: Zhimin Gao, Pichao Wang. Submitted 30 Sep 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38850 ; read: full text (PDF via pdftotext), accessed 2026-10-08; appendices (estimator derivations) not read.
- Systems and readouts tested: Qwen3-1.7B (non-thinking) that samples a rationale, then the answer distribution u(x, r) is read as the softmax over option tokens at the "Answer:" marker (readout formulation, not sampling the answer). Methods: SFT+TS (direct answer), RFT/STaR+TS (with/without KL anchor), GRPO+KL+TS, RLCD stage 1 (Brier or log-score readout), RLCD two-stage.
- Design: MMLU-Pro (10 options; first 1000 test, 300 dev), GSM8K-Verify (yes/no, proposals from Qwen3-0.6B, test 1,260), ChaosNLI (100 annotator labels per item; 400 test). 3 seeds, paired tests over test items, 95% bootstrap and sign-flip permutation p-values; all baselines temperature-scaled on dev. 400-800 update steps, 8 questions and M = 4 rationales per step.
- Key results (Table 1, 2, 3, 4): GSM8K-Verify: SFT+TS 70.4% accuracy, AURC 0.174; GRPO+KL+TS 91.8%, AURC 0.061, fitted T about 7; RLCD two-stage 92.2% (n.s. vs GRPO), Brier 0.135 vs 0.150, AURC 0.034 (p < 1e-3), T = 1.4; coverage decided at <=5% error 81.1% vs 19.4% (GRPO). MMLU-Pro: RLCD two-stage 47.7% vs SFT+TS 42.3% and GRPO 42.4%; at 20% error budget decides 28% vs 16% (SFT) and none for any reasoning baseline; RFT without KL collapses to empty rationales in all three seeds (32.7%). Stage-1 alone carries most of the gain; Brier vs log score matters little. Stage-2 forks: outcome reward matters (anchor-only control is worse by +4.4 points on GSM8K-Verify); correctness reward re-inflates confidence on MMLU-Pro (T 1.4 -> 3.8); proper-score reward is neutral on MMLU-Pro. ChaosNLI (aleatoric): direct SFT JSD to humans 0.074; mixture objective (lambda = 1) 0.121 with degenerate rationales; per-trace objective without KL 0.075 with empty rationales; no RL variant beat cross-entropy. Explicit-probability RLCD for direct scorers never beat CE: CLINC150 accuracy 94.6% vs 96.3%. Policy-gradient term at weight 1 from scratch: accuracy 43.7 vs 46.3 readout-only (monotonically worse with weight).
- Proposed method or recipe and its cost: "calibrate, then reinforce". Per-rationale objective: score each sampled rationale's own committed distribution with a strictly proper score (Brier or log). Identity J(E_r u) = E_r J(u) + Var_r(u): scoring the mixture of samples pays a bonus for disagreeing rationales; RLVR = mixture objective without the diversity term. Stage 1 (400 steps): train the readout after on-policy rationales with pathwise gradient only. Stage 2 (400 steps from stage 1): leave-one-out REINFORCE with the proper score as reward, coefficient c = 0.3 on the score-function term, KL anchor beta = 0.04. Cost: M = 4 rationales per question per step plus generation; the score-function gradient norm is 7-12x the pathwise term.
- Authors' stated limitations: 1.7B model, 400-800 steps, 3 seeds; GSM8K-Verify is constructed by the authors; compared with Jev only behaviourally; benefit of stage 2 cannot be predicted without running it. My caveats: baselines are SFT/RFT/GRPO with only post-hoc temperature scaling, not CE with a Brier term or other calibration-aware SFT; all uplift is for rationale-generating models, which is not the one-pass-readout setting of the lab's systems.
- Relevance to the lab: no direct lab experiment trains models. It bears on how to interpret "RLCD-trained" claims about the lab's candidate systems and on L27 (generation with short JSON). | 2609.35865 (PACT) | CE vs CE + structure terms, same optimiser | PACT ties the published CE recipe on accuracy (84.6 vs 85.2); extra terms stabilise a hot optimiser | structure terms, not RL, are what the data supports |

They agree on the substantive point: for a one-pass readout (all of the lab's systems except L27), policy-gradient RLCD gives no accuracy gain over cross-entropy, and no calibration gain survives per-type temperature scaling. The one apparent disagreement is 2609.38850, whose positive RLCD result is for a different object (rationale-then-readout), against baselines that are reasoning models trained with correctness reward; it does not contradict the others, and itself reports the null for direct scorers. The only calibration edge in the corpus is raw (pre-TS) ECE: PG has the lowest raw ECE in 2610.02486 (7.0 vs 9.3) and RL lowest in SanSi. A caution: the three headline comparisons use different RL variants (Laya's noise-perturbed REINFORCE; plain REINFORCE-LOO; rationale-level LOO), so "RLCD" is not one thing. For the lab: no training is done, so this only affects how to read vendor claims; a "RLCD-trained" label on a candidate system should not be taken as better one-pass calibration.

### 2. Order invariance: rotation averaging vs order-invariant heads vs block-causal attention

| Approach | Where applied | Evidence | Cost | Needs training |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rotation averaging of log-probabilities (AnyJev) | inference | flip 0.328 -> 0.138 (banking20), 0.334 -> 0.183 (newsgroups), 11/11 models; accuracy +7.2 / +5.9; but +0.16 / +0.56 points on a trained-quality 9B / 32B on BEV | K prefills (reducible to about 40-60% with the stopping rule) | no |
| Canonical sorting of options (AnyJev, ufakzeka-karar) | inference | exact invariance to listing order by construction (not measured in AnyJev) | free | no |
| Order-blind head / mask (ufakzeka-karar) | architecture | flip 0.000 by construction; sequential head with shuffling still flips 2.3-2.8% | none at inference | yes |
| Block-causal CI attention (2610.01601) | architecture | flips 8.3 -> 0.1, 18.2 -> 1.2, 7.2 -> 0.7%; accuracy equal or better; OOD NLL worse | none at inference (dense kernels) | yes |
| Prior-fused residual (SBERT2S1 PFR) | architecture | 0.3% vs 15.7% flips for the cross head | 2x memory for frozen prior | yes |
| JSD consistency term (PACT) | training | flip 9.8% vs 12.1-13.8%; no gain from K=4 | 2.5x training rows | yes |
| Shuffled-option training (LLM-as-Jev) | training | same-answer-under-rotation 90.2-91.3% vs 87.6% | none | yes |
| Order-blind mask on pretrained model, no training (AnyJev Sec. 8) | inference | exact invariance but accuracy at chance (0.042 vs 0.633) | free | fails |

What the lab could apply to J and G (21/60 and 14/60 flips under reversal) without training: (a) canonical sorting, giving exact invariance to caller order at no cost, with the caveat that it ties the answer to option names; (b) permutation (rotation) averaging of the per-option scores, in log space, over K orderings; for a 0.58B encoder this is cheap, and with K = number of options (often 3-6 in the lab's workloads) the cost is a few small forward passes. Averaging only original + reversed makes the "reversal" metric trivially zero, so evaluate on random permutations, not on reversal alone. Neither paper tested encoders with in-input option slots; AnyJev's evidence is for decoder letter logits, and the additive position-bias assumption behind exactness is untested for J/G. The lab should first check whether J and G scores respond to position additively (average of the reversed and original orders vs random permutations). A limit: rotation averaging cancels only position bias; errors that come from the content (set5: gold is almost always K27's 2nd choice) will not disappear, and the K=4 gain for strong models is small (0.16-0.56 points on BEV; PACT +0.5 to +2.4). Differences from set5 and set6: set5 found that re-asking with a narrowed option set just renormalises; permuted re-asks present a different prompt and are not a renormalisation, so they can add information; set6 p1 found persona variation adds none (n* = 1.0), so rotation is a different perturbation, not a persona. For K27 (pointer head, one pass) and W12/A27 (letter logits) the same recipe applies but the lab has not yet measured their own reversal flip rates; measure them before spending K passes of a 27B model.

### 3. Ordinal compression vs the lab's score usage

2609.38827 shows that Jev 1.13 and Kev (0.8B-9B) use only 67-76% of the effective gold support on ordinal tasks and that utilisation falls with K (to 26-75% at K = 14), that order randomisation does not remove it, and that a LoRA fix on 8 sources raises it to 85-87% on those sources only. The lab's context does not report any ordinal-scale utilisation measurement; lab results on the score primitive and on W3 priority levels are described in terms of accuracy and swap pairs. Two lab findings look related but are not shown to be the same phenomenon: (i) set6 p2's errors being 95% "hold course" on one redundant branch looks like an anchor option; (ii) the lab's fallback classes are nominal choices, where 2609.38827 finds little compression. Also ufakzeka-karar's weakest results are on ordinal scales (triage priority accuracy 0.500, macro F1 0.268), and PACT's EMD term reduces Score MAE (0.232 vs 0.311) rather than accuracy, so ordinal quality is an independent axis from choice accuracy. Practical use: compute R and TVD for any ordinal output of K27/W12/A27/J/G before trusting expected-level scores; keep scales coarse; do not assume the result carries to Kev-27B (untested).

### 4. Other notes
- Question-blindness: 2609.33689 shows a fine-tuned specialist (Laya FT) returns its training answer for 98.2-99.5% of changed questions while its gate keeps acting; invariance tests (paraphrase, wording) cannot distinguish insensitivity from robustness. The lab's priority-swap pairs and set3's opaque-key variants are the correct style of test; the wording votes in set4 are not.
- Readouts: letter logits (W12, A27; also AnyJev, SanSi, LLM-as-Jev's comparison baselines) are the best documented; the numbered prefix-free suffix scoring (2610.02076) removes the 26-option cap and matched generation on 231/231 items; SanSi and 2610.02076 both report that readout choice has small accuracy effects relative to backbone and training data, in line with 2609.32160's Finding 1 (see evaluation-methodology.md).
- Missing evidence: PACT (24% of pairs ignore the edit; necessity term hurts most when removed), SanSi (17.5% hard answers on removed-evidence items; 19.7% of errors at >= 0.9 confidence) and 2610.02267 (both System-1 models recognise under 40% of "no answer here" contexts) all agree with the lab's set3 finding; none provides a training-free fix.
